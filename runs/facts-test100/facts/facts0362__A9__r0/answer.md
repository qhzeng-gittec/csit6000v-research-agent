

According to `doc.txt`, to help address concerns about the individual and societal implications of rapidly evolving AI technologies, the Study Panel offers three general policy recommendations:

1. **Define a path toward accruing technical expertise in AI at all levels of government.** Effective governance requires more experts who understand and can analyze the interactions between AI technologies, programmatic objectives, and overall societal values. Without sufficient technical expertise, officials may either refuse to permit a potentially promising application (lacking the ability to assess safety or other metrics) or, if insufficiently trained, simply take the word of industry technologists and green light a sensitive application that has not been adequately vetted.

2. **Remove the perceived and actual impediments to research on the fairness, security, privacy, and social impacts of AI systems.** The document notes that some interpretations of federal laws such as the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act and the anti-circumvention provision of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act are ambiguous regarding whether and how proprietary AI systems may be reverse engineered and evaluated by academics, journalists, and other researchers. Such research is described as critical if AI systems with physical and other material consequences are to be properly vetted and held accountable.

3. **Increase public and private funding for interdisciplinary studies of the societal impacts of AI.** The document states that society is underinvesting resources in research on the societal implications of AI technologies, and that private and public dollars should be directed toward interdisciplinary teams capable of analyzing AI from multiple angles. Research questions range from basic research into intelligence to methods to assess and affect the safety, privacy, fairness, and other impacts of AI—including questions such as who is responsible when a self-driven car crashes or an intelligent medical device fails, how AI applications can be prevented from unlawful discrimination, and who should reap the gains of AI-enabled efficiencies and what protections should be afforded to people whose skills are rendered obsolete.
