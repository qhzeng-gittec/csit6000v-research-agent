Based on `doc.txt`, here is one finding for each study mentioned:

**Violent media and real-world crime**
- Markey and Ferguson 2017: "Countries that consume more video games have lower levels of violent crime than those devoid of this media."
- Markey, Markey, and French 2015: "Months when people play violent video games the most tend to be safer than months they play them less."
- Beerthuizen, Weijters, and van der Laan 2017: "Even when violent video games, like Grand Theft Auto, were first released, there tends to be a decrease in violent crimes."
- Cunningham, Engelstätter, and Ward 2016; Ward 2011: "These findings have been replicated by psychologists, economists, and sociologists at various universities considering numerous other variables."
- Messner 1986: "Violent assaults, rapes, and murders all decrease when people are watching extremely violent television shows."
- Dahl and DellaVigna 2009; Markey, French, and Markey 2015: "Years in which the most violent films were released saw decreases in violent crime, and crime consistently decreases in the days following the release of popular violent movies."
- Felson 1994 (routine activity theory): "For a violent crime to occur, a perpetrator must be in the same location as the victim, and this location tends to be free of those who would likely prevent the crime."
- Snider 2014: "Male gamers in the United States spend a total of 468 million hours each month playing video games."

**Video games and aggression**
- Bushman et al. 2016: "Aggressive acts tend not to cause such extreme physical harm."
- Krahé and Möller 2004; Anderson and Dill 2000; Greitemeyer 2019; Anderson et al. 2004: These questionnaires "attempt to measure aggression by using items that assess whether an individual might be 'jerky'... antisocial... gossipy... or—oddly enough—conservative."
- Bushman and Gibson 2011: Individuals who play violent video games "are more likely to expose others to loud irritating noises."
- Anderson and Dill 2000: Participants "report feeling more hostile on a questionnaire."
- Deselms and Altman 2003: Participants "give longer prison sentences to hypothetical criminals."
- Yang, Huesmann, and Bushman 2014: Participants "give hot sauce to people who do not like spicy food."
- Kühn et al. 2019: "Importantly, many other researchers cannot replicate these effects."
- Bartholow, Sestir, and Davis 2005: Scored aggression "as the product of the intensity and duration."
- Carnagey and Anderson 2005: Scored aggression as "the square root of the duration score multiplied by the intensity score."
- Elson et al. 2014: "One can make it appear as if video games increase aggression, decrease aggression, or have no effect on aggression even within the same sample."
- Ferguson 2015b; Hilgard, Engelhardt, and Rouder 2017: "On average, only 0.4 percent to 4 percent of the variance in minor forms of aggression can be explained by violent video games."
