**Bacterial meningitis**
- "Bacterial meningitis is a rare but potentially fatal disease." (doc.txt)
- "Bacterial meningitis can cause stroke, hearing loss, and permanent brain damage." (doc.txt)
- Pneumococcal meningitis "is the most common form of meningitis and is the most serious form of bacterial meningitis," and survivors "often suffer neurological damage ranging from deafness to severe brain damage." (doc.txt)
- For meningococcal meningitis, "Between 10-15 percent of cases are fatal, with another 10-15 percent causing brain damage and other serious side effects." (doc.txt)

**Other forms**
- Amoebic meningitis "can be rapidly fatal." (doc.txt)
- Fungal meningitis is "treatable," but "often recurs in nearly half of affected persons." (doc.txt)

**Progression and outcomes**
- "Both diseases can progress quickly and have the potential to cause severe, irreversible neurological damage." (doc.txt)
- "In more serious cases, these diseases can cause hearing and/or speech loss, blindness, permanent brain and nerve damage, behavioral changes, cognitive disabilities, lack of muscle control, seizures, and memory loss." (doc.txt)

**Mitigating factors**
- "Appropriate antibiotic treatment for most types of meningitis can greatly reduce the risk of dying from the disease." (doc.txt)
- "In most cases, people with very mild encephalitis or meningitis can make a full recovery, although the process may be slow." (doc.txt)
- "Individuals with bacterial meningitis typically show some relief 48-72 hours following initial treatment but are more likely to experience complications caused by the disease." (doc.txt)

The document also notes that "Outcome generally depends on the particular infectious agent involved, the severity of the illness, and how quickly treatment is given." (doc.txt)
