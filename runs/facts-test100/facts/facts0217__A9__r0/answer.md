Based on `doc.txt`, the two points cover distinct rights:

**Point 6 — Right to information**
- Every child and young person has a right to information about their health and healthcare in a form they can understand, including information about the choice of health care services available.
- It covers the freedom to seek, receive and impart information and ideas, orally and through other means of the child's choice (e.g., play and art).
- The language and format must suit the child's abilities and level of understanding, and the child must be given every opportunity to participate in conversations about their health and care.
- It includes access to information in Te Reo Māori and, for those from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds, in their own language.
- Health professionals must talk directly to children and young people (as well as families/whānau), be as open as possible, and tell them what will happen before a procedure, with honest information about their condition and treatment outcomes and help to select coping strategies.
- Timely, accurate information and advance preparation for hospitalisation, procedures or surgery are meant to give children a sense of control and mastery.

**Point 7 — Right to participate in decision-making**
- Every child and young person has a right to participate in decision-making and, as appropriate to their capabilities, to make decisions about their care, to the greatest extent possible in line with their capacities for understanding.
- This includes involvement in decisions about the use, return or disposal of bodily parts or substances removed, changed or added in the course of health care.
- Children should be offered healthcare choices wherever possible and are always entitled to a second opinion; their questions and ideas should be heard, and if their views cannot be acted on they are entitled to an explanation.
- Health professionals must be available, trained and committed to communicating with children; participation can include verbal and nonverbal communication, play, art and other media, with attention to nonverbal cues of those who use it as their main form of communication.
- Participation extends beyond the individual to encouraging children and young people as groups to be involved in consultation on the development, implementation and evaluation of services, policies and strategies affecting them.
- Informed consent must be sought before involvement in teaching or research, participants may withdraw at any time without giving a reason, non-participation must not alter access to treatment, and ethical oversight by a Human Research Ethics Committee is required for research in child healthcare services.

**Key difference:** Point 6 is about the child's right to *receive* understandable, honest, timely information about their health and care (and to impart information/ideas), while Point 7 is about the child's right to *participate in and make* decisions about their care — including decisions about bodily parts or substances, second opinions, group consultation on services and policies, and consent/withdrawal in teaching and research. Both points mention communication through play, art and other media, but Point 6 frames it as a means of receiving and imparting information, whereas Point 7 frames it as a form of participation in decision-making.
