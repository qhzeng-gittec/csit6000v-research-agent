**Overall trend**
- Data from multiple sources suggest that mental health symptoms and substance use have increased since the beginning of the pandemic. Symptoms include emotional distress and anxiety, depression, and trauma-related conditions.
- Most 2020 data suggest an increase in behavioral health morbidity in the United States over the course of the pandemic, though comparisons between years are imperfect due to methodological differences between rapid surveys and perennial surveys. Comprehensive national data for 2020 were not yet available.

**Mental health and stress**
- A SHADAC survey (April 2020) found over 90% of U.S. adults reported additional stress caused by the pandemic.
- A Kaiser Family Foundation survey found a rising share of Americans reporting pandemic-related stress negatively affecting their mental health: 32% in March 2020, 45% in April 2020, and 53% in July 2020.
- NCHS/Census Bureau Household Pulse Survey data indicate the percentage of Americans experiencing symptoms of a mental health disorder increased during the pandemic.
- CDC analysis showed socioeconomic and psychosocial-related emergency department (ED) visits increased in April 2020 compared with April 2019, even as total ED visits fell over 40%.

**Suicide**
- One CDC analysis found roughly twice as many U.S. adults reported serious consideration of suicide in the previous 30 days during the pandemic compared with 2018 (10.7% vs. 4.3%).
- The Disaster Distress Helpline saw a 335% increase in calls in the first five months of the pandemic, though the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline did not report increased call volume.
- Effects on suicide attempts and deaths are unclear: ED visits for suicide attempts decreased between March and October 2020 (but less than overall ED visits), and preliminary data suggest suicide deaths may have decreased in 2020. There were regional and demographic differences, including rising ED visits for suspected suicide attempts among adolescents (especially girls) from May 2020, and increased suicide mortality for Black residents in Maryland from March to May 2020 while it decreased for White residents.

**Substance use and overdoses**
- Preliminary ODMAP data show an 11% increase in fatal overdoses and a 19% increase in nonfatal overdoses from March through May 2020 compared with 2019, with nearly 62% of participating counties reporting increases.
- CDC data show overdose deaths were already rising before the pandemic but accelerated after it began; the rise was particularly notable for synthetic opioids, with increases also in deaths involving commonly prescribed opioids and heroin, which had been declining.
- CDC found more drug overdoses, including opioid overdoses, in ED visits between March and October 2020 than in the same 2019 period.
- The document also notes individuals with substance use disorders may be at higher risk of contracting SARS-CoV-2 (due to unstable housing, high incarceration rates, or inability to physically distance) and at higher risk for complications, since substance use can suppress the immune system or inhibit respiratory functioning.
