**Sideline evaluation (NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment / "Sideline Survey")**
- **Trigger:** Performed immediately when a player exhibits or reports a sign/symptom of concussion, spinal cord neuropraxia, or a concern is raised by gameday medical personnel. The player is removed to the sideline (or stabilized on the field) and his helmet is taken away.
- **Content (minimum):** (i) review of the "No-Go" criteria (loss of consciousness, ataxia, confusion, amnesia); (ii) inquiry into the history of the event; (iii) review of concussion signs and symptoms; (iv) all Maddocks' questions; (v) complete video review of the injury, including discussion with the Booth UNC; and (vi) a focused neurological exam (cervical spine exam, speech evaluation, gait/coordination/balance testing, and eye movements/pupillary exam).
- **Setting and method:** Conducted inside the medical evaluation tent on the sideline (or in the locker room if the Club medical staff elects), performed using the tablet or other technology assigned by the NFL, with completion of each component confirmed on that device.
- **Outcome:** If any element is positive, inconclusive, or suspicious, the player must be escorted to the locker room immediately for the full locker room assessment. If the Club physician, after consulting the sideline UNC, concludes there was no concussion, the player may return to play.

**Locker room evaluation (NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment / "Locker Room Exam")**
- **Trigger:** The more complete, follow-up evaluation — required when the sideline assessment is positive/inconclusive/suspicious, when "No-Go" criteria are present, or when symptoms worsen or progress.
- **Content:** A standardized acute evaluation tool developed by the NFL's Head, Neck and Spine Committee, based on the SCAT 5 (International Concussion in Sport Group, McCrory et al., 2017) modified for the NFL. It includes a brief neurocognitive assessment (which does not replace more comprehensive neurological evaluation or formal neurocognitive testing) and the modified Balance Error Scoring System (mBESS).
- **Purpose and use:** Can aid in diagnosis even with delayed onset of symptoms; used in conjunction with preseason baseline and post-injury testing to provide detailed data on the injury and recovery course, with comparison to baseline improving its value.
- **Documentation:** Clubs must maintain and upload all exams to the EMR, and a copy is given to both the player and the team medical staff.
- **Decision:** The Club physician, assessing in conjunction with the sideline UNC, is responsible for determining whether the player is diagnosed with a concussion — even if the assessment is "within normal limits" compared to baseline.

**In short:** the sideline survey is the immediate, on-the-spot screening (No-Go review, history, Maddocks' questions, video review, focused neuro exam) done in the sideline tent with NFL-assigned technology, which can clear a player to return to play; the locker room exam is the comprehensive, standardized (SCAT 5-based) assessment done in the locker room when the sideline findings are positive, inconclusive, or suspicious, used to confirm diagnosis (including delayed-onset symptoms) and to track injury and recovery against the player's baseline.
