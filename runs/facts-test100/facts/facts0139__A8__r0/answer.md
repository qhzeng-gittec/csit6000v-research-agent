**Hunger**
- You may feel hungry at first: "although you may feel hungry when you first try fasting, many people actually report reduced hunger once they are used to it" (doc.txt).
- The document explains that "Fasting decreases hunger hormones and increases fullness" (doc.txt).
- It also notes that "You may struggle at first if you are not used to going for extended periods of time without food, but much of this could be down to habit," and that "Most people adapt quite quickly though, so find it increasingly easy to fast" (doc.txt).

**Thirst / hydration**
- Yes, you may need to drink more, because "When you eat, you get some water from your food. So during a fast, you are missing out on some of the fluids you normally get. This can lead to you becoming dehydrated, causing side effects such as tiredness and headaches" (doc.txt).
- The document adds that "Dehydration can also be mistaken for hunger, which can make it more difficult to stick to your planned fast" (doc.txt).
- For staying hydrated, it says "The best option for staying hydrated is water," and that you can flavour it, or have "Coffee (black or flavoured with natural extracts), tea (black, green, herbal or fruit), or broth (bone or vegetable)" (doc.txt).

So, according to the document: initial hunger is common but tends to decrease as you adapt, while you do miss out on the water normally obtained from food and should drink water (or the other listed beverages) to avoid dehydration.
