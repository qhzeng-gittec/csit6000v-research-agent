**Point 6 — the right to information.** It states that "Every child and young person has a right to information, in a form that is understandable to them" (doc.txt). Its focus is on how information is provided:
- Information about "their health and healthcare," including "information about the choice of health care services available" (doc.txt).
- Ensuring "the language and format used are appropriate to the child's or young person's abilities and level of understanding" (doc.txt), including "the right of tamariki and rangatahi to have access to information in Te Reo Māori and for those from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds to have access to information in their own language" (doc.txt).
- Health professionals should "talk directly to children and young people, as well as to their families/whānau, even if the child or young person may seem unable to comprehend" (doc.txt).
- Children are "entitled to know what is going to happen to them before a procedure occurs and to be given honest information about their condition and treatment outcomes" (doc.txt).
- "Advance preparation for hospitalisation, healthcare procedures or impending surgery provides children and young people with a sense of mastery over the healthcare environment" (doc.txt).

**Point 7 — the right to participate in decision-making.** It states that "Every child and young person has a right to participate in decision-making and, as appropriate to their capabilities, to make decisions about their care" (doc.txt). Its focus is on involvement in decisions:
- Involvement "to the greatest extent possible in line with their capacities for understanding" (doc.txt), including "decisions about the use, return or disposal of any bodily parts or substances removed, changed or added in the course of health care" (doc.txt).
- "Children and young people should be offered healthcare choices wherever possible. Further, they are always entitled to a second opinion" (doc.txt), and "If their views cannot be acted on, they are entitled to an explanation" (doc.txt).
- Informed consent: "Informed consent is to be sought from children, young people and their families/whānau before they are involved in teaching or research," with the "opportunity to withdraw at any time without having to give a reason" (doc.txt), and "Ethical oversight by a Human Research Ethics Committee of all research projects conducted in child healthcare services is part of protecting the children and young people involved" (doc.txt).
- Participation extends beyond individual care to "encouraging and supporting children and young people as groups to be involved in consultation on the development, implementation and evaluation of the services, policies and strategies that have an impact on them" (doc.txt).

**Key differences:** Point 6 is about receiving understandable, honest, timely information (in appropriate language and format, with advance preparation), while Point 7 is about taking part in and making decisions about care (in line with the child's capabilities, including bodily parts, second opinions, informed consent, group consultation, and research/teaching participation). One element they share is that communication may occur "through play, art and other media of the child's or young person's choice" (doc.txt), which appears in both points.
