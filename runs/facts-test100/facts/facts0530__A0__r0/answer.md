

According to `doc.txt`, the near-zero biodiversity of the coffee plantations (because all European coffee plants descended from the same Arabian mother plant) had "devastating consequences":

- **Vulnerability to disease:** In the last decades of the nineteenth century, the fungal pathogen *Haemilia vestatrix* (rust disease) severely infected coffee plantations in Sri Lanka, India, Java, Sumatra, and Malaysia. Because the plants were essentially clones of one another, the rust disease "destroyed the coffee plantations one by one."
- **Loss of plantations and forced conversion:** As a result of the devastation, some plantations had to be replanted with *Coffea canephora* (syn. *Coffea robusta*), which has a natural resistance to rust, while others were converted into tea plantations altogether (as in the case of Sri Lanka).

In short, the lack of genetic diversity left the plantations with no natural defense against the rust fungus, leading to widespread destruction of the coffee crops and the loss or conversion of entire plantations.
