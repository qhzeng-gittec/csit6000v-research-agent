

Based on `doc.txt`, the challenges to Hokkaido's economy described in the article are:

1. **Unbalanced industrial structure** – Despite well-developed infrastructure, Hokkaido's industrial structure is still biased toward natural-resources supply industries. Manufacturing is underdeveloped: its share of Hokkaido GDP is less than 10%, compared with more than 20% for the Japanese average. Within manufacturing, food processing dominated in 2005, and the share of assembly industry was only 12%, versus 48% in the Japanese average.

2. **Over-reliance on heavy infrastructure investment** – The long-standing strategy of heavy infrastructure investment created an excessive construction industry that now requires continuous infrastructure investment, and this situation has not allowed diversification of the industrial structure.

3. **Decreasing natural resources** – Hokkaido now faces problems of decreasing natural forestry and fishery resources, along with many impoverishing areas that used to depend on the coal mining industry.

4. **Damage to the fishery industry** – The introduction of the 200-mile economic zone system internationally in 1976 greatly damaged Japanese northern sea fishery, which had been the main profit source for Hokkaido's fishery industry.

5. **Decline and end of the coal industry** – Since the 1960s, the energy revolution and the shift from coal to oil caused the coal industry to decline. Hokkaido once produced 20 million tons of coal annually, but the last coal mine closed in 2002. Areas that had depended on coal were greatly affected and still struggle with economic decline and collapsing communities.

6. **Severe global competition in agriculture** – Although Hokkaido ranks No. 1 in Japan for producing many agricultural products, global competition has become more severe, requiring efforts to differentiate its products from foreign and other regions' products.

7. **Long-lasting economic recession** – Under the prolonged recession, Hokkaido's regional economy has been weakened, and the financial condition of many local self-governments is in very critical conditions.
