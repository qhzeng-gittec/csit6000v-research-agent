

**Introduction**
- *Staphylococcus aureus* is a major bacterial human pathogen causing a wide variety of clinical manifestations.
- Infections are common in both community-acquired and hospital-acquired settings.
- Treatment is challenging due to multi-drug resistant strains such as MRSA (Methicillin-Resistant *S. aureus*).
- *S. aureus* is found in the environment and in normal human flora on skin and mucous membranes (most often the nasal area) of most healthy individuals.
- It does not normally infect healthy skin, but if it enters the bloodstream or internal tissues, it may cause potentially serious infections.
- Transmission is typically by direct contact, though some infections involve other methods.

**Epidemiology**
- *S. aureus* (including drug-resistant strains like MRSA) is found on skin and mucous membranes; humans are the major reservoir.
- Up to half of all adults are colonized, and approximately 15% of the population persistently carry *S. aureus* in the anterior nares.
- Higher colonization rates (up to 80%) occur in health care workers, regular needle users (e.g., diabetics and IV drug users), hospitalized patients, and immunocompromised individuals.
- Transmission is person-to-person via direct contact or fomites.
