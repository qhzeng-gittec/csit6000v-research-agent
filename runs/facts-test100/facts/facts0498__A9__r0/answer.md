Based on `doc.txt`, the entities that handled Mr. Taylor's disability claim and their responsibilities were as follows:

**VA (Department of Veterans Affairs).** VA received Mr. Taylor's claim for service-connected disability benefits in 2007, and a VA medical examiner diagnosed him with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) resulting from his time at Edgewood, which was reaggravated during his service in Vietnam. VA ultimately granted him a total disability rating with an effective date of February 28, 2007, the day VA received his claim, relying on 38 U.S.C. § 5110 (implemented under 38 C.F.R. § 3.400), which provides that, absent certain exceptions, the effective date shall not be earlier than the date of receipt of the application. After the BVA remanded the case in February 2014, VA procured a secrecy oath that most participants in the Edgewood program signed, and VA and the BVA again concluded that Mr. Taylor was entitled to benefits starting February 28, 2007. The Federal Circuit later held that the government unconstitutionally denied Mr. Taylor access to the VA adjudicatory system—the exclusive forum for securing his legal entitlement to the benefits—through the secrecy oath and threat of criminal prosecution for violating it, and the determination of his effective date (which could be September 7, 1971, the date of his discharge) was left pending VA's assessment of the issue on remand.

**Board of Veterans' Appeals (BVA).** The BVA first reviewed Mr. Taylor's appeal of the VA determination in July 2010, when he sought an effective date of September 7, 1971, the day he was discharged from service. The BVA held that 38 U.S.C. § 5110 prevented the effective date from being any earlier than the day he filed his claim, stating that "there was nothing stopping [Mr. Taylor] from filing the claim . . . earlier and [VA] asking for a delay in the adjudication of the case following the obtaining of important evidence." After the CAVC vacated that decision, the BVA in February 2014 remanded the case to VA to ascertain the secrecy oath's language and reassess the case. In April 2017, the BVA again concluded that benefits began February 28, 2007, giving three reasons: (1) Mr. Taylor could have filed a disability claim earlier than 2007 premised on his experiences serving in Vietnam, which were partially responsible for his PTSD; (2) he could have divulged his participation in the Edgewood program because he had done so on a previous occasion in pursuit of care, though the health care provider at that time did not believe him; and (3) Section 5110 delineates when VA benefits can begin and does not provide for equitable tolling.

**U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC).** In June 2013, a single-judge CAVC decision held that the BVA "failed to provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases for its . . . decision"; the CAVC sought the language of the original secrecy oath, vacated the BVA's decision, and remanded the case. In April 2019, a 2-1 panel of CAVC judges affirmed the BVA's determination that Mr. Taylor was only entitled to benefits beginning February 28, 2007, detailing three components of its analysis: first, Mr. Taylor was not entitled to relief under the Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause because he did not have a property interest in his VA benefits prior to February 28, 2007, relying on Federal Circuit precedent that individuals gain a constitutionally cognizable property interest in VA benefits only upon filing claims; second, the court determined it lacked authority to provide equitable relief by ordering VA to establish a process through which he and similarly situated veterans could establish effective dates preceding their claim-filing dates; and third, the court held that 38 U.S.C. § 5110 required the effective date to be February 28, 2007, the date he filed his claim.

**Federal Circuit.** A three-judge Federal Circuit panel issued its opinion in June 2021, ruling in favor of Mr. Taylor and holding that: (1) the Veterans Court may exercise equitable powers, within the scope of its statutory jurisdiction, to ensure that all veterans entitled to benefits receive them; (2) the effective date requirement under 38 U.S.C. § 5110(a)(1) is not jurisdictional and can be subject to equitable considerations and relief; (3) where the Government has affirmatively and intentionally prevented veterans from seeking medical care and VA benefits to which they are entitled under threat of criminal prosecution, the CAVC is within its authority to hold the Government equitably estopped from asserting that those veterans are not entitled to an earlier effective date; and (4) the government is equitably estopped from asserting § 5110(a)(1) against Mr. Taylor because the government's actions caused him to file his benefits claim when he did. The panel reversed the CAVC's decision and remanded the case. In July 2021, the Federal Circuit voted to hear the case en banc; the full court heard oral argument on February 10, 2022, but then stayed all proceedings in Taylor until the Supreme Court decided Arellano v. McDonough, and after that decision requested supplemental briefing by the parties. In June 2023, the full Federal Circuit decided in favor of Mr. Taylor: after finding equitable estoppel unavailable under Section 5110(a)(1) in light of Supreme Court precedent, the court held that the government violated Mr. Taylor's constitutional right to due process under the Fifth Amendment, concluding that he had "a constitutional right of access to adjudicatory forums" and that the VA adjudicatory system and VA benefits were such a forum and entitlement, and that the government unconstitutionally denied him access through the secrecy oath and threat of criminal prosecution. The court ruled that the relevant statutory limits on effective dates were "unconstitutional as applied" in Mr. Taylor's case, determined that he was entitled to "the effective date he would have had if no unconstitutional denial of access had occurred," which could be September 7, 1971, the date of his discharge, pending VA assessment on remand, and reversed the CAVC's decision and remanded the case.
