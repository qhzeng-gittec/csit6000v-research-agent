**Why 2025 instead of 2024:** Nintendo hasn't officially announced any follow-up to the Switch yet, but there are enough rumors to suggest the next console will arrive sometime in 2025 rather than 2024. In February 2024, the Japanese publication Nikkei reported that the proposed Switch 2 would launch in March 2025 at the earliest, contradicting previous reports. According to that article, the 2025 date was likely due to Nintendo stockpiling consoles to avoid scalping at launch — the document notes that securing a new Nintendo console has historically been tricky, recalling how the 2017 Switch launch suffered from inflated aftermarket pricing due to crafty resellers. Adding to the intrigue, Nintendo won't be exhibiting at this year's Gamescom in Cologne, Germany, which raises questions about what to expect from the company in the latter half of 2024. Additionally, Nintendo's president Shuntaro Furukawa confirmed that Nintendo will support the current-gen Switch until at least March 2025, which aligns with a 2025 transition.

**What it will look like:** There isn't a lot of confirmed information on the design. However, a VGC report suggests the next-gen Switch may potentially feature an LCD screen instead of the OLED screen on the current top model. VGC also reported that development kits are already with partner studios, and sources cited some returning features: the new console could keep the portability of its predecessor and feature a cartridge slot for physical games. In May 2023, a patent was filed (published in September) suggesting a revamped Joy-Con controller that could help with the notorious Joy-Con drift issue — the patent details a magnetic field for controls rather than the plastic circuit boards in the current generation. That said, patents don't necessarily translate directly to future products, so there's no guarantee. The document also notes there may be several versions of the follow-up, just as there are multiple current models.

**Backwards compatibility:** There's no official word yet. Nintendo of America president Doug Bowser declined to comment on the rumored console, but in an interview with Inverse he discussed the Nintendo Account system that players' digital libraries are tied to, saying it will allow Nintendo to communicate with players and help ease a transition to a new platform. This could suggest your current Switch games may work on the next console, but the document advises taking that with a pinch of salt. It's also unclear whether accessories will be compatible — it's possible all your current controllers will work, but that's not yet known.
