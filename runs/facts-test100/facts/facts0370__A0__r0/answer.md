

To apply for EI, you should apply right away even if you don't yet have your Record of Employment (ROE), which you need for every employer you worked for in the past 12 months (though if your employer sends it online, you don't need a copy), and you'll need proof of employment such as pay stubs and T4 slips; if you don't apply within 4 weeks after your employment ends, you will probably get fewer EI payments. When you apply, you must answer questions on a special form about why you quit or were fired, whether you tried to fix the problem at work, and (if fired) whether your employer gave you warnings, after which Service Canada may interview you and contact your employer. If you quit, you won't qualify for regular EI unless you had "just cause," meaning you had no other reasonable choice, such as facing discrimination, unpaid or forced overtime, a hostile or illegal employer, pressure to leave, dangerous working conditions, or significant wage cuts or major changes to your duties. If you were fired, benefits are denied only if Service Canada decides you were fired for "misconduct," which usually means doing something wrong on purpose, such as threatening or being violent, destroying company property on purpose, being away from work without permission, or not obeying an employer's order; Service Canada—not your employer—makes this determination, so you should apply even if you were fired. If Service Canada disqualifies you, they will send a letter stating that you cannot get regular EI benefits. That letter also tells you that you have the right to ask Service Canada to reconsider their decision, which you must request in writing within 30 days of finding out about the decision.
