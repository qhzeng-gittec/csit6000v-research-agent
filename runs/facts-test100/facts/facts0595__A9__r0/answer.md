

**Summary of the article (doc.txt): New over-the-counter topical eczema probiotic**

- **What it is:** A new over-the-counter topical eczema probiotic, formulated by Skinesa and called **Defensin**, based on the bacterium *Roseomonas mucosa* (R. mucosa).
- **Origin:** The result of seven years of scientific discovery and research at NIAID's Laboratory of Clinical Immunology and Microbiology (LCIM), part of the National Institutes of Health.
- **The probiotic:** R. mucosa is a commensal bacterium that occurs naturally as part of a typical healthy skin microbiome. Scientists led by Ian Myles, M.D., M.P.H., chief of the LCIM Epithelial Research Unit, isolated and cultured specific strains of R. mucosa in the laboratory, conducted preclinical (laboratory/animal) and clinical (human) studies, and made the bacteria available for commercial, non-therapeutic development.
- **What it does for the skin:**
  - Safely relieves eczema symptoms in both adults and children.
  - Reduces eczema-related skin inflammation.
  - Enhances the skin's natural barrier function, which normally retains moisture and keeps out allergens.
  - Helps restore skin lipids (oils) that individuals with eczema are deficient in, due to microbiome imbalances.
- **Clinical study results:** In Phase 1/2 open-label and Phase 2 blinded, placebo-controlled studies, most people experienced greater than 75% improvement in eczema severity after application of R. mucosa.
  - Improvement was seen on all treated skin sites, including the inner elbows, inner knees, hands, trunk, and neck.
  - Most participants needed fewer corticosteroids, experienced less itching, and reported a better quality of life.
  - Benefits persisted after treatment ended: therapeutic R. mucosa strains remained on the skin for up to eight months in participants observed for that duration.
- **Context on eczema:** Also known as atopic dermatitis, it is a chronic inflammatory skin condition affecting approximately 20% of children and 10% of adults worldwide, characterized by dry, itchy skin that can compromise the skin barrier and increase vulnerability to bacterial, viral, and fungal skin infections.
- **Use:** R. mucosa-based topical interventions could simplify or complement current eczema management, when used in consultation with an individual's healthcare provider.
- **Future plans:** NIAID will conduct an additional clinical trial to generate further evidence on R. mucosa's efficacy in reducing eczema symptoms. Those data could form the basis of an FDA application to have the product regulated as a nonprescription drug and made accessible to a broader population. Study results are expected in 2024.
