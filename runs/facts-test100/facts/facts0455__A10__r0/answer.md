**Overall trend**
- "Data from multiple sources suggest that mental health symptoms and substance use have increased since the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic. These symptoms include emotional distress and anxiety, depression, and trauma-related conditions." (doc.txt)
- "most of the 2020 data suggest an increase in behavioral health morbidity in the United States over the course of the COVID-19 pandemic." (doc.txt)

**Stress and mental health**
- A SHADAC survey in April 2020 "found that over 90% of U.S. adults reported experiencing additional levels of stress caused by the COVID-19 pandemic." (doc.txt)
- A Kaiser Family Foundation survey found a rising share of Americans reporting pandemic-related stress affecting their mental health: "In March 2020, 32% of respondents felt that worry or stress related to coronavirus had a negative impact on their mental health. In April 2020 that number rose to 45%, and in July 2020, 53% reported that pandemic-related stress was affecting their mental health." (doc.txt)
- NCHS data "the percentage of Americans experiencing symptoms of a mental health disorder appears to have increased during the COVID19 pandemic." (doc.txt)
- CDC analysis of emergency department visits showed "socioeconomic and psychosocial-related visits increased during April 2020 (compared with April 2019), while total ED visits decreased over 40%." (doc.txt)

**Suicide**
- "approximately twice as many U.S. adults reported serious consideration of suicide in the previous 30 days compared with 2018 (10.7% versus4.3%)." (doc.txt)
- The Disaster Distress Helpline "experienced a 335% increase in calls during the first five months of the pandemic." (doc.txt)
- However, "it appears that suicide mortality has decreased compared with previous years," and ED visits for suicide attempts decreased between March and October 2020 compared with 2019, though "in May 2020 ED visits for suspected suicide attempts began to increase among adolescents, especially girls," and in Maryland "suicide mortality rates increased for Black residents from March 2020 to May 2020, while decreasing for White residents" (doc.txt)

**Substance use and overdoses**
- ODMAP "reported an increase of 11% in fatal overdoses and a 19% increase in nonfatal overdoses from March through May 2020 compared with the same months in 2019." (doc.txt)
- CDC data showed "the rate of overdose deaths accelerating after the pandemic began," with the rise "particularly notable for deaths involving synthetic opioids" and increases in deaths involving "commonly prescribed opioids and heroin" (doc.txt)
- CDC found "a higher number of drug overdoses—including opioid overdoses—between March and October 2020 compared with the same period in 2019." (doc.txt)
- The document also notes that "Individuals with substance use disorders may be at higher risk of contracting SARS-CoV-2 due to unstable housing situations, high incarceration rates, or the inability to physically distance themselves," and may be at higher risk for complications because "substance use can often suppress the immune system or inhibit respiratory functioning." (doc.txt)

**Caveats noted in the document**
- "Comprehensive national data for 2020 are not yet available," and "the methodological differences between these surveys and perennial surveys make comparisons between years imperfect" (doc.txt)
- ODMAP submissions "appeared to be trending upward prior to the onset of the pandemic, making it difficult to determine the effects of the pandemic and mitigation measures using these data." (doc.txt)
