**What a concussion is**
According to sports medicine physician Kristin Ernest, MD, a concussion is "any force transmitted to the brain itself." While it generally occurs with a blow to the head, any injury that causes the brain to shift rapidly within the skull (such as a whiplash injury) can cause a concussion.

**Symptoms**
Symptoms can vary from child to child, but the most common are headache, nausea, dizziness, irritability or crying, trouble concentrating, and sleep disturbance (sleeping more or less than usual).

**Diagnosis and treatment**
- A concussion will not show up on imaging tests such as MRI. It is diagnosed through medical evaluation of symptoms and often neurocognitive testing.
- Concussions are treated with both physical and cognitive rest, including rest from all athletic activities, as well as from homework and screen time.

**How it affects a child**
The document states the following about children:
- Recovery generally takes about 7–10 days, but **longer for younger children** and for those with a history of migraine or ADHD.
- If a concussion is undiagnosed and left untreated, it can lead to permanent brain damage.
- Those who have a first concussion are 3–6 times more likely to suffer another concussion than an athlete who has not had one, and returning to play before the brain has fully healed can put the athlete at higher risk for complications if a second concussion is sustained.
- Separately, "subconcussive hits" — repeated blows to the head that don't result in concussions — can, over a long period of time, have long-term health effects, primarily chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE), a progressive degenerative disease of the brain.
