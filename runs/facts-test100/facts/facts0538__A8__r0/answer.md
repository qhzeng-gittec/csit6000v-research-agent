**How the initiative shifts the "Defender's Dilemma"**

The document defines the dilemma as an asymmetry: "attackers need only one successful, novel threat to break through the best defenses. On the flip side, defenders need to deploy the best defenses at all times across increasingly complex digital terrain with no margin for error. Google calls this the "Defender's Dilemma," and there has never been a reliable way to tip that balance." (doc.txt)

The proposed shift is that "Google believes AI at scale can tackle the Defender's Dilemma. AI can do so by allowing security professionals and defenders to scale up their work in threat detection and related cybersecurity defense requirements." (doc.txt) In practical terms, the document describes four mechanisms:

- **Anticipation:** the initiative "aims to employ AI not only to respond to threats but to anticipate and neutralize them before they can cause harm," because "the traditional reactive cybersecurity model is no longer sufficient in a world where cyberthreats are becoming increasingly sophisticated and pervasive." (doc.txt)
- **Detection:** "The new initiative includes deploying AI-driven algorithms designed to identify and analyze patterns indicative of cyber threats," trained on data "across its global network" to "learn from the full range of threats and teach them to adapt to new tactics employed by cybercriminals." (doc.txt)
- **Vulnerability management:** "by identifying vulnerabilities within software and systems, AI can significantly reduce the window of opportunity for attackers to exploit these weaknesses," plus "AI's ability to suggest and implement fixes to vulnerabilities, streaming the patching process and, in doing so, further reducing the risk of a breach." (doc.txt)
- **Incident response:** the initiative "outlines the use of AI in incident response and automates the analysis of indices to identify the source, method and extent of an attack." (doc.txt)
