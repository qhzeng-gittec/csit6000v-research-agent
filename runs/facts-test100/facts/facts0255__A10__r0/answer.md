According to the article, if you can't qualify for a normal (unsecured) credit card, the alternatives it describes are:

1. **Open a secured credit card.** The article notes that "if your credit is heavily damaged, you may have trouble qualifying for a credit card with which to build that payment history. This is where a secured credit card can help" (doc.txt). It adds that "Secured credit cards can be pretty easy to get, even if your credit is damaged. That's because secured cards rely on a cash security deposit to minimize risk to the issuer" (doc.txt). It also says, "If you can't qualify for an unsecured card without an annual fee, consider a secured credit card instead" (doc.txt), and that "Even if you have significant credit damage, you can likely find an issuer willing to offer you a secured credit card" (doc.txt). It suggests that "Banks where you already have a good reputation and your local credit union are often the best places to find a secured card" (doc.txt).

2. **Become an authorized user on someone else's card.** "Another way to rebuild credit is to be added as an authorized user on another person's card (such as a trusted family member)" (doc.txt). "As long as the account is in good standing, being added as an authorized user can help raise your credit score" (doc.txt), though the article cautions that "if the cardholder or the authorized user runs up a high balance, both users could see credit score damage. Only tie your credit score to individuals you trust" (doc.txt).
