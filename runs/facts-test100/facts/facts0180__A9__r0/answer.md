**NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment (Sideline Survey)**
- Triggered when a player exhibits or reports a sign or symptom of concussion, spinal cord neuropraxia, or a concern is raised by gameday medical personnel (Club athletic trainer, Club physicians, Booth ATC Spotter, coach, teammate, game official, or sideline/Booth UNCs).
- The player is immediately removed to the sideline or stabilized on the field, the helmet is taken away, and the player must undergo the entire Sideline Survey.
- At a minimum it consists of: (i) a review of the "No-Go" criteria (loss of consciousness, ataxia, confusion, amnesia) — if present, the player is brought to the locker room immediately and shall not return to play; (ii) inquiry regarding the history of the event (before, during, and after the suspected mechanism of injury); (iii) review of concussion signs and symptoms; (iv) all Maddocks' questions; (v) complete video review of the injury, including discussion with the Booth UNC; and (vi) a focused neurological exam (cervical spine examination including range of motion and pain, evaluation of speech, testing of gait/coordination/balance, and eye movements and pupillary exam).
- It is conducted inside the medical evaluation tent on the sideline (or in the locker room if the Club medical staff elects to conduct it there), performed using the tablet or other technology assigned by the NFL, with completion of each component confirmed using the same.
- If any element is positive, inconclusive, or suspicious for a concussion, the player must be escorted to the locker room immediately for the complete NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment. If, after consultation with the sideline UNC, the Club physician concludes the player did not sustain a concussion, the player may return to play.

**NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment (Locker Room Exam)**
- It is the standardized acute evaluation tool developed by the NFL's Head, Neck and Spine Committee, used by Clubs' medical staffs and designated UNCs to evaluate potential concussions during practices and on game day.
- It is based on the Standardized Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT 5) published by the International Concussion in Sport Group (McCrory, et al., 2017), modified for use in the NFL (Attachment A).
- It can be used to aid in the diagnosis of concussion even if there is a delayed onset of symptoms.
- Its ongoing use in conjunction with preseason baseline and post-injury testing provides detailed data regarding each athlete's injury and recovery course, and comparing its results to the preseason baseline improves the value of the instrument.
- Clubs must maintain and upload all such exams to the EMR, and a copy is given to both the player and the team medical staff.
- The Club physician is responsible for determining whether the player is diagnosed as having a concussion; the athlete may have a concussion despite completing the assessment "within normal limits" compared to baseline, due to the potential limitations of the Assessment.
- Its neurocognitive assessment is brief and does not replace a more comprehensive neurological evaluation or more formal neurocognitive testing, and the modified Balance Error Scoring System (mBESS) is an important, validated component.

In short, the sideline survey is the immediate, minimum-component on-field screening (No-Go review, history, Maddocks' questions, video review, focused neurological exam) done in the sideline tent with NFL-assigned technology, while the locker room exam is the complete, standardized (SCAT 5-based) assessment used for diagnosis — including with delayed-onset symptoms — with comparison to preseason baseline, EMR documentation, and the mBESS.
