**Why the FHA would otherwise apply:** The FHA "prohibits discrimination in housing based on: race or color, national origin, religion, sex (including sexual orientation and gender identity, per a new executive order), familial status and disabilty" (doc.txt). A contract clause limiting rentals to Protestants is a religious restriction, and the document notes that "any language published that indicates preference or limitations based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin is discrimination" (doc.txt).

**Why the exemption applies here:** The document states: "For example, if a dwelling has four or fewer units and the owner lives in one of them, they are exempt from the FHA" (doc.txt). Your situation fits this exactly: a duplex (two units) in which the landlord lives in the other apartment. The document contrasts this with a state that does *not* follow the FHA's broad dispositions: "However, they would not be exempt under the Pennsylvania Human Relations Act unless the dwelling contained only two units and one was owner-occupied" (doc.txt). Since your state follows the FHA's broad dispositions, the four-or-fewer-units/owner-occupied exemption applies to your landlord.

**Result:** Because the dwelling is exempt from the FHA, the religious-rental restriction in the contract is not prohibited by the FHA, and the landlord can enforce the notice to leave.
