

According to `doc.txt`, satisfaction with customer service interactions drops during the holiday season for two related reasons:

1. **A sudden, temporary surge in volume.** Retailers bring in 20–40% of their annual sales in the weeks leading up to Christmas, and the Zendesk Benchmark found a clear correlation between the drop in satisfaction and the number of tickets per agent: as tickets per agent increased, satisfaction decreased.

2. **Customers are different and more difficult during the holidays.** The document notes that holiday customers are often more unpleasant because of stress, make purchase decisions less thoughtfully, and have less experience with retail processes such as coupons and returns. This leads to more purchase regrets and returns, and agents report having to spend more time on education and misunderstandings (e.g., customers who don't know how to find a return label or use a coupon before paying) at a time when they are already busy.

The document frames this as the "holiday dip": a measurable, consistent decline in satisfaction during Q4, driven by the combination of strained capacity (more tickets per agent) and a harder-to-serve customer base.
