

Based solely on `doc.txt` ("Gaming PC vs. Console: Which Should You Buy in 2024?"), here's how the two compare on your three points:

**1. Raw graphical performance (PC wins)**
- The current console generation (PS5, Xbox Series X|S) can technically output 8K and 120 FPS, but the document calls that "marketing bullshit." In practice, the most graphically demanding games are "lucky to upscale to 4K at a framerate of 60 FPS," and many titles include a 30 FPS "graphics" or "visuals" mode.
- On PC, 60 FPS is considered the bare minimum, 120+ FPS is preferable, and native resolutions beyond 4K are possible (e.g., 7,680 x 2,160 on the Samsung Neo G9 super-ultrawide — playable only on PC).
- Console versions of games generally run on lower quality presets — roughly equivalent to the "Medium" or "High" preset of the PC release.
- **Ray tracing:** a win for PC. Consoles can handle it, but game support is "underwhelming" — even big next-gen exclusives like Starfield lack it. Cross-platform titles that do support it, like Cyberpunk 2077, "usually stick to a level of quality that's a notch or two below the maximum available on PC."
- Caveats for PC: Nvidia's tendency to under-provide video memory is catching up to cards with less than 16GB of VRAM (the amount PS5 and Xbox Series X have), and some PC ports have optimization problems. The document concludes PC beats console on both framerate and visual quality, and that gap will widen as this console generation ages.

**2. Game availability (PC wins)**
- Consoles have a "remarkable" library: most games are cross-platform, and backwards compatibility with previous-gen titles boosts each console's library into the thousands.
- But compared to PC, "those numbers look absolutely adorable." A PC's library is estimated at over 100,000 titles — over 12,000 games were released on Steam in 2022 alone. A PC can also emulate most older game libraries and, in some cases, even modern games.

**3. Price of a solid PC that keeps up with consoles**
- A good mid-range, off-the-shelf desktop or gaming laptop with performance comparable to an Xbox Series X or PS5: **$1,500–$2,000** (basic Dell/HP desktops at the lower end; boutique builders like Origin PC or Digital Storm at the higher end).
- DIY route: a "console-slaying" desktop for **under $1,500** with an AMD Ryzen 5600X and a budget GPU like a Radeon RX 7800 XT.
- Warning: a PC priced like an Xbox Series X or PS5 will have an outdated graphics card that can't handle games designed for modern consoles, and PCs at entry-level console prices usually lack a proper GPU entirely.

**Bottom line for your use case:** Since you want Cyberpunk-level visuals with ray tracing and high FPS, the document's evidence points to PC — consoles cap out around upscaled 4K/60 FPS (often 30 FPS in graphics mode) and run ray tracing a notch or two below PC quality, while a $1,500–$2,000 PC (or a sub-$1,500 DIY build) is what it takes to match or beat the current consoles.
