**Company overview**
- Timberland Bancorp, Inc., a Washington corporation, is the holding company for Timberland Bank, which opened in 1915 and serves consumers and businesses across Grays Harbor, Thurston, Pierce, King, Kitsap and Lewis counties, Washington, through 23 branches (including its main office in Hoquiam).
- At September 30, 2022, the Company had total assets of $1.86 billion, net loans receivable of $1.13 billion, total deposits of $1.63 billion, and total shareholders' equity of $218.57 million.
- The holding company's activities are limited to passive investment activities and oversight of the Bank; the report therefore relates primarily to the Bank's operations.

**Business and profitability**
- The Bank is community-oriented, offering savings products to retail and business customers while concentrating lending on real estate-secured loans (residential construction, one- to four-family residential, multi-family, and commercial real estate), plus adjustable-rate residential mortgage loans, commercial business loans, and other consumer loans.
- Profitability depends primarily on net interest income after provision for (recapture of) loan losses. Net interest income is the difference between interest earned on interest-earning assets (mainly loans and investments) and interest paid on interest-bearing liabilities (mainly deposits and borrowings). Management aims to keep its net interest margin in the top quartile of Washington State peers.
- Since March 2022, the FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 300 basis points to 3.00%–3.25% as of September 30, 2022, and by another 75 basis points in November 2022 to 3.75%–4.00%. The Company believes its balance sheet is structured to benefit from variable-rate asset repricing and a higher net interest margin if rates continue to rise.
- The provision for loan losses was $270,000 for the year ended September 30, 2022 (mainly due to loan portfolio growth), versus no provision for the year ended September 30, 2021 (reflecting the improving economy and declining forecasted probable loan losses from COVID-19).
- Non-interest income for the year ended September 30, 2022 consisted primarily of service charges on deposit accounts, gains on sales of loans, ATM and debit card interchange fees, an increase in the cash surrender value of BOLI, escrow fees, and other operating income. Non-interest expense consisted primarily of salaries and employee benefits, premises and equipment, advertising, ATM/debit card fees, postage and courier, amortization of CDI, state and local taxes, professional fees, FDIC insurance premiums, loan administration and foreclosure expenses, data processing and telecommunications, deposit operation expenses, and other items.

**Operating strategy**
- The Company's objective is to operate the Bank as a well capitalized, profitable, independent, community-oriented institution in its primary market area, delivering returns to shareholders by focusing on higher-yielding assets (commercial real estate, construction, and commercial business loans), increasing core deposits, managing problem assets, managing expenses efficiently, and seeking expansion opportunities.
- Strategy objectives include: expanding presence in existing market areas (western Washington) by leveraging bank consolidation and a community-bank emphasis on responsive, personalized service; portfolio diversification (limiting speculative construction and land development loans in favor of lower-risk profiles, while continuing owner/builder, custom construction, multi-family, commercial business, and commercial real estate loans, and cross-selling products); increasing core deposits and retail deposit products (including technology-based products such as business cash management and remote deposit); managing exposure to fluctuating interest rates (retaining loans with periodic rate adjustment features or short-term nature, such as ARMs and short-term construction loans, while generally selling longer-term fixed-rate mortgages into the secondary market); continuing mortgage banking revenues (selling fixed-rate residential mortgages into the secondary market with servicing retained, and originating custom construction and owner/builder loans for sale upon completion); and maintaining strong asset quality.
- Asset quality: non-performing loans to net loans receivable were 0.18% at September 30, 2022 and 0.29% at September 30, 2021; non-performing assets to total assets were 0.12% and 0.18%, respectively; non-performing assets decreased to $2.17 million from $3.17 million. The Bank seeks to reduce non-performing assets through collections, writedowns, modifications, and sales of OREO, and takes proactive steps such as negotiating payment plans, forbearances, loan modifications and extensions, and accepting short payoffs on delinquent loans.
