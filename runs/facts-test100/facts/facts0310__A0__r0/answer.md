

Based on `doc.txt` (a scoping review of clinical prediction models for neonatal sepsis in low- and middle-income countries), here is a summary of the predictors used in the included models:

**Overall predictor counts**
- The 82 included studies performed 109 evaluations validating **44 distinct models**.
- A total of **135 predictors** of sepsis were included across all models:
  - **82 clinical parameters** (signs, symptoms, or risk factors)
  - **53 laboratory parameters**
- The **median number of predictors per model was 6** (range 2 to 110, interquartile range 4).

**Composition of models by predictor type**
- **14 models (32%)** included only clinical parameters
- **12 models (27%)** included only laboratory parameters
- **18 models (41%)** included both clinical and laboratory parameters

**Most common laboratory parameters**
- White cell count: **17 models (39%)**
- C-reactive protein (CRP): **16 models (36%)**
- Platelet count: **15 models (34%)**

**Most common clinical parameters**
- Neonatal fever: **13 models (30%)**
- Gestational age: **11 models (25%)**

**Model development methods (context for predictor selection)**
- Most models were developed using **logistic regression (16 models, 36%)**, often with stepwise selection to select predictors.
- **10 models (23%)** consisted of a scoring system based on univariable predictor performance, literature review, and expert opinion.

Note: the document states that the full list of the 135 predictors is in the supplementary appendix, so only the counts and the most common predictors are reported in the main text.
