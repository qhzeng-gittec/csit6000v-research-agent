

To apply, you should "apply for EI right away, even if you do not have your ROE yet," using proof of employment such as pay stubs and T4 slips, since "It is easier to apply for EI if you have your Record of Employment (ROE)" and "you will need an ROE for every employer you worked for in the past 12 months," but "If you do not apply within 4 weeks after your employment ends, you will probably get fewer EI payments." When you apply, you have to answer questions on a special form about why you quit or were fired, and Service Canada may interview you and contact your employer before deciding. If you quit, you will not qualify for regular EI benefits unless you had "just cause," which "means that you had to quit because you had no other reasonable choice," such as being discriminated against, an employer doing something against the law, dangerous working conditions, or a significant wage cut. If you were fired, benefits are denied only if Service Canada finds "misconduct," which "usually means doing something wrong on purpose," such as threatening someone, destroying company property, being away from work without permission, or not obeying an order, and "Service Canada decides if you were fired for misconduct, not your employer." If Service Canada disqualifies you, they will tell you in a letter, and "the letter will also tell you that you have the right to ask them to reconsider their decision," which "you must ask for this in writing within 30 days of finding out about Service Canada's decision."
