Based on `doc.txt`, the study examined weight changes among 4.24 million US adults (ages 18–84) from January 2019 through May 2021 using a large EHR database. Its outcome was:

- **A small increase in weight during the first year of the pandemic**: compared with the pre-pandemic weight trend, there was a mean increase of only **0.1 kg** in the first year of the pandemic (March 2020 through March 2021).
- **Variation by subgroup**: weight changes during the pandemic varied by sex, age, and initial BMI, but the **largest mean increase across these characteristics was less than 1.3 kg**.
- **Who gained the most**: weight increases were generally greatest among **women, adults with a BMI of 30 or 35 kg/m², and younger adults**.

In short, the study found that pandemic-related weight gain in US adults was small (about 0.1 kg on average in the first year, with subgroup increases under 1.3 kg), consistent with other large studies suggesting pandemic-related weight increases were smaller than earlier reports had indicated.
