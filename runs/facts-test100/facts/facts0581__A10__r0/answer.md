1. **Accuracy and bias**: "Some primary concerns revolve around the accuracy of the technology, including potential race-, gender-, and age-related biases" (doc.txt).

2. **Database collection, retention, and security**: Concerns include "the collection, retention, and security of images contained in various facial recognition databases" (doc.txt).

3. **Public notification**: A concern is "public notification regarding the use of facial recognition and other image capturing technology" (doc.txt).

4. **Lack of governing policies or standards**: Another concern is "policies or standards governing law enforcement agencies' use of the technology" (doc.txt).

The document also notes related developments and issues:

- These concerns "have manifested in actions such as federal, state, and city efforts to prohibit or bound law enforcement agencies' use of FRT," and "some companies producing facial recognition software, such as Microsoft, IBM, and Amazon, have enacted new barriers to law enforcement using their technologies" (doc.txt).
- "There have been heightened concerns following several revelations, including that Clearview AI, a company that developed image-search technology used by law enforcement agencies around the country, had amassed a database of over 3 billion images against which probe photos could be compared" (doc.txt).
- There is also an opacity concern: "the frequency and extent to which FRT is used at various phases of the criminal justice system (from generating leads and helping establish probable cause for an arrest or indictment, to serving as evidence in courtrooms) is unknown" (doc.txt).
- The document notes a limitation on how the technology's output can be used: FRT "is generally used by law enforcement in one-to-many searches to produce a gallery of potential suspects ranked by similarity and not to provide a single affirmative match. As such, the technology currently might not be relied upon in the same way that other biometric evidence might" (doc.txt).
