

To apply, you should apply for EI right away even if you do not yet have your Record of Employment (ROE), though it is easier to apply with one, and you will need an ROE for every employer you worked for in the past 12 months (a copy is not required if your employer sends it online, but you will need proof of employment such as pay stubs and T4 slips). If you do not apply within 4 weeks after your employment ends, you will probably get fewer EI payments. If you quit, you will not qualify for regular EI benefits unless you had "just cause," meaning you had to quit because you had no other reasonable choice; examples include discrimination, an employer or supervisor refusing to pay overtime, being unfair or hostile, doing something against the law, pressuring you to leave, expecting you to work in dangerous conditions, or significantly cutting your wages or making major changes to your duties, though even one of these examples might not be enough. If you were fired, Service Canada will not give you benefits if it decides you were fired for "misconduct," which usually means doing something wrong on purpose and is more than not being able to do the job well, with examples including threatening or being violent, destroying company property on purpose, being away from work without permission, or not obeying an order from your employer; Service Canada, not your employer, makes this decision, so you should apply even if you were fired. When you apply, you must answer questions on a special form about why you quit or were fired, whether you tried to fix the problem at work, and (if you quit) whether you tried to get other work before quitting, after which Service Canada may interview you and contact your employer before deciding whether you quit without just cause or were fired for misconduct. If Service Canada disqualifies you, it will tell you in a letter that you cannot get regular EI benefits, and that letter will also tell you that you have the right to ask them in writing, within 30 days of finding out about the decision, to reconsider and change it. Even if you cannot get regular EI benefits, you might still be able to get the special benefits listed in the document.
