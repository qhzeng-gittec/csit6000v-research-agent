

To apply, you need a Record of Employment (ROE) for every employer you worked for in the past 12 months, though "It is easier to apply for EI if you have your Record of Employment (ROE)" and "you should apply for EI right away, even if you do not have your ROE yet," using proof of employment such as pay stubs and T4 slips, because "If you do not apply within 4 weeks after your employment ends, you will probably get fewer EI payments" (doc.txt). When you apply, you must answer questions on a special form about why you quit or were fired, whether you tried to fix the problem, and whether your employer gave you warnings, after which "Service Canada may interview you. They may also contact your employer" (doc.txt). If you quit, you will not qualify for regular EI benefits "unless you had 'just cause'. This means that you had to quit because you had no other reasonable choice," with examples including discrimination, an employer refusing to pay overtime, dangerous working conditions, or significant wage cuts (doc.txt). If you were fired, benefits are denied only if Service Canada finds "misconduct," which "usually means doing something wrong on purpose," such as threatening someone, destroying company property on purpose, being away from work without permission, or not obeying an order, and "Service Canada decides if you were fired for misconduct, not your employer" (doc.txt). If Service Canada disqualifies you, they will tell you in a letter that you cannot get regular EI benefits, and "you have the right to ask them to reconsider their decision," and "You must ask for this in writing within 30 days of finding out about Service Canada's decision" (doc.txt).
