**Earnings** (item 16): Typically refers to the profits or net income of a business during a specific period. Earnings represent the financial performance of a company and are a key indicator of its profitability.

**Dividends** (item 15): Payments made by a company to its shareholders, typically in the form of cash or additional shares of stock. They represent a portion of the company's profits that is distributed to its owners.

**The key difference:** Earnings are the profits (net income) a company generates during a period — a measure of its financial performance and profitability. Dividends, by contrast, are not the profits themselves but rather a *portion* of those profits that the company chooses to pay out to its shareholders, usually as cash or additional shares of stock. In short, earnings are what the company earns, while dividends are a distribution of part of those earnings to the owners.
