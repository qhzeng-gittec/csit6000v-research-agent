The document describes two acquisitions and their consequences:

**Amazon's acquisition of Whole Foods Market (August 28, 2017, for approximately $13.2 billion)**

- Integration with Amazon's online services: "By acquiring Whole Foods Market, Amazon obtained brick-and-mortar grocery store locations that it was able to integrate with its online services." Shoppers with an Amazon Prime membership "are eligible for discounts and free pickup or delivery of Whole Foods Market groceries in selected zip codes," and "Amazon Hub Lockers—where consumers can pick up products purchased on Amazon's website—are often located in Whole Foods Markets."
- Competitive effects in grocery retail: "Amazon's acquisition of Whole Foods Market may have increased competition in the grocery retail market." "Competitive pressure from Amazon may have incentivized other grocery retailers to start offering online delivery services"—Walmart launched an online delivery service in selected cities in 2017, Kroger did so in 2018, and in 2020 Walmart launched Walmart+, "a membership delivery service that does not have a minimum order requirement, similar to an Amazon Prime membership." "Consumers may have benefited from food retailers offering their own online delivery services, particularly as many of these stores offer free delivery on orders over $35."
- Pressure on third-party services: "These changes may have also increased pressure on other online grocery delivery services, such as Instacart... the service launched in 2012 and stopped delivering groceries from Whole Foods in 2019."
- Concerns about dominance: "Amazon's acquisition of Whole Foods Market raised concern about its growing dominance in the retail industry, particularly in e-commerce." According to eMarketer, "Amazon had the greatest share of e-commerce sales at 38.7% in 2020; Walmart had the second-greatest share at 5.3%," and the House Subcommittee on Antitrust staff report finds that "a market share of 50% or higher may be a more credible estimate of Amazon's share of online sales, and that over 60% of all U.S. online product searches begin on Amazon."
- Data and bargaining power: "Through its acquisition of Whole Foods, Amazon gained access to additional consumer data, strengthening its bargaining power with suppliers."
- Related vertical integration (private label AmazonBasics, its own delivery system, with reported investment of "$60 billion since 2014 in its delivery network") means that "[b]y integrating vertically, Amazon may be able to further strengthen its position in e-commerce; if, for example, it is able to provide faster delivery, consumers could benefit even if it becomes more difficult for other companies to compete."

**Facebook's acquisition of Instagram (agreement announced April 9, 2012, for $1 billion)**

- Antitrust scrutiny: "On December 9, 2020, the FTC filed a lawsuit against Facebook, alleging that 'Facebook has maintained its monopoly position by buying up companies that present competitive threats,'" and "A coalition of 46 state attorneys general, led by New York Attorney General Letitia James, filed a parallel lawsuit against Facebook, also alleging that Facebook acquired companies to eliminate competitive threats." "Both lawsuits specifically mention Facebook's acquisitions of Instagram and WhatsApp, a messaging app for mobile devices."
- Intent to eliminate a competitor: Prior to the acquisition, Facebook CEO Mark Zuckerberg stated in an internal email that "Instagram has become a large and viable competitor to us on mobile photos, which will increasingly be the future of photos." "This statement has been used to support the claim that Facebook acquired Instagram with the intention of eliminating a potential competitor."
- Growth and profitability of Instagram: "Instagram was a relatively new company when it was acquired, and grew rapidly thereafter, from about 100 million monthly active users (MAUs) in February 2013 to 500 million MAUs in June 2016 and 1 billion MAUs in June 2018." "As it grew in popularity, Instagram was able to use Facebook's resources, such as its advertising services and its infrastructure, which hosts and processes large amounts of consumer data. These have been key to the profitability of Instagram."
- Counterfactual: "It is possible that without the merger, Instagram would have been among the platforms that have struggled to compete in digital markets because of resource constraints," as illustrated by Friendster, which "turned down a $30 million buyout offer from Google in 2003 but then struggled with technical difficulties as its user base grew; users left the platform for other social media sites, and Friendster eventually closed down."
- Evaluation difficulty: "It is unclear how successful Instagram would have been had it not been acquired by Facebook, illustrating the difficulty of predicting whether a nascent firm could become a viable competitor," and "At that time, Instagram was described as a photo-sharing app, arguably competing with apps like Photobucket and Flickr, rather than with Facebook."
