

Based solely on `doc.txt`, the three vehicle types differ as follows:

**Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs)**
- Powered by an internal combustion engine (running on conventional or alternative fuel) *plus* an electric motor that uses energy stored in a battery.
- Do **not** require a plug to charge the battery; instead they charge via regenerative braking (using the electric motor as a generator) and the internal combustion engine.
- The electric motor's extra power allows a smaller engine, giving better fuel economy without sacrificing performance, while retaining the power and range of conventional vehicles.

**Plug-In Hybrid Electric Vehicles (PHEVs)**
- Also powered by conventional fuel plus a battery-powered electric motor, but have **larger battery packs than HEVs**, which makes it possible to drive using only electric power for an "all-electric range" of about 10 to 40 miles in current models.
- Can be charged in several ways: by an outside electric power source (plugging in), by the internal combustion engine, or through regenerative braking.
- If never plugged in, their fuel economy is about the same as a similarly sized HEV; if fully charged and driven shorter distances than the all-electric range, they can run on electric power only.

**All-Electric Vehicles (EVs)**
- Use **only** a battery to store the electrical energy that powers the motor — they use no other fuel at all.
- Batteries are charged by plugging the vehicle into an electric power source.
- The EPA considers EVs zero-emission vehicles because their motors produce no exhaust or emissions (though electricity production may contribute to air pollution).
- Have a shorter range per charge than most conventional vehicles per tank of gas; manufacturers typically target a minimum range of 100 miles, which the Federal Highway Administration says is sufficient for more than 90% of U.S. household vehicle trips.

**In short:** HEVs and PHEVs both combine an internal combustion engine with an electric motor, but PHEVs have larger batteries and can be plugged in to charge, enabling a 10–40 mile all-electric range. EVs have no combustion engine or other fuel at all — they run purely on a battery charged by plugging in, and are considered zero-emission.
