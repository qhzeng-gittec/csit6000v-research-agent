

According to `doc.txt`, informal financial services fall into two broad categories — user-owned services and services offered for profit — and the document gives the following examples:

**User-owned informal financial services**
- **Simple reciprocal arrangements between neighbours** — e.g., *kuri kalyanam* among Moslems in Kerala State, southern India (a cash gift brought to a feast, later returned doubled by the host), and a kind of *hui* in Vietnam involving pooling of resources for one person, reciprocated later by others.
- **Savings clubs** — e.g., an accumulating savings club in Mexico, where neighbours place frequent but irregular savings with trusted shopkeepers and receive the cash back (with no interest) just before Christmas.
- **Rotating savings and credit associations (ROSCAs)** — described as an extremely common phenomenon, e.g., "partners" in Jamaica and Britain, *bui* in Vietnam, *njangi* in Cameroon, rickshaw ROSCAS in Dhaka, Bangladesh (drivers save a set amount daily until a rickshaw is bought and allocated by lottery), and "merry-go-rounds" among Kikuyu women in Kenya, used to accumulate money for household utensils or clothes.
- **Insurance funds** — e.g., *iddir* (funeral funds) in urban Ethiopia, which pay out for funeral expenses and sometimes cover other costs such as medical expenses or losses due to fire or theft.
- **Building societies** — e.g., a building society in a lower-middle-class area of Dhaka, Bangladesh, where 165 Public Works Department employees save 200 taka a month and lend the accumulated cash to members to buy land and building materials.
- **Systems of co-operative business finance.**

**Informal financial services for profit**
- **Deposit takers (money-guards)** — e.g., a mobile *alajo* in Nigeria, who goes door-to-door by bicycle, takes small daily savings from customers, and charges a negative interest rate (deducting one day's savings per month from withdrawals).
- **Money-lenders** — e.g., "Tamilians" in Cochin, India, who lend to poor slum dwellers on a daily basis with set terms (3 rupees deducted at source per 100 rupees lent, repaid over ten weeks).
- **Pawnbrokers** — e.g., a goldsmithing-shop pawnbroker in Vijayawada, Western India, who lends against gold, silver, and other collateral.
- **Trade credit and hire purchase.**

The document also notes, more generally, that informal services available include savings facilities, provision of credit for consumption, and funding for predictable but expensive events such as marriages and funerals.
