

**Introduction**
- S. aureus is a major bacterial human pathogen causing a wide variety of clinical manifestations.
- Infections are common in both community-acquired and hospital-acquired settings.
- Treatment remains challenging due to the emergence of multi-drug resistant strains such as MRSA.
- S. aureus is found in the environment and in normal human flora on the skin and mucous membranes (most often the nasal area).
- It does not normally cause infection on healthy skin, but if it enters the bloodstream or internal tissues, it may cause a variety of potentially serious infections.
- Transmission is typically by direct contact, though some infections involve other transmission methods.

**Epidemiology**
- S. aureus (including drug-resistant strains such as MRSA) is found on the skin and mucous membranes, and humans are the major reservoir.
- It is estimated that up to half of all adults are colonized.
- Approximately 15% of the population persistently carry S. aureus in the anterior nares.
- Some populations have higher colonization rates (up to 80%): health care workers, persons who use needles regularly (diabetics and IV drug users), hospitalized patients, and immunocompromised individuals.
- S. aureus can be transmitted person-to-person by direct contact or by fomites.
