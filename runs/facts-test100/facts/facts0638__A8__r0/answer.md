

The article is part of a special PCD collection of 20 articles published from January 2020 through November 2022, which highlights efforts to prevent, manage, and control traditional CVD risk factors. The collection uses multiple data sources collected before or during the pandemic, and it advances understanding of effective CVD risk management and intervention programs in multiple settings, in the general population and among high-risk groups, with a health equity lens.

Within this framework, hypertension is treated as one of the central CVD risk factors. The article states that the greatest contributors to CVD-related years of life lost globally are tobacco exposure, hypertension, high body mass index, and high fasting plasma glucose. It reports that hypertension affects more than 4 billion people worldwide, representing a near doubling in the absolute prevalence of hypertension since 1990. In the US, nearly half of adults (47%) have hypertension, but only about 1 in 4 (24%) have their condition under control.

The article also emphasizes that disparities in hypertension, stroke, and stroke mortality exist, exhibiting significant sociodemographic, for example racial, and geographic, for example rural–urban, county, and zip code, variations. Disparities are largely influenced by demographic, socioeconomic, and environmental factors. In the US, disparities in CVD morbidity, mortality, and risk factors have persisted for decades, with concerning stagnation and significant upward trends since the early 2000s.

On the pediatric side, the article describes a study by Goulding and colleagues that used National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey data collected from 2011 through 2018 to provide estimates of the prevalence of high blood pressure among US children aged 8 to 17 years. The authors documented that elevated blood pressure was most prevalent among children who were older, male, or non-Hispanic Black, with factors beyond inequalities in body weight likely contributing to disparities in elevated blood pressure.

The article further describes intervention programs that are evaluated, such as behavioral modifications strengthening chronic disease awareness, use of self-measured blood pressure monitoring, and sodium intake reduction. It also mentions that zip code variations in infant mortality rates have been documented in association with a high prevalence of maternal cardiometabolic high-risk conditions, which include chronic or gestational hypertension, in 2 counties in Texas. The findings from these articles, the article concludes, could direct efforts to implement appropriate strategies to prevent, manage, and control CVD in populations at high risk.
