**Getting the judge to dismiss the case**
- In Texas, the decision to dismiss a criminal case can lie within the discretion of the judge, and a dismissal can happen at any stage, from the initial filing of charges to trial.
- A dismissal occurs when the judge determines there is not enough evidence to proceed, that the prosecution has failed to meet its burden of proof beyond a reasonable doubt, or in some instances when there are procedural errors or violations of the defendant's constitutional rights.
- A judge will not review a case for errors or deficiencies on their own. Instead, your criminal defense lawyer must petition the court to dismiss the case by filing a proper motion that sets forth the facts and arguments supporting the dismissal.
- The document notes that motion practice involves complicated legal requirements and procedures, and recommends having an experienced defense attorney who can identify grounds for dismissal and file persuasive motions.

**Expunging your record**
- A dismissal does not mean the charge, arrest, or citation are expunged from your record. In Texas, dismissed cases are typically still part of your criminal record, but they may be eligible for expunction or nondisclosure in certain circumstances.
- Expunction completely removes the record of the case, as if it never happened, while nondisclosure limits access to the record by most employers and the public.
