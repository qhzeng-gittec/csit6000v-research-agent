**Leonard Kleinrock**
- "Leonard Kleinrock at MIT published the first paper on packet switching theory in July 1961 and the first book on the subject in 1964." (doc.txt)
- "Kleinrock convinced Roberts of the theoretical feasibility of communications using packets rather than circuits, which was a major step along the path towards computer networking." (doc.txt)
- After Roberts' 1965 experiment showed that "the circuit switched telephone system was totally inadequate for the job," the text notes that "Kleinrock's conviction of the need for packet switching was confirmed." (doc.txt)
- During ARPANET development, "the network measurement system was prepared by Kleinrock's team at UCLA." (doc.txt)
- "Due to Kleinrock's early development of packet switching theory and his focus on analysis, design and measurement, his Network Measurement Center at UCLA was selected to be the first node on the ARPANET." (doc.txt)
- In September 1969, "BBN installed the first IMP at UCLA and the first host computer was connected," and "One month later, when SRI was connected to the ARPANET, the first host-to-host message was sent from Kleinrock's laboratory to SRI." (doc.txt)

**Lawrence G. Roberts**
- Licklider "convinced his successors at DARPA, Ivan Sutherland, Bob Taylor, and MIT researcher Lawrence G. Roberts, of the importance of this networking concept." (doc.txt)
- In 1965, "working with Thomas Merrill, Roberts connected the TX-2 computer in Mass. to the Q-32 in California with a low speed dial-up telephone line creating the first (however small) wide-area computer network ever built." (doc.txt) The experiment showed that "the time-shared computers could work well together, running programs and retrieving data as necessary on the remote machine," (doc.txt)
- "In late 1966 Roberts went to DARPA to develop the computer network concept and quickly put together his plan for the "ARPANET", publishing it in 1967." (doc.txt)
- While BBN built the IMPs, "the network topology and economics were designed and optimized by Roberts working with Howard Frank and his team at Network Analysis Corporation," (doc.txt)
- In July 1972, "Roberts expanded its utility by writing the first email utility program to list, selectively read, file, forward, and respond to messages." (doc.txt)
