

Based on `doc.txt`, the main findings from the industry survey (drawn from a strategic sample of more than 300 digital leaders from more than 50 countries and territories) are:

**Confidence in journalism**
- Just 47% of editors, CEOs, and digital executives are confident about the prospects for journalism in the year ahead, with about 12% expressing low confidence. Concerns include rising costs, declining advertising revenue, slowing subscription growth, and increasing legal and physical harassment. A reason for optimism is that closely fought elections in the US and elsewhere could temporarily boost consumption and interest, though with potential further damage to trust.

**Declining social media referral traffic**
- 63% of respondents are worried about a sharp decline in referral traffic from social media. Chartbeat data cited in the report shows traffic to news sites from Facebook fell 48% in 2023, and from X/Twitter fell 27%. In response, 77% say they will focus more on their own direct channels, 22% will cut costs, and 20% will experiment with alternative third-party platforms.
- Publishers plan to put more effort into WhatsApp (+61 net score) and Instagram (+39) following Meta's opening of broadcast channels, while interest in TikTok (+55) and YouTube (+44) remains strong. Google Discover is becoming more important but volatile. Sentiment toward Facebook (-38) and X/Twitter (-39) has worsened.

**Content strategy**
- Publishers plan to create more video (+64 net score), newsletters (+52), and podcasts (+47), but roughly the same number of news articles. Notably, 54% admit their companies are mostly focused on maximising attention rather than being respectful of their audience's time (37%).

**News avoidance and fatigue**
- Selective news avoidance and news fatigue are major concerns for media companies trying to sustain interest in difficult stories (e.g., Gaza and Ukraine). Counter-strategies considered very important include better explanation of complex stories (67%), more solutions-oriented/constructive storytelling (44%), and more inspirational human stories (43%). There was less support for more positive (21%) or entertaining (18%) news.

**Business/revenue**
- 80% say subscription and membership will be an important revenue stream, ahead of display and native advertising. Most publishers with a paid model report slightly increased or stable subscription numbers despite the difficult economic outlook.

**AI and licensing**
- With publishers pursuing licensing deals with AI platforms, there is little optimism that benefits will be shared equally: 35% believe most of the money would go to big publishers, and 48% feel there would be very little money for any publisher.
- The most important use of AI for publishers is back-end news automation (56%), followed by better recommendations (37%) and commercial uses (28%). Publishers are ambivalent about AI for content creation, which over half consider the biggest reputational risk.

**Emerging technology**
- Experimental interfaces such as AR/VR glasses, lapel pins, and other wearables will feature in the year ahead, but respondents consider existing voice-activated devices (headphones, smart speakers) upgraded with AI to be the most likely option (41%) to displace or supplement the smartphone in the medium term.
