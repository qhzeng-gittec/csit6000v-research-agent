

Here is what the document says about the Supreme Court's Code of Conduct, in plain terms:

**What it is and when it came about**
- On November 13, 2023, the Supreme Court announced it was, for the first time, adopting a Code of Conduct "to set out succinctly and gather in one place the ethics rules and principles that guide the conduct of the Members of the Court" (doc.txt).
- The Court noted that most of the rules "are not new" and that the Code "largely represents a codification of principles that we have long regarded as governing our conduct" (doc.txt). In other words, it mostly wrote down rules the Justices already followed.

**What it contains**
- The Code "is a set of five ethical canons and accompanying commentary," and the canons are "nearly the same as the existing canons in the Code of Conduct for U.S. Judges, which applies only to lower federal judges" (doc.txt).
- "While many provisions of the Supreme Court Code generally address conflicts of interest and appearances of impropriety, specific Code provisions discuss requirements related to financial activities and compliance with the EIGA" (doc.txt).
- "Canon 4(H) declares the Justices' commitment to comply with financial disclosure laws," and the commentary states that the Justices comply with the EIGA, STOCK Act, and current Judicial Conference regulations on financial disclosure (doc.txt).
- The commentary also says the Justices comply with Judicial Conference regulations on "gifts, foreign gifts and decorations, and outside earned income, honoraria, and employment" (doc.txt).
- The document notes the Code "seemingly does not impose any new financial disclosure requirements on the Justices" (doc.txt).

**What it does not do**
- "The Code, however, does not include any enforcement mechanisms, meaning there is no process to address alleged violations of the Code" (doc.txt).
- Because of this, the document says "compliance with federal ethics laws may be left to the discretion of the Justices" (doc.txt).

**Related context the document raises**
- There is "some uncertainty as to whether these regulations apply to the Supreme Court," since the Chief Justice explained in 2011 that the Judicial Conference "is an instrument for the management of the lower federal courts, its committees have no mandate to prescribe rules or standards for any other body" (doc.txt).
- Even so, the Justices "have acknowledged that they file 'the same financial disclosure reports as other federal judges'" (doc.txt).
- The document also mentions that proposed legislation, including congressional attempts to enforce the Code through legislation or oversight, "may raise questions regarding Congress's authority to regulate the Supreme Court," and that this authority "is untested" (doc.txt).
