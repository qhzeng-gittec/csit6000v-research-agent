- **Legislative framework**: "Generally, U.S. law prohibits commercial enterprises (i.e., nonfinancial firms) from operating banks" (doc.txt). "Since at least as far back as the Glass-Steagall Act (Sections 20, 21, 26, and 32 of the Banking Act of 1933; P.L. 73-66), and then in the Bank Holding Company Act, Congress has sought to separate banking enterprises that take deposits and make loans from commercial enterprises producing and selling goods and services" (doc.txt). "The separation aims to prevent one company from both offering a product and the loan to purchase that product funded with deposits publicly guaranteed by FDIC insurance" (doc.txt).
- Industrial loan companies (ILCs) are an exception: "ILCs are financial institutions charted in six states that function much like typical banks. Unlike other banks, ILCs can be owned by nonfinancial, commercial firms" (doc.txt). "Under the Bank Holding Company Act (12 U.S.C. 1841 (c)(2)(h)), ILCs, provided they meet certain criteria, are exempt from the definition of bank" (doc.txt), so a company that owns a bank is not a bank holding company and is not subject to Federal Reserve oversight. However, "ILCs are considered state banks for the purposes of the FDIC Act (12 U.S.C. 1813 (a)(2))" and "are defined as banks and thus eligible for deposit insurance" (doc.txt). "This patchwork legal framework creates the opportunity for a company with other commercial interests to offer banking services, obtain deposit insurance, and operate nationally without being subject to consolidated supervision by the Federal Reserve System" (doc.txt).
- **Relevant history**: "In the mid-2000s, both **Walmart** and **Home Depot** began unsuccessful attempts to establish or acquire ILCs" (doc.txt). "Both ultimately dropped their attempts in the face of public opposition" (doc.txt). "The FDIC imposed an official moratorium on considering new insurance applications from ILCs between July 2006 and January 2008, and Section 603 of the Dodd-Frank Act imposed another temporary statutory moratorium between July 2010 and July 2013" (doc.txt).
- "The FDIC did not grant an ILC insurance application again until March 2020, when it approved two ILCs—**Nelnet** and **Square**—for deposit insurance" (doc.txt). "**Square** offers many of the same services as the Big Techs in this report, including mobile wallet with payment and peer-to-peer transfer functions" (doc.txt).
