

# New OTC Eczema Probiotic: Key Features

**Overview**
- NIAID (National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, part of NIH) research has led to a new over-the-counter topical eczema probiotic.
- The product, called **Defensin**, is formulated by Skinesin (Skinesa) and is based on **Roseomonas mucosa (R. mucosa)**, a bacterium naturally present on healthy skin.
- It is the result of seven years of research in NIAID's Laboratory of Clinical Immunology and Microbiology (LCIM).
- The probiotic could simplify or complement current eczema management when used in consultation with a healthcare provider.

**About the Probiotic (R. mucosa)**
- R. mucosa is a **commensal bacterium** — it occurs naturally as part of the typical skin microbiome.
- Eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition affecting about 20% of children and 10% of adults worldwide, causing dry, itchy skin and a compromised skin barrier that retains moisture and blocks allergens.
- People with eczema have microbiome imbalances and are deficient in certain skin lipids (oils). NIAID researchers demonstrated that R. mucosa **helps restore those lipids**.
- Scientists led by Dr. Ian Myles (chief of the LCIM Epithelial Research Unit) isolated and cultured R. mucosa in the lab, ran preclinical (lab/animal) and clinical (human) studies, and made the bacteria available for commercial, non-therapeutic development.

**What It Does for the Skin**
- Specific strains of R. mucosa **reduced eczema-related skin inflammation** and **enhanced the skin's natural barrier function** in both adults and children.
- In Phase 1/2 open-label and Phase 2 blinded, placebo-controlled clinical studies, most people experienced **greater than 75% improvement in eczema severity**.
- Improvement was seen on all treated sites: inner elbows, inner knees, hands, trunk, and neck.
- Participants needed **fewer corticosteroids**, experienced **less itching**, and reported **better quality of life**.
- Benefits **persisted after treatment ended** — therapeutic R. mucosa strains remained on the skin for up to eight months in participants observed for that duration.

**Future Plans**
- NIAID will conduct an additional clinical trial to generate more evidence on efficacy.
- Those data could support an FDA application to have the product regulated as a nonprescription drug, making it accessible to a broader population.
- Study results are expected in 2024.
