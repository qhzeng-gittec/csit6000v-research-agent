**Potential benefits of continuing Adderall**
- **Treated ADHD:** Your providers can discuss the benefits of treating your condition versus the risks of untreated illness during pregnancy.
- **Fertility:** Taking it as directed is not expected to make it harder to get pregnant.
- **Miscarriage:** Not expected to increase the chance of miscarriage.
- **Birth defects:** Most studies suggest first-trimester use does not increase birth defects above the 3–5% background risk; a large study of ADHD stimulants in pregnancy found no increased risk with amphetamines.
- **Child development:** A Danish study (limited by grouping all ADHD medications) suggested no increase in neurodevelopmental disorders, like ADHD, in children of people who continued ADHD medication during pregnancy versus those who stopped.
- **Breastfeeding:** No adverse effects were reported in 4 infants whose mothers took dextroamphetamine, and the benefit may outweigh possible risks.

**Potential detriments**
- **Pregnancy-related problems:** Data are limited, but use has sometimes been associated with poor growth (small size and/or small head size), low birth weight (under 5 lbs 8 oz), and preterm delivery (before week 37).
- **Side effects to monitor:** Weight loss from decreased appetite, changes in heart rate, and changes in blood pressure. The sheet recommends discussing monitoring of these with your provider to improve outcomes for you and your baby.
- **Newborn withdrawal:** It is not known whether use throughout pregnancy could cause withdrawal symptoms in a newborn; this hasn't been well studied.
- **Breastfeeding concerns:** Amphetamine and dextroamphetamine pass into breast milk; effects on infant behavior and brain development are not well studied. Large doses may lower milk supply in newly breastfeeding people. The product label recommends not using it while breastfeeding, though benefits may outweigh risks.
- **If you stop:** Sudden stopping can cause withdrawal in some people, and it's unknown how that may affect pregnancy. Providers may suggest slowly reducing the dose.

**Key takeaway from the document:** Talk with your healthcare providers before making any changes to how you take your medication, and discuss monitoring of side effects and the best treatment plan for you. This information is based on published literature and should not replace medical care and advice from your provider.
