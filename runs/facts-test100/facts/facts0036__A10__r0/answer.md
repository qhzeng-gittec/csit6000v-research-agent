

Based on `doc.txt`, the following vaccine types are described:

**Live-attenuated vaccines** — "stimulate the innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs) with long-term immunity and may develop hypersensitivity. The main drawback of these vaccines is their costly safety and efficacy assessments" (doc.txt).

**Inactivated viral vaccines** — "poorly provoke cellular immune responses which mitigate their efficacy" (doc.txt). An inactivated COVID-19 vaccine was manufactured by Sinovac and Wuhan Institute of Biological Products (Sinopharm) in April 2020 (doc.txt).

**Subunit vaccines** — "safe, with some defects including low immunogenicity, booster or adjuvant requirement, and high cost" (doc.txt).

**Nucleic acid-based vaccines** (next-generation) — "developed based on sequence information. They include DNA or mRNA sequences of antigens that strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses" (doc.txt). Two subtypes:
- **DNA-based**: advantages include "fast production, and the earliest COVID-19 vaccines in clinical trials," plus "their stability in various storage conditions" (doc.txt).
- **RNA-based**: "received more attention from pharmaceutical companies like Pfizer/Biontech and Moderna. In contrast to DNA vaccines, they stimulate effective humoral immune response as TLR ligand without adjuvant, and its sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation" (doc.txt).

**Toll-like receptors (TLRs) and vaccines** — The document links TLRs to vaccines in two ways: (1) live-attenuated vaccines work "by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs)," which drives their innate, cellular, and humoral immune stimulation; and (2) RNA-based vaccines act "as TLR ligand," stimulating an effective humoral immune response without needing an adjuvant (doc.txt).
