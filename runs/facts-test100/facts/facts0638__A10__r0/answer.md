The article discusses hypertension in several contexts related to cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk and management.

Hypertension is identified as one of the greatest contributors to CVD-related years of life lost globally, alongside tobacco exposure, high body mass index, and high fasting plasma glucose. The article states that "Hypertension affects more than 4 billion people worldwide, representing a near doubling in the absolute prevalence of hypertension since 1990." In the United States, "nearly half of adults (47%) have hypertension, but only about 1 in 4 (24%) have their condition under control."

The article notes that "Disparities in hypertension, stroke, and stroke mortality exist, exhibiting significant sociodemographic (eg, racial) and geographic (eg, rural–urban, county, zip code) variations." These disparities are part of a broader pattern where "CVD and its related risk factors are increasingly recognized as growing indicators of global health disparities," with differences in morbidity and mortality existing among high-, middle-, and low-income countries and across ethnic groups.

Regarding intervention and management, the article states that "Intervention programs, such as behavioral modifications strengthening chronic disease awareness, use of self-measured blood pressure monitoring, and sodium intake reduction, are evaluated." These programs are part of broader efforts to prevent, manage, and control traditional CVD risk factors by instituting timely intervention programs, identifying social determinants of health, examining disparities in CVD risks, and implementing collective efforts through community-based approaches.

The article also references a study by Goulding and colleagues who "used National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey data collected from 2011 through 2018 to provide estimates of the prevalence of high blood pressure among US children aged 8 to 17 years." The authors "documented that elevated blood pressure was most prevalent among children who were older, male, or non-Hispanic Black, with factors beyond inequalities in body weight likely contributing to disparities in elevated blood pressure."

Additionally, Salahuddin and colleagues "documented zip code variations in infant mortality rates associated with a high prevalence of maternal cardiometabolic high-risk conditions (chronic or gestational diabetes, chronic or gestational hypertension, smoking during pregnancy, and prepregnancy obesity) in 2 counties in Texas."

The article frames these findings within a special collection of 20 articles published from January 2020 through November 2022, which "advance our understanding of effective CVD risk management and intervention programs in multiple settings — in the general population and among high-risk groups — with a health equity lens."
