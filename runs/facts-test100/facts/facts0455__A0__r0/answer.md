**Overall**
Data from multiple sources indicate that mental health symptoms and substance use increased since the pandemic began. Symptoms include emotional distress and anxiety, depression, and trauma-related conditions. Although 2020 national data were incomplete and survey comparisons are imperfect, most 2020 data suggest increased behavioral health morbidity in the United States over the course of the pandemic.

**Stress and mental health**
- A SHADAC (Robert Wood Johnson Foundation) April 2020 survey found over 90% of U.S. adults reported additional stress caused by the pandemic.
- A Kaiser Family Foundation survey found a rising share of Americans reporting that pandemic-related stress negatively affected their mental health: 32% in March 2020, 45% in April 2020, and 53% by July 2020.
- NCHS (CDC) Household Pulse Survey data suggest the percentage of Americans experiencing symptoms of a mental health disorder increased during the pandemic.
- CDC analysis showed socioeconomic/psychosocial-related emergency department (ED) visits rose in April 2020 versus April 2019, even as total ED visits fell over 40%.
- The document notes that stress can be managed through adaptive coping (social connections, physical activity, hobbies, sleep hygiene) or maladaptive coping (substance use, excessive screen time, risky behaviors), which can worsen functioning long term.

**Suicide**
- One CDC analysis found roughly twice as many U.S. adults reported serious consideration of suicide in the prior 30 days during the pandemic as in 2018 (10.7% vs. 4.3%).
- The Disaster Distress Helpline saw a 335% increase in calls in the first five months of the pandemic (though the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline reported no increase in call volume).
- Effects on suicide attempts and deaths were mixed: ED visits for suicide attempts between March–October 2020 fell versus 2019 (but less than overall ED visits), and preliminary 2020 data suggest suicide deaths may have decreased compared with the prior three years.
- Demographic/regional differences emerged: ED visits for suspected suicide attempts began rising among adolescents—especially girls—in May 2020, and Maryland researchers found suicide mortality increased for Black residents while decreasing for White residents from March to May 2020.

**Substance use and overdoses**
- ODMAP (ONDCP) reported an 11% increase in fatal overdoses and a 19% increase in nonfatal overdoses from March–May 2020 versus the same months in 2019, with nearly 62% of participating counties reporting increases.
- CDC data showed overdose death rates accelerating after the pandemic began, with particularly notable rises in deaths involving synthetic opioids, and increases in deaths involving commonly prescribed opioids and heroin (which had previously been declining).
- ED visits for drug overdoses, including opioid overdoses, were higher from March–October 2020 than in 2019.
- The document also notes people with substance use disorders may face higher risk of contracting SARS-CoV-2 (due to unstable housing, incarceration, or difficulty distancing) and higher risk of severe complications (because substance use can suppress the immune system or impair respiratory function).

In short, the document portrays the pandemic as driving widespread increases in stress, mental health symptoms, suicidal ideation, and drug overdoses, with some countervailing trends in suicide deaths and attempts and notable variation by region and demographic group.
