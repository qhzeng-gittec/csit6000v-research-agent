Based solely on `doc.txt`, Northrop Grumman describes the following types of risk within its cyber threat landscape:

1. **External cybersecurity threats/attacks** – The company works to protect its computing environments and products from cybersecurity threats, using multi-layered defenses, data analytics to detect anomalies, and a 24x7 Cybersecurity Operations Center for threat detection and response. It notes it has experienced cybersecurity incidents in the past (though none have materially affected the company to date).

2. **Insider threats** – The company maintains an insider threat program designed to "identify, assess, and address potential risks from within our Company," evaluated consistent with industry practices, customer requirements, and applicable law (including privacy considerations).

3. **Third-party risk** – The company conducts information security assessments before sharing or allowing the hosting of sensitive data in computing environments managed by third parties, and its standard terms and conditions require certain security protections.

4. **Supply chain / supplier cyber-related risks** – The company requires suppliers to comply with its standard information security terms and conditions and to complete information security questionnaires to "review and assess any potential cyber-related risks depending on the nature of the services being provided," and it provides training and resources to support cybersecurity resiliency in its supply chain.

The document also notes that the CISO's team has expertise spanning cybersecurity threat assessments and detection, mitigation technologies, incident response, cyber forensics, insider threats, and regulatory compliance, and that the company shares threat and vulnerability mitigation information with government, customer, industry, and supplier partners (e.g., the National Defense Information Sharing and Analysis Center) to address cyber threats across the defense industrial base and supply chain.
