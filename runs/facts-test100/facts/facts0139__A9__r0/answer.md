**Hunger**
- You may feel hungry when you first try fasting, and you may struggle at first if you're not used to going for extended periods without food. Much of this could be down to habit: if you've had a set routine for a long time, your body expects food at certain times, and not getting it can trigger feelings of hunger.
- However, fasting decreases hunger hormones and increases fullness, so many people actually report reduced hunger once they are used to it. Most people adapt quite quickly and find it increasingly easy to fast.
- Missing a meal may result in slightly more food being eaten at the next meal, but total energy intake will still be less than if you had eaten both meals.

**Thirst / hydration**
- The document doesn't say you will become thirstier, but it notes that when you eat, you get some water from your food, so during a fast you are missing out on some of the fluids you normally get. This can lead to dehydration, causing side effects such as tiredness and headaches, and dehydration can also be mistaken for hunger, making it harder to stick to the fast.
- To stay hydrated, the best option is water (which can be flavoured with a slice of lemon, orange, lime or cucumber); coffee (black or flavoured with natural extracts), tea (black, green, herbal or fruit), or broth (bone or vegetable) are also suitable options.
