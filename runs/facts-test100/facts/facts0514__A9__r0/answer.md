

According to doc.txt, metformin is used alone or with other medications, including insulin, to treat type 2 diabetes, which is a condition in which the body does not use insulin normally and therefore cannot control the amount of sugar in the blood. Metformin is in a class of drugs called biguanides. It helps to control the amount of glucose (sugar) in the blood by decreasing the amount of glucose absorbed from food, decreasing the amount of glucose made by the liver, and increasing the body's response to insulin, a natural substance that controls the amount of glucose in the blood. The document states that metformin is not used to treat type 1 diabetes, a condition in which the body does not produce insulin. It also notes that this medication may be prescribed for other uses.

Regarding side effects, the document states that metformin may cause changes in blood sugar, and that you should know the symptoms of low and high blood sugar. It lists the following side effects: diarrhea, nausea, stomach discomfort, gas, indigestion, constipation, lack of energy or weakness, change in sense of taste, and headache. You should tell your doctor if any of these symptoms are severe, do not go away, go away and come back, or do not begin for some time after you begin taking metformin. Some side effects can be serious; the document lists chest pain as a serious symptom and instructs you to call your doctor immediately or get emergency treatment if you experience it. The document also states that metformin may cause other side effects, and to call your doctor if you have any unusual problems while taking the medication. If you experience a serious side effect, you or your doctor may send a report to the FDA's MedWatch Adverse Event Reporting program.
