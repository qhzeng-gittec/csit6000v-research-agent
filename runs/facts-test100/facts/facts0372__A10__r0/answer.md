**Introduction**
- *Staphylococcus aureus* is "a major bacterial human pathogen" that causes a wide variety of clinical manifestations (doc.txt).
- Infections are common in both community-acquired and hospital-acquired settings, and treatment is challenging due to "the emergence of multi-drug resistant" strains such as MRSA (doc.txt).
- It is found in the environment and in normal human flora on the skin and mucous membranes, "most often the nasal area," of most healthy individuals (doc.txt).
- It does not normally infect healthy skin, but "if it is allowed to enter the bloodstream or internal tissues," it may cause potentially serious infections (doc.txt).
- "Transmission is typically from direct contact," though some infections involve other methods (doc.txt).

**Epidemiology**
- S. aureus, including drug-resistant strains such as MRSA, is found on the skin and mucous membranes, and "humans are the major reservoir for these organisms" (doc.txt).
- It is estimated that "up to half of all adults are colonized, and approximately 15% of the population persistently carry" S. aureus in the anterior nares (doc.txt).
- Higher colonization rates (up to 80%) occur in "health care workers, persons who use needles on a regular basis" (i.e., diabetics and IV drug users), hospitalized patients, and immunocompromised individuals (doc.txt).
- S. aureus "can be transmitted person-to-person by direct contact or by fomites" (doc.txt).
