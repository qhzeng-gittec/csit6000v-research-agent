**Minerals/ions in seawater that affect performance**
- **Alkaline earth metal ions — Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺**: their complex composition and the insoluble deposits they form during electrolysis make seawater hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) challenging.
- **Chloride ions (Cl⁻)**: Cl⁻ has a high affinity for the metal active sites of the catalysts, with strong depassivation and penetrating properties.
- (Related factor: reduced ionic mobility in seawater diminishes the pH dependence of the HER, so higher applied voltages are needed to reach the desired efficiency.)

**What causes the decrease in performance**
- The Cl⁻ ions strongly adsorb onto the metal active sites of the catalysts; because of their strong depassivation and penetrating properties, this leads to severe degradation/corrosion of the catalyst and its active sites, which hinders direct seawater electrolysis for hydrogen production.
- The insoluble deposits formed by Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ during electrolysis also contribute to the challenge.

**How to prevent performance degradation**
- The main strategy presented in the document is a **protective "armor" layer**: introducing a **V₂O₃ layer by in situ reduction** into a catalyst with low loading of Pt and Ni₃N (forming Pt-Ni₃N@V₂O₃). The V₂O₃ layer acts as an "armor" during electrolysis, markedly reducing the adsorption of Cl⁻ and the alkaline earth cations (Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺) from seawater and preventing corrosion of the active sites on the electrode. With this protection, the catalyst maintains its performance for at least 500 hours at industrial-grade current density.
- The document also lists other strategies developed to address this bottleneck: **selective active site engineering, three-dimensional structure design, carbon layer protection**, and (per Guo et al.) **generating an in situ localized alkaline environment** to enhance the kinetic processes.
