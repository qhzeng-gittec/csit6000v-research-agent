**Uses:** Metformin is a medication in a class of drugs called biguanides. It is used alone or with other medications, including insulin, to treat type 2 diabetes — a condition in which the body does not use insulin normally and therefore cannot control the amount of sugar in the blood. Metformin helps control the amount of glucose (sugar) in the blood by decreasing the amount of glucose absorbed from food, decreasing the amount of glucose made by the liver, and increasing the body's response to insulin. The document notes that Metformin is not used to treat type 1 diabetes.

**Potential side effects:** Metformin may cause changes in blood sugar, and patients should know the symptoms of low and high blood sugar. Side effects to report to a doctor if they are severe, do not go away, or recur include: diarrhea, nausea, stomach discomfort, gas, indigestion, constipation, lack of energy or weakness, change in sense of taste, and headache. A serious side effect listed is chest pain, in which case one should call a doctor immediately or get emergency treatment. The document also states that Metformin may cause other side effects, and patients should call their doctor if they have any unusual problems while taking the medication.
