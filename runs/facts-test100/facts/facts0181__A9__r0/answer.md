**Overall context (2.1):** Since 2010 the government has introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC to tackle non-compliance, securing and protecting over £250 billion that would otherwise have gone unpaid. Significant reforms include the General Anti-Abuse Rule, Accelerated Payments, implementation of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action Plan, and measures to clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance. The stated aim is for everyone to pay the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are.

**Specific strategies:**

1. **Modernising tax debt collection for non-paying businesses (2.2):** A call for evidence on how HMRC can modernise collection of tax debts to reflect the changing economy, including e-commerce and businesses operating in the UK without a physical presence. It also seeks views on HMRC's approach to the small minority of business taxpayers who do not engage with HMRC and delay paying, forcing costly enforcement action. The proposals will not affect taxpayers in temporary financial difficulty who need support to get back onto a sustainable footing, including those affected by the pandemic.

2. **Implementing the OECD's Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules (2.3):** Announced at Spring Budget 2021, with a technical consultation and draft regulations now issued. These rules require advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) to report information to tax authorities on certain prescribed arrangements and structures, including those that could circumvent the Common Reporting Standard. The information will be exchanged with relevant tax authorities to deter non-compliance, assist in identifying and challenging evasion, and support tax policy development.

3. **Improving Transfer Pricing Documentation (2.4):** Legislation in 2022 to require businesses in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action 13, plus a supporting summary audit trail, taking effect from April 2023. The government will continue reviewing the case for an International Dealings Schedule.

4. **Extending tax conditionality to licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland (2.5):** From April 2023, renewal of certain licences will be conditional on applicants completing checks confirming they are appropriately registered for tax. Licensing bodies must obtain confirmation of the check before deciding on renewal, making it harder for traders to operate in the hidden economy. This is consistent with reforms in England and Wales from April 2022, and the government remains committed to exploring wider application of tax conditionality.

5. **Strengthening and modernising ISA compliance and penalties (2.6):** Following a December 2020 announcement on the sufficiency of checks on Innovative Finance ISA managers and the penalties regime, the government is publishing a call for evidence on proposals to enhance ISA compliance, to encourage ISA managers and investors to get things right first time. The proposals apply to all ISA types: Cash, Stocks and Shares, Junior, Lifetime and Innovative Finance.

6. **Consulting on making SDLT reliefs fairer (2.7):** A consultation on Stamp Duty Land Tax reliefs for purchases of mixed property and multiple dwellings, which are being exploited by some purchasers to unfairly reduce their tax liability. The consultation sets out potential changes to make the system fairer and reduce the scope for misuse and incorrect claims.

7. **New stakeholder forum on tackling offshore tax non-compliance (2.8):** An HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents to explore ideas to tackle offshore tax non-compliance, following two spring 2021 discussion documents under HMRC's 'No Safe Havens' strategy. These sought views on reducing offshore non-compliance in relation to non-UK income, gains and assets, and on reducing and preventing international tax debt.

8. **Exploring the potential of VAT split payment (2.9):** An alternative method of VAT collection where the tax element of a digital payment could be remitted directly to HMRC. The work is exploratory, and the government will continue assessing its potential for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation, working collaboratively with the payments industry, including through the Industry Working Group established in 2018.

9. **Publishing an annual report on marketed tax avoidance schemes (2.10):** HMRC is publishing its annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes in the UK, setting out the numbers and characteristics of taxpayers using avoidance schemes in tax year 2019-2020, including data on occupations, locations, ages and average declared incomes.

The document also notes (Chapter 3, 3.1) that the government continues to take action to level the playing field and increase transparency, with the tax system needing to remain fit for the modern world.
