**Hard information**
- It is the type of data large lenders typically collect from borrowers who possess more conventional financial metrics and documentation (e.g., sales fluctuations, costs of inputs, specific industry factors).
- It can be used in automated and statistical underwriting methodologies to price loans.
- It is already quite uniform, so large lenders may already have automated technological systems that can handle large volumes of standardized and digitized financial data, making reporting likely less expensive per applicant.

**Soft information**
- It is gathered by small lenders through relationship lending, meaning they must develop close familiarity with their customers to collect it.
- It contains circumstantial details about factors such as non-standardized business risks, insufficient collateral, or weak or thin (business) credit histories.
- Because of soft information, the loan underwriting process to determine more customized loan products and loan pricing is generally less algorithmic and more labor intensive.
- It is more unique to applicant circumstances, infrequent, and localized, so standardizing the data for electronic collection and reporting is challenging.
- The reporting cost per applicant is likely more expensive for small lenders that lack the volume of applications to justify the costs to convert soft information to digital and secure formats.

In short, hard information is conventional, uniform, and suited to automated underwriting and reporting, while soft information is circumstantial, applicant-specific, and harder to standardize and report.
