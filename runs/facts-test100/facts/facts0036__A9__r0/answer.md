**Live-attenuated vaccines**
- Stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like receptors (TLRs)
- Provide long-term immunity
- May develop hypersensitivity
- Main drawback: costly safety and efficacy assessments

**Inactivated viral vaccines**
- Poorly provoke cellular immune responses, which mitigates their efficacy
- Example: an inactivated COVID-19 vaccine manufactured in April 2020 by Sinovac and Wuhan Institute of Biological Products (Sinopharm)

**Subunit vaccines**
- Safe
- Defects: low immunogenicity, requirement for a booster or adjuvant, and high cost

**Nucleic acid-based vaccines (next-generation)**
- Developed based on sequence information; include DNA or mRNA sequences of antigens
- Strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses
- Advantages: fast production; they were the earliest COVID-19 vaccines in clinical trials
- DNA-based vaccines: a noticeable advantage is stability in various storage conditions
- RNA-based vaccines: received more attention from companies such as Pfizer/Biontech and Moderna; unlike DNA vaccines, they stimulate an effective humoral immune response as a TLR ligand without adjuvant, and their sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation
