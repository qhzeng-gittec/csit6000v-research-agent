**Point 6 — the right to information**
- "Every child and young person has a right to information, in a form that is understandable to them." (doc.txt)
- It covers information "about their health and healthcare," including "information about the choice of health care services available." (doc.txt)
- It includes "the freedom to seek, receive and impart information and ideas, not only orally but also through other means of the child's or young person's choice, such as play and art." (doc.txt)
- It requires that "the language and format used are appropriate to the child's or young person's abilities and level of understanding," and includes "the right of tamariki and rangatahi to have access to information in Te Reo Māori and for those from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds to have access to information in their own language." (doc.txt)
- It requires health professionals to "talk directly to children and young people," and entitles them "to know what is going to happen to them before a procedure occurs and to be given honest information about their condition and treatment outcomes, and to be helped to select and practice strategies for coping." (doc.txt)
- It emphasizes that "Advance preparation for hospitalisation, healthcare procedures or impending surgery provides children and young people with a sense of mastery over the healthcare environment and helps them to cope more effectively with potentially stressful situations." (doc.txt)

**Point 7 — the right to participate in decision-making**
- "Every child and young person has a right to participate in decision-making and, as appropriate to their capabilities, to make decisions about their care." (doc.txt)
- It includes "the right to be involved in decisions about the use, return or disposal of any bodily parts or substances removed, changed or added in the course of health care." (doc.txt)
- It states that "they are always entitled to a second opinion," and that if a child's views "cannot be acted on, they are entitled to an explanation." (doc.txt)
- It requires that "the health professionals caring for them ought to be available, trained and committed to communicating with children and young people," and that "Informed consent is to be sought from children, young people and their families/whānau before they are involved in teaching or research." (doc.txt)
- It extends beyond the individual: "It includes encouraging and supporting children and young people as groups to be involved in consultation on the development, implementation and evaluation of the services, policies and strategies that have an impact on them." (doc.txt)
- It adds that those who agree to participate in teaching or research "must have the opportunity to withdraw at any time without having to give a reason," that "The decision not to participate in teaching or research must not alter access to treatment," and that "Ethical oversight by a Human Research Ethics Committee of all research projects conducted in child healthcare services is part of protecting the children and young people involved." (doc.txt)

**Key differences**
- Point 6 is about the child's right to *receive and impart information* (in understandable form, in their own language, directly from health professionals, with advance preparation for procedures and hospitalisation). Point 7 is about the child's right to *be involved in and make decisions* about their care.
- Point 6 focuses on the content and delivery of information (understandable language/format, honesty about condition and outcomes, advance preparation). Point 7 focuses on decision-making (choices offered, second opinion, explanation when views cannot be acted on, informed consent, group-level consultation on services and policies, and research/teaching consent, withdrawal, and ethical oversight).
- Both points mention communication through play, art and other media, but in different roles: in Point 6 these are means by which children "seek, receive and impart information and ideas," while in Point 7 they are "opportunities to communicate through play, art and other media of the child's or young person's choice" as part of participating with health professionals, including attention to "nonverbal cues and communication" from infants, very young children and those with disabilities. (doc.txt)
