**Minerals/ions in seawater that affect performance**

- Alkaline earth metal ions: "the complex composition of seawater and the insoluble deposits formed by alkaline earth metal ions such as Ca2+, Mg2+ during electrolysis make seawater HER challenging" (doc.txt)
- Chloride (Cl-): "Cl- has a high affinity for the metal active sites of the catalysts as they have strong depassivation and penetrating properties" (doc.txt)
- Additionally, seawater's ionic properties matter: "The pH of natural seawater is typically around 8.0 and reduced ionic mobility in seawater leads to a diminished pH dependence of the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER)... necessitating higher applied voltages to attain the desired efficiency in hydrogen production" (doc.txt)

**What is causing the decrease in performance**

- Chloride adsorption and corrosion: Cl- "has a high affinity for the metal active sites of the catalysts as they have strong depassivation and penetrating properties (13, 14), leading to severe degradation of the catalyst and hindering direct seawater electrolysis for hydrogen production" (doc.txt)
- Insoluble deposits: "the insoluble deposits formed by alkaline earth metal ions such as Ca2+, Mg2+ during electrolysis make seawater HER challenging" (doc.txt)

**How to prevent performance degradation**

- The main strategy in this work is a protective V2O3 layer: "we present a protective strategy by introducing a V2O3 layer by in situ reduction into a catalyst with low loading of Pt and Ni3N. The V2O3 layer acts as an 'armor' during electrolysis, markedly reducing the adsorption of Cl- and alkaline earth cations (Ca2+ and Mg2+) from seawater and preventing corrosion of the active sites on the electrode" (doc.txt). With this protection, "the assembled Pt-Ni3N@V2O3 catalyst exhibits remarkable HER activity in alkalized natural seawater and maintains its performance for at least 500 hours at industrial-grade current density" (doc.txt)
- Other strategies the document mentions: "various strategies have been developed, including selective active site engineering (20, 21), three-dimensional structure design (22, 23), and carbon layer protection (24, 25)" (doc.txt), and Guo et al. "enhanced the kinetic processes by generating an in situ localized alkaline environment, achieving excellent seawater catalytic performance at 60°C" (doc.txt)
