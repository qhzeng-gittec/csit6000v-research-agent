**Methylphenidate (MPH)** — a stimulant
- *Mechanism of action:* Increases dopamine (DA) signalling through multiple actions, including blockade of the DA reuptake transporter, amplification of DA response duration, disinhibition of the dopamine D2 receptor, and amplification of DA tone. It is also an inhibitor of noradrenaline (NE) re-uptake.
- *Risks:* Appears to cause minor increases in blood pressure (BP) and heart rate (HR), with no strong data to suggest it increases the QT interval.
- *Other notes:* Strong evidence that it is effective in improving ADHD symptoms. In Italy, only immediate-release MPH is currently approved for ADHD treatment, and patients must be enrolled in the ADHD medication registry before it can be prescribed. One year of MPH treatment may be beneficial for enduring normalisation of neural correlates of attention, and chronic use in drug-naive boys with ADHD enhanced neuropsychological functioning on "recognition memory" tasks with modest executive demands.

**Atomoxetine (ATX)** — a non-stimulant
- *Mechanism of action:* A selective inhibitor of synaptic re-uptake; in vivo, it specifically increases extracellular DA levels in the prefrontal cortex but not in the striatum, probably by modulating cortical synaptic DA uptake via the NE transporter.
- *Risks:* Limited data suggest it may increase BP and HR in the short term; in the long term it appears to only increase BP. Its effects on the QT interval remain uncertain.
- *Other notes:* Strong evidence that it is effective in improving ADHD symptoms. In Italy, only ATX and immediate-release MPH are currently approved for ADHD treatment, and registry enrollment is required before prescription.

**Dextroamphetamine** — a stimulant
- *Mechanism of action:* Increases the synaptic activity of DA and NE by increasing the release of the neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft, decreasing reuptake back into the presynaptic neuron, and inhibiting their catabolism.
- *Risks:* The document does not list specific risks for dextroamphetamine, but it is grouped with MPH among stimulant medications with strong evidence of effectiveness in improving ADHD symptoms.

**Guanfacine**
- *Mechanism of action:* A selective alpha2A adrenergic receptor agonist that improves working memory by stimulating postsynaptic alpha2A adrenoceptors, strengthening the functional connectivity of prefrontal cortex networks.
- *Risks:* The document does not list specific risks for guanfacine; it is noted as effective in reducing ADHD symptoms.

**General safety and monitoring points from the document:**
- ADHD pharmacological therapies are generally well-tolerated, but concerns about the cardiovascular safety of some of these drugs prompted examination of the effects of ATX and MPH on BP, HR, and ECG parameters.
- Because the current cardiovascular evidence is based on research not specifically designed to investigate these effects, it is difficult to draw firm conclusions.
- Patients receiving pharmacological treatment for ADHD should always be closely monitored for both common and unusual potentially severe adverse effects.
- Drug treatment should be part of a comprehensive treatment plan including psychosocial, behavioural, and educational advice and interventions; in Italy, pharmacological treatment can only be initiated after failure of cognitive behavioural therapy over 6 months or longer.
