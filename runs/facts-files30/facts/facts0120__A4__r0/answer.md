Research on black tea and coronavirus, as described in doc.txt, centers on theaflavins — polyphenols that are characteristic of black tea — and includes three main lines of study:

1. **Neutralization of coronavirus (Clark et al., 1998):** Theaflavins extracted from black tea were shown to be able to neutralize bovine coronavirus (and rotavirus) infections [E1]. The combination of theaflavin fractions (TF1 + TF2a + TF2b + TF3) was more active than the sum of the individual fractions, indicating synergism among the theaflavin components [E2]. The study concluded that theaflavin and theaflavin gallate derivatives have in vitro inactivation activity against both rotavirus and coronavirus, and that the crude black tea extract itself was also able to neutralize the coronavirus [E3].

2. **Virtual screening against SARS-CoV-2 (Lung et al., 2020, Journal of Medical Virology):** In a bioinformatics study screening 83 compounds from the Chinese medicine system for binding to the RNA dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) of SARS-CoV-2, theaflavin emerged as the best compound based on docking score (predicted binding affinity), hydrophobic interactions, and additional hydrogen bonds near the active site of RdRp, confirmed by lower binding energy when docked into the catalytic pocket [E4]. The authors suggested that theaflavins could be used as a lead compound for developing a SARS-CoV-2 inhibitor targeting RdRp, and noted that theaflavins are present in black tea, with the highest theaflavin contents found in the black teas of Assam [E5].

3. **Inhibition of SARS-CoV 3C-like protease (Chen et al., 2005, Evidence Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine):** An earlier study from Taiwan convincingly demonstrated inhibition of SARS-CoV 3C-like protease (3CLPro) activity by theaflavin-3,3'-digallate (TF3) [E6]. In that study, Puer and black tea extracts were more potent than green or oolong tea extracts in inhibiting the chymotrypsin-like (3CLPro) protease of SARS-CoV [E7]. Water extracts of TF2b (theaflavin-3-gallate), TF3 (theaflavin digallate), and tannic acid were the most effective 3CLPro inhibitors, with inhibitory concentrations (IC50) of less than 10 μM [E8].

The document also cautions that, regarding the SARS-CoV-2 RdRp findings, further in vivo, animal, and clinical trials would be required to carry the research forward [E6].
