

Both installment loans and mortgages are loans in which "the amount that will be paid by the borrower will include the original amount plus interest." (doc.txt) Both are repaid through amortization, "the process of spreading out a loan into a series of fixed payments" (doc.txt); indeed, "amortizing a mortgage is similar to amortizing other loans." (doc.txt)

Differences: A mortgage is "a long term loan that is used for the purchase of a house" (doc.txt), and the lending agency "requires that the house be used as collateral for the loan" (doc.txt); if the holder "is unable to make the payments the lender can take possession of the house" (doc.txt). Mortgages "generally tend to be for longer time periods than an installment loan" (doc.txt), and "the terms of the mortgage will often change over the course of the mortgage" (doc.txt) — a twenty-year mortgage may be signed for a five-year term, after which "a new agreement will be required and the conditions of the mortgage usually change" (doc.txt). Mortgage amortization "generally involves many more payment periods" (doc.txt). Installment loans, by contrast, finance "major purchases such as vehicles or furniture" (doc.txt), with terms "from a few years to perhaps twenty years or more (in the case of real estate)" (doc.txt). When to choose which: The document ties each loan to its purpose: an installment loan is the option for a qualifying consumer buying items like a vehicle or television, while a mortgage is the loan used to purchase a house. The document does not discuss other factors for choosing between them.
