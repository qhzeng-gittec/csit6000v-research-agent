

Similarities: both are loans in which the amount paid by the borrower "will include the original amount plus interest" (doc.txt). Both are repaid in regular installment payments, each of which "will include an interest charge" (doc.txt), and both can be amortized, "the process of spreading out a loan into a series of fixed payments" (doc.txt); "amortizing a mortgage is similar to amortizing other loans" (doc.txt).

Differences:
- Purpose: a mortgage is "a long term loan that is used for the purchase of a house" (doc.txt); installment loans cover "major purchases such as vehicles or furniture" (doc.txt).
- Collateral: the lender requires "that the house be used as collateral," so if the holder "is unable to make the payments the lender can take possession of the house" (doc.txt). No such collateral is described for installment loans.
- Length: "Mortgages generally tend to be for longer time periods than an installment loan" (doc.txt), and mortgage amortization "generally involves many more payment periods" (doc.txt).
- Terms: "the terms of the mortgage will often change over the course of the mortgage" (doc.txt); a twenty-year mortgage may be signed for a five-year term, after which "a new agreement will be required" (doc.txt).

Choosing: An installment loan suits a consumer who "cannot afford to pay $25000 or more for a new vehicle" and "may not want to wait three or four years until they have saved enough money" (doc.txt). A mortgage is the option for buying a house. The document gives no further guidance on choosing between them.
