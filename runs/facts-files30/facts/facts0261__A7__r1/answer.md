## Before the Federal Income Tax
- "The tax mechanisms used during first 150 years or so of U.S. tax history bears little resemblance to the current system of taxation." (doc.txt)
- The Constitution restricted "direct" taxation by the federal government, so "the federal government relied on indirect taxes including taxes on imports (tariffs) and excise taxes." (doc.txt)
- "Tariffs were the major source of U.S. government receipts from the beginning of the nation up to the early 1900's. For example, in 1800 custom duties comprised about 84% of government receipts." (doc.txt)
- Internal revenue in the early 20th century came mainly from alcohol: "In 1900 over 60% of internal revenue collections came from alcohol excise taxes with another 20% from tobacco excise taxes." (doc.txt)
- The framers were wary of taxing power: "Taxation of the American Colonies by a distant and corrupt England was a driving force behind the American Revolution. Consequently, they believed in decentralized taxation and delegated most public revenue collection to localities, which relied primarily on property taxes." (doc.txt)
- In peacetime the federal government met expenses with modest excise taxes and tariffs; in wartime (e.g., the War of 1812) "federal taxes were temporarily raised to finance the war or pay down the ensuing debts," then reduced after the crisis.
- The Civil War "initiated an increase in both excise tax and tariff rates. Government revenue collections increased by a factor of seven between 1863 and 1866." (doc.txt) The most significant policy was "the institution of the first national income tax," with "a maximum rate of 10% along with generous exemptions" so "only about 10% of households were subject to any income tax." (doc.txt) It "generated over 20% of federal revenues in 1865" but "in 1872 it was allowed to expire." (doc.txt)
- The Populist movement of the late 1800s revived interest in an income tax "as a vehicle of social justice." (doc.txt) A federal income tax with a $4,000 exemption "was instituted in 1894 but the Supreme Court ruled it unconstitutional in 1895." (doc.txt) "Eventually, in 1913 the 16th Amendment was ratified creating the legal basis for the federal income tax." (doc.txt)
- The initial tax was progressive but modest: "in the first few years only about 2% of households paid any income tax – tax rates of only 1%-7% prevented it from generating significant revenues." (doc.txt)

## The Growth of Direct Taxation
- **World War I:** "the top marginal rate increased dramatically from 7% in 1915 to 67% in 1917." (doc.txt) Corporate taxes "accounting for over one-quarter of internal revenue collections in 1917," and "In 1916 the estate tax was created." (doc.txt)
- **1920s:** "Unlike previous wars, much of the tax system laid down during World War I remained in place after the war. In the period from 1910 to 1925 tariffs fell from about half of government receipts to less than 15%." (doc.txt) The new income taxes "made up nearly half of government receipts in the mid 1920s." (doc.txt)
- **Great Depression/New Deal:** "In 1932 tax rates were increased in an attempt to boost federal revenue." (doc.txt) The most significant measure was "the creation of old-age insurance" (Social Security), funded by "a separate tax, split equally between employers and employees." (doc.txt)
- **World War II:** Personal exemptions "were reduced by half between 1939 and 1942 – meaning the income tax reached well into the middle class for the first time." (doc.txt) "the top marginal tax rate reached a record high of 94% in 1944," and the government began "withholding federal taxes from an employee's paycheck." (doc.txt) As a result, "the number of individual taxpayers grew from 3.9 million in 1939 to 42.6 million in 1945, and federal income tax collections over the period leaped from $2.2 billion to $35.1 billion." (doc.txt) "In 1940, federal income tax had accounted for only 16 percent of the taxes collected by all levels of government; by 1950 the federal income tax produced more than 51 percent of all collections." (doc.txt)
- **Postwar to 1980s:** "Changes to the tax system between the end of World War II and the 1980's were generally minor." (doc.txt) The Social Security tax rose from an initial 2% to 6.13% by 1979; Medicare and Medicaid were established in the 1960s; and "Across-the-board tax cuts in 1964 reduced marginal rates for both low- and high-income households (the top marginal rate fell from 91% in 1963 to 70% in 1965)." (doc.txt)

## From the "Reagan Revolution" to the Bush Tax Cuts
- "The Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981 (ERTA) enacted the largest tax cut in American history" (measured in constant dollars), based on supply-side theory; it "phased in a reduction in the top tax rate from 70% to 50%." (doc.txt) In reality, "ERTA resulted in the largest reduction in federal revenues of any tax bill since World War II." (doc.txt)
- "in 1982, the largest peacetime tax increase was passed," (doc.txt) and "Social Security reforms were enacted in 1983 that increased Social Security tax rates and initiated taxation of some benefits." (doc.txt)
- The Tax Reform Act of 1986 "reduced top income tax rates even further – from 50% in 1986 to 28% in 1988" and "lowered the top corporate tax rate from 46% to 34%." (doc.txt)
- Despite the cuts, "total federal revenues increased about 76% from 1980-1988 while GDP increased 83%," and "the share of revenues from both individual and corporate taxation fell (by 9% and 16% respectively) while the portion from social insurance taxes increased by 38%." (doc.txt)
- "the first President Bush reneged on his campaign promise of 'no new taxes' and agreed to a compromise tax proposal in 1990 that raised the top marginal tax bracket to 31%." (doc.txt)
- "President Clinton reinstated additional progressivity in 1993 by creating the 36% and 39.6% individual tax brackets. In 1993, the corporate tax rate was increased slightly to 35%." (doc.txt)
- "The most recent important tax legislation was the $1.35 trillion Bush tax cut passed in 2001," which lowered individual rates across the board, scheduled repeal of the estate tax in 2010, and increased retirement contributions; "the top marginal bracket fell from 39.6% in 2001 to 38.6% in 2002 but eventually fell to 35.0% in 2006." (doc.txt) A smaller 2003 cut "lowered the maximum tax rate on capital gains and dividends," and "the 2009 American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009 instituted or expanded various tax credits such as a payroll tax credit of $400 per worker and an expanded tax credit for college tuition." (doc.txt)

## Summary Data on U.S. Tax History
- "federal tax revenues have grown considerably during the 20th century, even after adjusting for inflation. A large increase in federal tax collections occurred during World War II, with relatively consistent growth after about 1960." (doc.txt)
- "total tax collections have generally grown as a percentage of GDP over the 20th century," with the largest leap during World War II; "federal tax revenues now substantially exceed state and local tax revenues"; and there has been "the decline in federal taxes as a percentage of GDP since 2000... federal taxes as a percentage of GDP were lower in 2009 than in any year since the 1940s." (doc.txt)
- In composition: "at the beginning of the century federal taxation was dominated by excise taxes"; "Corporate taxes became the most significant source of federal revenues for the period 1918-1932"; "Personal income taxes became the largest source of federal revenues in 1944 and have remained so"; and "Since about 1950, social insurance taxes have increased their share of federal revenues from about 10% up to nearly 40%." (doc.txt)
- For state and local taxes: "the two major trends that are evident are a decline in the importance of property taxes and an increase in the importance of personal income taxes except for a recent reversal of these trends in the last few years." (doc.txt)

The document notes that "The history of taxation is primarily derived from Brownlee (1996)." (doc.txt)
