According to doc.txt, ISPs have transitioned into edge providers mainly through acquisitions and by launching their own streaming/content services:

- **AT&T**: In 2018, AT&T acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, as well as other cable channels [E1]. AT&T also announced plans to introduce a new edge provider, HBO Max, to stream video programming for no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers, with other customers reportedly charged a subscription fee [E2].

- **Comcast**: In 2011, Comcast became the majority owner of NBCUniversal and thereby obtained minority ownership of Hulu, an edge provider that streams video programming to subscribers [E3]. After Walt Disney obtained "full operational control" of Hulu in 2019, Comcast retained its 33% financial stake [E4]. Comcast also announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, Peacock, with three subscription options: a free ad-supported option, a premium version with more programming for a fee, and a premium version with no ads for a higher fee; the premium version is to be offered free to subscribers of Comcast and Cox Communications [E5].

- **Verizon**: In 2015, Verizon acquired AOL, an ISP and edge provider, and in 2016 it acquired the core business of Yahoo, an edge provider [E6]. In 2017, it combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—to create Oath [E7].

In short, the document describes ISPs moving into the edge-provider role by acquiring content/edge businesses (Time Warner, NBCUniversal/Hulu, AOL, Yahoo) and by creating or planning their own streaming services (HBO Max, Peacock, Oath).
