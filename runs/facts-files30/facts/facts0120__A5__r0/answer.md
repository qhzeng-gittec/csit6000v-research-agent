The document (doc.txt) does not discuss "health benefits" of black tea in the usual nutritional sense; instead it reviews black tea's antiviral activity against coronaviruses. The relevant research is:

1. **Clark et al. (1998) – bovine coronavirus:** Theaflavins extracted from black tea were shown to neutralize bovine coronavirus (and rotavirus) infections. Crude black tea extract and individual theaflavin fractions were tested, and the combination of fractions (TF1 + TF2a + TF2b + TF3) was more active than the sum of the individual fractions, indicating synergism among the theaflavin components. The theaflavin and theaflavin gallate derivatives showed in vitro inactivation activity against both rotavirus and coronavirus, and the crude black tea extract itself could neutralize the coronavirus [E1].

2. **Lung et al. (2020) – SARS-CoV-2 (in silico):** In a virtual screening of 83 traditional Chinese medicine compounds for binding to the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) of SARS-CoV-2, theaflavin was the best compound based on its predicted binding affinity (idock score), hydrophobic interactions, and additional hydrogen bonds near the active site, confirmed by a lower binding energy when docking the catalytic pocket of SARS-CoV-2 RdRp. This suggested theaflavins could serve as a lead compound for developing a SARS-CoV-2 RdRp-targeting inhibitor. Theaflavins are present in black tea, with the highest contents in Assam black teas. The authors note that further in vivo, animal, and clinical trials are required [E2, E3].

3. **Chen et al. (2005) – SARS-CoV 3C-like protease:** A Taiwan study demonstrated that Theaflavin-3,3′-digallate (TF3) inhibits the 3C-like protease (3CLPro) activity of SARS-CoV (2002 epidemic). Puer and black tea extracts were more potent than green or oolong tea extracts in inhibiting 3CLPro. A docking screen of a 720-compound natural product library identified tannic acid and TF2b (theaflavin-3-gallate) as active against 3CL protease, and water extracts of TF2b, TF3, and tannic acid were the most effective 3CLPro inhibitors, with IC50 values below 10 μM [E3, E4].

The document does not address confirmed health benefits in humans.
