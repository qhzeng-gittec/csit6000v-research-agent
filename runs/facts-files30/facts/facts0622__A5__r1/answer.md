<answer>
What the Apgar score is:
The Apgar score is a "vitality index" used worldwide for almost every newborn immediately after birth. It is based on five components — skin color, heart rate, reflex irritability, muscle tone, and respiration — each scored from 0 to 2, giving a total in which 7–10 is considered normal and 10 represents the optimal condition [E1].

Significance for health outcomes (from the study in doc.txt, a Danish cohort of over 2.2 million live births):
- Even "normal" scores carry some risk: individuals with Apgar scores of 7–9 — the range current guidelines consider reassuring — still had higher risks of overall mental disorders in childhood, including organic disorders and neurodevelopmental disorders such as intellectual disability, pervasive developmental disorders, childhood autism, and ADHD [E2]. Prior studies cited in the article also found that scores of 7–9 are associated with higher risks of neonatal mortality, neonatal morbidity, and adverse long-term neurological outcomes compared with a score of 10 [E5]. The authors conclude that 5-min Apgar scores "even within the normal range 7–9, are not totally reassuring" [E6].
- Dose-response relationship: the overall risk of mental disorders increased as the Apgar score decreased from 9 toward 7 [E5].
- Compromised (lower) scores: these were associated with elevated risks of organic disorders and neurotic disorders in childhood — findings the authors say were reported for the first time in this study [E2]. They observed 3–5-fold risks of intellectual disability in childhood (up to age 18), and their findings further support that compromised 5-min Apgar scores were associated with childhood autism [E3, E4].
- Early adulthood: compromised 5-min Apgar scores were not found to be associated with mental disorders during early adulthood [E2].

Possible explanations offered by the authors:
- A less-than-optimal Apgar score may be a sign of the cumulative effect of adverse prenatal events (e.g., gestational diabetes, preterm birth, restricted fetal growth); the 7–9 range in particular may indicate "subtle but still detrimental intrauterine insults which will act negatively on fetal brain development" [E7].
- The link to organic disorders implies that adverse prenatal insults such as hypoxia-ischemia, white matter injury, reduced blood flow, or malnutrition can have a long-lasting impact on brain function later in life [E8].
- The association with neurotic disorders may be at least partly mediated by socioeconomic factors, since individuals with compromised Apgar scores tended to be born into families with worse socioeconomic status [E9].
- The authors also note that a distressed infant typically receives resuscitation before the 5-minute Apgar is assigned, so a score of 7–9 may not fully reflect severe conditions that existed prior to the assessment [E10].
