

According to `doc.txt`, the section "Examples of ISPs Becoming Edge Providers" describes the following moves:

**AT&T**
- Acquired a content creator: "In 2018, AT&T acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, as well as other cable channels."
- Announced a new edge provider: "AT&T has announced plans to introduce a new edge provider—HBO Max—to stream video programming for no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers; other customers will reportedly be charged a subscription fee."

**Comcast**
- Gained ownership of an edge provider through a media acquisition: "In 2011, Comcast became the majority owner of NBCUniversal, which owns television networks and broadcast stations, and thus obtained minority ownership of Hulu, an edge provider that streams video programming to subscribers."
- Planned its own streaming service: "Comcast also announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, Peacock," with three subscription options: "a free option supported by ads, a premium version with more programming for a fee, and the premium version with no ads for a higher fee." The premium version "is to be offered for free to subscribers of Comcast and Cox Communications."

**Verizon**
- Acquired existing edge providers: "In 2015, Verizon acquired AOL, an ISP and edge provider, and in 2016, it acquired the core business of Yahoo, an edge provider."
- Combined the acquired products: "It combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—in 2017 to create Oath."

In short, the document shows ISPs transitioning into edge providers by acquiring content/edge businesses (Time Warner, Hulu via NBCUniversal, AOL, Yahoo), bundling acquired edge products into new entities (Oath), and launching their own streaming services (HBO Max, Peacock).
