

## The system itself

EI is "The EI program, which is insurance-based, is designed to protect individuals who have lost their" (doc.txt) "job for reasons outside their control, while they look for new employment or upgrade their skills." (doc.txt). "It may also help unemployed individuals across the country find employment." (doc.txt), and it "provides temporary financial assistance to workers who are sick, pregnant, caring for a newborn or newly adopted child, or caring for a family member who has a serious medical condition." (doc.txt)

History: "The EI program marked its 75th year in 2015, having been implemented in 1940." (doc.txt) "The last major reform occurred in 1996." (doc.txt) "At that time, the program introduced changes to better reflect its primary objective of promoting employment, and to better emphasize that access to the program is linked to" (doc.txt) "significant work attachment." (doc.txt)

Roles: "Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) is the department of the Government of Canada" (doc.txt) "responsible for developing, managing and delivering programs and services." (doc.txt) "The Minister of Employment and Social Development is responsible for the EI act, except for PART IV - Insurable Earnings and Collection of Premiums, and PART VII - Benefit Repayment." (doc.txt) "The Canada Revenue Agency is responsible for resolving any questions pertaining to the insurability of employment or earnings (Part IV), and for the collection of EI premiums (Part VII)." (doc.txt) "Service Canada oversees all legislative provisions of the act, including the determination of benefit eligibility and entitlement, and issues EI benefits (Part I) directly to EI claimants." (doc.txt) "Provinces and territories deliver the majority of Employment Benefits and National Employment Services (Part II)." (doc.txt), delivered through "Agreements (LMDA) funded by the Government of Canada." (doc.txt) "The Canada Employment Insurance Commission (CEIC) is a departmental corporation of ESDC." (doc.txt) "It plays a key role in administering the EI program, including the making of regulations, with the approval of the Governor-in-Council" (doc.txt), and "In addition to its role in EI premium rate setting, the CEIC produces the annual EI Monitoring and Assessment Report." (doc.txt) "The Social Security Tribunal of Canada (SST) is an independent administrative body composed of a General Division and Appeal Division, that makes decisions on appeals related to the Act." (doc.txt)

Funding/insurability: "All workers employed in insurable employment are required to pay EI premiums." (doc.txt) "The fundamental requirement for an employment to be insurable is that there is an employer-employee relationship; i.e. the person is not self-employed." (doc.txt)

## The actual benefits

Part I: "As previously stated, Part I of the EI program provides temporary income support to workers who" (doc.txt) "have lost their job for reasons outside their control, while they look for new employment or upgrade" (doc.txt) "their skills. EI Part I also provides temporary financial assistance to workers who are sick," (doc.txt) as well as to those pregnant, caring for a newborn or newly adopted child, or caring for a family member with a serious medical condition. Part II: "The purpose of Part II of the EI Act is to provide employment assistance to eligible insured" (doc.txt) "participants, through various programs and services." (doc.txt)

- Regular benefits: "Regular benefits are available to individuals who lose their jobs for reasons outside their control" (doc.txt) "and who are available for and actively seeking employment." (doc.txt)
- Special benefits: "Special benefits are available to insured workers and self-employed individuals who are too sick to" (doc.txt) work, are pregnant or have recently given birth, or must be away from work to provide care or support.
  - "Maternity benefits: for biological mothers who are pregnant or have given birth." (doc.txt) They "cover the period surrounding the child's birth (up to 15 weeks)." (doc.txt)
  - "Parental benefits: for any legal parent to care for their newborn or newly adopted child or" (doc.txt) "children. One parent can receive these benefits, or they can share benefits between them (up to 40" (doc.txt) "weeks of standard parental benefits or 69 weeks of extended parental benefits when sharing)." (doc.txt) "However, one parent cannot receive more than 35 weeks of standard parental benefits or 61 weeks of" (doc.txt) the extended option.
  - "Sickness benefits: for people who cannot work due to injury, illness, or the need to be isolated in" (doc.txt) "quarantine because they may be carrying a disease (up to 26Footnote1 weeks)." (doc.txt)
  - "Compassionate Care benefits: for people who must be away from work temporarily to provide care or" (doc.txt) "support to a family member who is gravely ill with a significant risk of death within 26 weeks." (doc.txt)
  - "Family Caregiver benefits: for family members who must be away from work temporarily to care for or" (doc.txt) "support a critically ill or injured family member." (doc.txt) They "can be shared between several family members (up to 35 weeks for caring for a child; up to 15" (doc.txt) "weeks for caring for an adult)." (doc.txt)
  - "In certain circumstances various types of special benefits may be combined up to a maximum of 102" (doc.txt) "weeks of benefits within a maximum 104 week benefit period." (doc.txt)
- "Fishing benefits – are for eligible self-employed fishers who do not meet the eligibility" (doc.txt) "requirements for EI regular benefits in their region." (doc.txt)
- "Job creation partnerships, work sharing, training benefits: for clients participating in" (doc.txt) "developmental programs." (doc.txt)
