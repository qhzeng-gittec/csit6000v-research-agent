

Per doc.txt, installment loans and mortgages share core features: both are loans repaid with principal plus interest, and both are amortized, spread out into a series of fixed payments, each covering interest and principal. Mortgage amortization is similar to amortizing other loans, and installment loans can also last "a few years to perhaps twenty years or more (in the case of real estate)."

The differences are:

- **Purpose and collateral**: A mortgage is a long-term loan used for the purchase of a house, and the house itself serves as collateral — if the borrower fails to make payments, the lender can take possession of the house. An installment loan is for major purchases such as vehicles or furniture.

- **Term and conditions**: Mortgages generally tend to be for longer time periods than installment loans, and their terms often change over the course of the mortgage. For example, a twenty-year mortgage may be signed for a five-year term, after which a new agreement is required and the conditions usually change. The agreement may allow lump-sum payments or changing the payment amount.

- **Payment count**: Mortgage amortization generally involves many more payment periods.

**Choosing which**: Choose a mortgage when buying a house, since it is designed for home purchases and is secured by the house. Choose an installment loan for major purchases like vehicles or furniture when the buyer cannot pay in full, since it allows regular payments until the loan is paid off rather than full repayment on a single maturity date.
