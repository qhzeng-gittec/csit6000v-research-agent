**Similarities:** Both are loans in which "the amount that will be paid by the borrower will include the original amount plus interest" (doc.txt). Neither requires full repayment on a specific date: "the borrower is required to make regular (installment) payments until the loan is paid off." Both are amortized: "amortizing a mortgage is similar to amortizing other loans."

**Differences:** A mortgage is "a long term loan that is used for the purchase of a house," and the lender "requires that the house be used as collateral for the loan," so if the borrower is unable to make payments, "the lender can take possession of the house." Installment loans are described for "major purchases such as vehicles or furniture." Mortgages "generally tend to be for longer time periods than an installment loan," and their terms "will often change over the course of the mortgage": for example, a twenty-year mortgage may be signed for a five-year term, after which "a new agreement will be required and the conditions of the mortgage usually change." Installment loans "can vary in length from a few years to perhaps twenty years or more (in the case of real estate)."

**Choosing one over the other:** The document ties each loan to its purpose: an installment loan is the option for a qualifying consumer who cannot afford to pay the full price of a vehicle or furniture, and a mortgage is used for the purchase of a house. It states no other criteria for choosing one over the other.
