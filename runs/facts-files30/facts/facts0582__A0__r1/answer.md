## Surgical options and their pros/cons

The goal of all these procedures is to **ease pressure on the nerve**, thereby alleviating pain and other symptoms. The choice depends on the location of the herniated disc, the severity of the pain, and the disability it causes.

**1. Open discectomy**
- What it is: Open surgery to remove the herniated section of the disc.
- Pros/cons: The document gives no specific pros or cons beyond the general risks of discectomy (below).

**2. Endoscopic spine surgery**
- What it is: A long thin tube (endoscope) is used to remove the herniated section of the disc.
- Pros: Minimally invasive, requires only a tiny incision, only a small scar forms, and it results in a **quicker recovery**.
- Cons: None listed in the document.

**3. Surgery on the core of the spinal disc**
- What it is: Instruments access the core of the disc, and a vacuum removes the core, making the disc smaller and reducing pressure on the nerve.
- Pros: Reduces pressure on the nerve.
- Cons/Limitation: **Only possible if the outer layer of the disc is not damaged** — which is often not the case, since a herniated disc by definition has a torn or ruptured outer layer (annulus fibrosis).

**4. Laminotomy or laminectomy**
- What it is: Removal of part (laminotomy) or all (laminectomy) of the lamina — the part of the vertebrae that covers and protects the spinal canal — followed by a discectomy.
- Pros: Lumbar procedures help relieve leg/sciatic pain from lower-back herniations; cervical procedures help relieve neck and upper-limb pain from neck herniations.
- Cons: Often requires a **spinal fusion** afterward (see below).

**5. Spinal fusion (SF)**
- What it is: Joining two bones together with screws, typically done after a laminotomy/laminectomy to stabilize the spine.
- Pros: Stabilizes the spine.
- Cons: People who have had an SF may **experience pain and feel that the treatment restricts certain movements**.
- When needed: Typically required after lumbar laminotomies. For cervical procedures, it's required if the surgeon operates from the front of the neck, but rarely if from the back.

**6. Artificial disc surgery (ADS)**
- What it is: An alternative to spinal fusion in which the damaged disc is replaced with an artificial one.
- Pros: Usually associated with **less pain and less restricted movement** compared to spinal fusion.
- Cons: None listed, though it's only mentioned as an option for some people who undergo laminotomy.
