<answer>
Based only on doc.txt, here is what the document says about VBAC (vaginal birth after cesarean):

**Benefits of VBAC compared to a repeat C-section** [E1]:
- Faster recovery and shorter hospital stay.
- No abdominal surgery.
- Avoiding or lowering C-section risks like heavy bleeding and infection.
- Vaginal birth helps clear amniotic fluid from your baby's lungs as it passes through the birth canal, better preparing the baby to breathe oxygen after birth.
- Your baby gets an immune system boost as it travels through the birth canal.
- Less risk of complications from repeat surgeries like scarring or injuries to nearby organs.
- Personal preference — some people want the experience of a vaginal birth.

**Risks** [E2, E3, E4, E5, E6]:
- The main risk is uterine rupture: because a C-section leaves a scar on the uterus, the pressure of labor could cause the uterus to rupture along the scar. Although rare, a uterine rupture is a serious complication that can have life-threatening results, and if you're at high risk for it, your provider will typically not attempt a VBAC [E2, E3].
- Other risks of attempting a VBAC are blood loss, infection, and an emergency C-section (which is riskier than a planned C-section) [E4].
- The risk of uterine rupture depends on the type of incision from your prior C-section: a low transverse incision has the lowest risk (about 0.9%, slightly less than 1 in 100), a low vertical incision has a higher risk, and a high vertical ("classical") incision in the upper part of the uterus has the highest risk [E5, E6].

**Success rate** [E7]:
- Up to 75% of people who had one C-section went on to have a successful vaginal birth in their next pregnancy.
- The chance drops to about 50% if you've had two or more prior C-section deliveries.

**Criteria that work in favor of a successful VBAC** [E8]:
- Previous C-section deliveries (one or two) with low transverse incisions.
- A previous vaginal birth.
- No other uterine surgeries (such as fibroid removal/myomectomy).
- No history of uterine rupture.
- No medical condition (like fibroids or problems with the location of your placenta) that makes vaginal delivery risky.
- Going into labor on your own before your due date.
- A prior successful VBAC without complications (such as ruptured uterus) makes future successful vaginal deliveries more likely.

**Factors that lower the chances of success** [E9]:
- A prior C-section due to labor not progressing (not dilating or effacing).
- Being older than 35.
- Obesity or overweight.
- Giving birth to a large fetus or expecting multiples.
- Being past your due date.
- Having had a baby within the last 18 months (short time between pregnancies).
- Having preeclampsia (providers may prefer a C-section).

**Practical steps to discuss with your provider** [E10, E11]:
- Make sure your provider looks at your medical records, including the location and direction of the uterine incision and the reasons for any prior C-sections.
- Discuss: past pregnancies and deliveries; the reason(s) you had a C-section; the safety of VBAC vs. a repeat C-section for you and your baby; reasons to consider or avoid each option; a plan for inducing labor (or not); whether the healthcare facility can manage emergency deliveries; and your plan for more children.
