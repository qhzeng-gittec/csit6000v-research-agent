

# Summary of EI (based solely on `doc.txt`)

## 1. The System Itself

- **What it is:** EI is an insurance-based program designed to protect individuals who have lost their job for reasons outside their control while they look for new employment or upgrade their skills. It may also help unemployed individuals across the country find employment.
- **History:** The program was implemented in 1940 and marked its 75th year in 2015. The last major reform occurred in 1996, when changes were introduced to better reflect its primary objective of promoting employment and to emphasize that access to the program is linked to significant work attachment.
- **Roles and responsibilities:**
  - **Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC)** is the government department responsible for developing, managing, and delivering programs and services; the Minister of Employment and Social Development is responsible for the EI Act except Part IV (Insurable Earnings and Collection of Premiums) and Part VII (Benefit Repayment).
  - **Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)** resolves questions about the insurability of employment or earnings (Part IV) and collects EI premiums (Part VII).
  - **Service Canada** (a federal institution within ESDC) oversees all legislative provisions of the Act, determines benefit eligibility and entitlement, and issues EI benefits (Part I) directly to claimants, aiming to provide timely, accurate payments and services.
  - **Provinces and territories** deliver the majority of Employment Benefits and National Employment Services (Part II) through Labour Market Development Agreements (LMDA) funded by the Government of Canada, enabling them to design, deliver, and manage skills and employment programs for unemployed individuals, particularly those eligible for EI benefits.
  - **Canada Employment Insurance Commission (CEIC)**, a departmental corporation of ESDC, administers the program, makes regulations (with Governor-in-Council approval), reviews and approves policies, sets EI premium rates, and produces the annual EI Monitoring and Assessment Report.
  - **Social Security Tribunal of Canada (SST)** is an independent administrative body (General Division and Appeal Division) that makes decisions on appeals related to the Act.
- **Premiums and insurable employment:** All workers in insurable employment must pay EI premiums; whether a worker can qualify for benefits is not a criterion for premium payment, since it is the employment, not the individual, that is insured. The fundamental requirement for insurable employment is an employer–employee relationship (i.e., the person is not self-employed), and CRA decides whether an employment is insurable and whether premiums are payable.
- **Purpose:** Part I provides temporary income support to workers who lost their job for reasons outside their control, plus temporary financial assistance to workers who are sick, pregnant, caring for a newborn or newly adopted child, or caring for a family member with a serious medical condition. Part II provides employment assistance to eligible insured participants through various programs and services.

## 2. The Actual Benefits

- **Regular benefits:** For individuals who lose their jobs for reasons outside their control and who are available for and actively seeking employment.
- **Special benefits:** For insured workers and self-employed individuals who are too sick to work, pregnant or recently given birth, caring for a newborn or newly adopted child, or temporarily away from work to care for a family member with a serious medical condition. These include:
  - **Maternity benefits** – for biological mothers who are pregnant or have given birth, covering the period surrounding the child's birth (up to 15 weeks).
  - **Parental benefits** – for any legal parent to care for a newborn or newly adopted child(ren); one parent can receive them or they can be shared (up to 40 weeks of standard parental benefits, or 69 weeks of extended parental benefits when sharing; one parent cannot receive more than 35 weeks of standard or 61 weeks of extended).
  - **Sickness benefits** – for people who cannot work due to injury, illness, or quarantine isolation (up to 26 weeks).
  - **Compassionate Care benefits** – for people temporarily away from work to care for a family member gravely ill with a significant risk of death within 26 weeks; one family member can receive them or they can be shared (up to 26 weeks).
  - **Family Caregiver benefits** – for family members temporarily away from work to care for a critically ill or injured family member; one family member can receive them or they can be shared (up to 35 weeks for caring for a child; up to 15 weeks for caring for an adult).
  - In certain circumstances, various special benefits may be combined up to a maximum of 102 weeks of benefits within a maximum 104-week benefit period.
- **Fishing benefits:** For eligible self-employed fishers who do not meet the eligibility requirements for EI regular benefits in their region.
- **Job creation partnerships, work sharing, and training benefits:** For clients participating in developmental programs.
