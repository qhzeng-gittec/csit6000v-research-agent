

Based on `doc.txt`, AWIA Section 2015 amends the Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA) to revise the Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF) program, and all of its discussed subsections relate to drinking water:

- **Section 2015(a)** – Expressly authorizes DWSRF funds for projects to replace or rehabilitate aging drinking water treatment, storage, or distribution systems. Under prior EPA guidance these were already eligible if needed to protect public health, and they account for 66.1% of the estimated drinking water infrastructure need, but they had not been explicitly identified in statute before AWIA.

- **Section 2015(c)** – Revises DWSRF provisions for disadvantaged communities: it raises the share of a state's capitalization grant that may be used to subsidize loans for disadvantaged communities from 30% to 35% (while conditionally requiring at least 6% for such subsidies) and extends the amortization period for those loans from 30 to 40 years.

- **Section 2015(d)** – Extends repayment and amortization terms for all DWSRF-financed projects: the initial principal and interest payment deadline moves from one year to 18 months after project completion, and the amortization period for assisted projects can be extended from 20 to 30 years.

- **Section 2015(e)** – Requires EPA to include the cost of replacing lead service lines in the drinking water infrastructure needs survey (conducted every four years), which EPA uses to allot DWSRF appropriations among states; this could affect some states' allotments.

- **Section 2015(f)** – Removes the fiscal-year limitation on the 10% set-aside of a state's DWSRF capitalization grant, so states may use a portion of the grant to fund source water assessments or update existing ones (previously limited to FY1996–FY1997).

- **Section 2015(g)** – Requires EPA to gather information on DWSRF administration from state drinking water administrators and report to Congress on best practices to facilitate the application process and improve DWSRF financial management and sustainability.

In short, every part of Section 2015 described in the document concerns drinking water: expanding eligible DWSRF uses (aging systems, lead line replacement), easing loan terms, broadening source water assessment funding, and improving program administration.
