

The article in `doc.txt` describes tirzepatide, a diabetes and weight loss drug marketed as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity, and its potential to help people with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). The connection between the medication and sleep apnea is that tirzepatide is a "long-acting glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) receptor agonist and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist," and in two new studies it "was so effective at reducing sleep disruptions in patients with obesity and obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) that 40% to 50% no longer needed to use a continuous pressure airway positive (CPAP) device."

The link between the two conditions is obesity. The article notes that "OSA affects 1 billion people worldwide and 30 million American adults, many of whom are undiagnosed. Obesity is a common risk factor," and that "40% of those with obesity have OSA and 70% of those with OSA have obesity." Because CPAP is "an effective and the most-used intervention for OSA, but many patients refuse to use the device, stop using it, or cannot use it," a medication that treats obesity offers a different route. The article adds that "Should tirzepatide eventually gain US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for OSA, it would be the first drug approved for the condition."

The evidence comes from the SURMOUNT-OSA studies, "two double-blind randomized controlled trials in patients with obesity and moderate-to-severe OSA" conducted at 60 sites in nine countries. Patients had more than 15 events per hour on the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI) and a body mass index of 30 kg/m2 or greater. Those not using a CPAP device were enrolled in study 1, and those using one in study 2. Participants received "either the maximum tolerated dose of tirzepatide (10 or 15 mg by once-weekly injection) or placebo for 1 year," along with lifestyle counseling to reduce food intake by 500 kcal/day and engage in at least 150 min/week of physical activity.

The benefits reported include:

- A large reduction in apnea events: "By 1 year, patients taking tirzepatide had 27 to 30 fewer events/hour compared with 4 to 6 fewer events/hour for those taking placebo."
- Meaningful improvement in severity: "Up to half of those who received tirzepatide in both trials had less than 5 events/hour or 5 to 14 AHI events/hour and an Epworth Sleepiness Scale score of 10 or less," a level at which "CPAP therapy may not be recommended."
- Weight loss: "patients taking the medication lost 18% to 20% of their body weight."
- Cardiovascular and metabolic benefits: tirzepatide "also lowered C-reactive protein levels and systolic blood pressure," with a decrease in systolic blood pressure "of 9.7 mm Hg in study 1 and 7.6 mm Hg in study 2 at Week 48."
- Better sleep quality: patients "reported fewer daytime and nighttime disturbances, as measured using the Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System Short Form scale for Sleep-Related Impairment and Sleep Disturbance."

The article also reports risks: "The most common adverse events were diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting, which occurred in approximately a quarter of patients taking tirzepatide. There were two adjudicated-confirmed cases of acute pancreatitis in those taking tirzepatide in study 2."

Experts quoted in the article are largely positive. Lead author Atul Malhotra said the studies "mark a significant milestone in the treatment of OSA, offering a promising new therapeutic option that addresses both respiratory and metabolic complications." Sanjay R. Patel noted that "the integration of obesity management into the approaches to care for obstructive sleep apnea has lagged," that "as many as half of patients abandon CPAP therapy within 3 years," and that the blood-pressure improvement "was substantially larger than effects seen with CPAP therapy alone and indicate that tirzepatide may be an attractive option for those patients who seek to reduce their cardiovascular risk." He also raised concerns about high discontinuation rates of GLP-1 receptor agonists and racial disparities in their use. Louis Aronne said the trials demonstrate "the treatment of obesity with tirzepatide plus CPAP is really the optimal treatment for obstructive sleep apnea and obesity-related cardiometabolic risks," while noting it is "still not clear whether tirzepatide had an independent effect in the OSA trial" or whether the results were primarily due to weight loss, adding, "I believe that over time we'll see that this particular effect in sleep apnea is related to weight."

One caveat: the document does not address whether tirzepatide would be appropriate or effective for you personally, only what was observed in these trials of patients with obesity and moderate-to-severe OSA.
