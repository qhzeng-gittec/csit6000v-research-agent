## What the Apgar score is

The Apgar score is a "vitality index" used worldwide for almost every newborn immediately after birth. It is based on five components:

1. Skin color
2. Heart rate
3. Reflex irritability
4. Muscle tone
5. Respiration

Each item is scored from 0 to 2, giving a total score. A total of 7–10 is considered normal, with 10 representing the optimal condition.

## The study behind the article

The article describes a large population-based cohort study using Danish national registers. It identified 2,272,473 live singleton births from 1978–2015 in the Danish Medical Birth Registry; after exclusions (missing sex data, missing/invalid 5-minute Apgar scores, and deaths or emigration before age 1), the final cohort comprised 2,213,822 births. To the authors' knowledge, it was the first study to examine the association of the full spectrum of mental disorders with the 5-minute Apgar score.

## Key findings on health outcomes

**Even "normal" scores of 7–9 carry elevated risk.** Current guidelines treat Apgar scores of 7 or higher as reassuring, but the study found that individuals with scores in the clinically "normal" range of 7–9 still had higher risks of:
- Overall mental disorders in childhood
- Organic disorders
- Neurodevelopmental disorders, including intellectual disability, pervasive developmental disorders, childhood autism, and ADHD

There was a dose-response relationship: the overall risk of mental disorders increased as the Apgar score decreased from 9 toward 7. Prior studies also found that scores of 7–9 were associated with higher risks of neonatal mortality, neonatal morbidity, and adverse long-term neurological outcomes compared with a score of 10.

**Compromised (low) scores carry greater risk.** Compromised 5-minute Apgar scores were associated with:
- Intellectual disability in childhood (up to age 18), with 3–5-fold risks — corroborating a Swedish study that reported a 9-fold risk of intellectual disability in term infants with low 5-minute Apgar scores
- Childhood autism (the typical and most severe type)
- Organic disorders and neurotic disorders in childhood — a novel finding reported for the first time in this study

**No association found in early adulthood.** Compromised 5-minute Apgar scores were not found to be associated with mental disorders during early adulthood. The authors suggest this may be due to incomplete Apgar records during the early years of the Danish register, and note that follow-up (up to a maximum attained age of 39) was not long enough to detect late-onset disorders such as dementia.

## Why the association may exist

The authors note that the causes of mental disorders are multifactorial. Adverse prenatal events (e.g., gestational diabetes, preterm birth, restricted fetal growth) can have a "programming effect" on fetal brain development. While the Apgar score does not clarify any causal pathway, a less-than-optimal score at birth may be a potential sign of the cumulative effect of such adverse prenatal events. In particular, the clinically reassuring but suboptimal range of 7–9 may indicate subtle but still detrimental intrauterine insults that negatively affect fetal brain development. Regarding organic disorders specifically, the increased risk with low Apgar scores implies that adverse prenatal insults (e.g., hypoxia-ischemia, white matter injury, reduced blood flow, malnutrition) exert a long-lasting impact on brain function in later life. For neurotic disorders, the authors note that individuals with compromised Apgar scores tended to be born into families with worse socioeconomic status (e.g., mothers living alone with low education), so socioeconomic factors may at least partially mediate that association.

## Practical takeaway

The article's central message is that 5-minute Apgar scores routinely available in contemporary neonatal settings — even within the "normal" range of 7–9 — are not totally reassuring. A less-than-optimal Apgar score at birth may be an indicator for a broad scope of mental disorders in childhood, not merely neurodevelopmental disorders.
