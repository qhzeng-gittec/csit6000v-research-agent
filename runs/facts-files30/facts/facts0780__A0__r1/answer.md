**AT&T**
- In 2018, acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, as well as other cable channels (the DOJ unsuccessfully tried to block the merger).
- Announced plans to introduce a new edge provider, **HBO Max**, to stream video programming at no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers, with a subscription fee for other customers.

**Comcast**
- In 2011, became the majority owner of NBCUniversal (which owns television networks and broadcast stations), thereby obtaining a minority ownership stake in **Hulu**, an edge provider that streams video programming to subscribers. (In 2019, Disney obtained "full operational control" of Hulu, but Comcast retained its 33% financial stake.)
- Announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, **Peacock**, with three subscription options: a free ad-supported option, a premium version with more programming for a fee, and a premium no-ads version for a higher fee. The premium version is to be offered free to subscribers of Comcast and Cox Communications.

**Verizon**
- In 2015, acquired **AOL**, an ISP and edge provider.
- In 2016, acquired the core business of **Yahoo**, an edge provider.
- In 2017, combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—to create **Oath**.

In short, according to the document, ISPs have transitioned into edge providers by acquiring content/edge businesses (Time Warner, AOL, Yahoo, NBCUniversal/Hulu) and by launching their own streaming services (HBO Max, Peacock) or consolidating acquired edge products (Oath).
