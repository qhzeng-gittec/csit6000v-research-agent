According to doc.txt, changing marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would have the following effects:

1. **Medical marijuana marketing**: Because substances other than Schedule I have accepted medical uses and may be lawfully dispensed by prescription under the CSA, rescheduling marijuana to Schedule III "would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market their products consistent with federal law" [E1]. However, this would not automatically legalize medical marijuana: products would still have to comply with other federal legal requirements, such as the FD&C Act (e.g., FDA approval via an NDA or BLA), before they could be legally marketed in the United States [E1].

2. **Medical research controls**: Generally, rescheduling from Schedule I to Schedule III "loosens the controls on medical research involving that drug," and researchers and manufacturers of Schedule III substances are subject to less-onerous registration requirements than those for Schedule I substances [E2].

3. **Limitation on the research benefit**: Medical researchers and drug sponsors of marijuana- or CBD-containing drugs would not actually benefit from these looser restrictions without congressional action, because the 2022 Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research Expansion Act (P.L. 117-215) created separate requirements for marijuana researchers and manufacturers to expedite DEA registration, and those separate requirements would not be affected by rescheduling without additional congressional action [E3].

4. **Other THC compounds unaffected**: Other tetrahydrocannabinols would remain Schedule I substances, because they are scheduled separately from marijuana and are not included in the DOJ's proposed rule rescheduling marijuana [E4].
