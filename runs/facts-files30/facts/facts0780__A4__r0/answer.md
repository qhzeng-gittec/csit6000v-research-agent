According to doc.txt, ISPs have transitioned into edge providers primarily through acquisitions and by launching their own streaming/content services:

- **AT&T**: In 2018, AT&T acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, as well as other cable channels [E1]. AT&T also announced plans to introduce a new edge provider, HBO Max, to stream video programming for no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers, with other customers reportedly charged a subscription fee [E2].

- **Comcast**: In 2011, Comcast became the majority owner of NBCUniversal, which owns television networks and broadcast stations, and thereby obtained minority ownership of Hulu, an edge provider that streams video programming to subscribers [E3]. Comcast also announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, Peacock, with three subscription options: a free ad-supported option, a premium version with more programming for a fee, and a premium version with no ads for a higher fee [E4].

- **Verizon**: In 2015, Verizon acquired AOL, an ISP and edge provider, and in 2016 it acquired the core business of Yahoo, an edge provider [E5]. In 2017, it combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—to create Oath [E6].
