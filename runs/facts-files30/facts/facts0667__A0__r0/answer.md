## What is monkeypox?
Mpox (formerly known as monkeypox) is a disease caused by infection with the Monkeypox virus, which is part of the same family as the virus that causes smallpox. It is a zoonotic disease, meaning it can be spread between animals and people, and it is endemic (found regularly) in parts of Central and West Africa. The virus has been found in small rodents, monkeys, and other mammals in these areas. It is not related to chickenpox.

## Is it currently spreading?
Yes, according to the document:
- **Clade I** is responsible for the **current rise of cases in Central and Eastern Africa**.
- **Clade II** is the type that caused the **global outbreak that began in 2022**, and it is endemic to West Africa.
- Before 2022, cases outside endemic regions were rare and usually linked to travel or imported animals.

## Direct effects and how dangerous is it?
- **Direct effects:** People with mpox often get a **rash, along with other symptoms**. The rash goes through several stages, including **scabs**, before healing.
- **Danger level:** The document states that cases of mpox are **not life-threatening**, but some people are more likely to get severely ill, including:
  - People with severely weakened immune systems
  - Children younger than 1
  - People with a history of eczema
  - People who are pregnant
- **By virus type:**
  - **Clade I** historically caused higher numbers of severe illnesses, with **up to 10% of people dying** from it; recent outbreaks have seen much lower death rates of **about 1–3.3%**.
  - **Clade II** infections are generally less severe, with **more than 99.9% of people surviving**.

## Ways to prevent mpox
The document lists several ways to protect yourself and others:
1. **Getting vaccinated** — check with your healthcare provider to find out if the mpox vaccine is recommended for you.
2. **Avoiding close, skin-to-skin contact** with people who have a rash that looks like mpox.
3. **Avoiding contact with materials** a person with mpox has used, including sharing eating utensils and cups, and handling their bedding or clothing.
4. **If you do get mpox:** isolate at home, and cover lesions and wear a mask if you must be around others.
