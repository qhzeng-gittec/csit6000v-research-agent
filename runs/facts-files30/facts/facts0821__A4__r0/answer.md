<answer>
In doc.txt, AWIA Section 2015 is part of the "DWSRF Program Revisions (AWIA Sections 2002, 2015, and 2022)" and makes the most substantial revisions to the Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF) provisions of the Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA) since 1996, expanding eligible uses of DWSRF assistance, giving states more flexibility, and making DWSRF assistance more accessible to public water systems [E7]. Every subsection of Section 2015 in the document relates to drinking water:

- **Section 2015(a)**: Amends SDWA to expressly allow DWSRF funds to be used for projects that replace or rehabilitate aging drinking water treatment, storage, or distribution systems. These projects had been eligible under EPA guidance if needed to protect public health, and this category accounts for 66.1% of the estimated drinking water infrastructure need; before AWIA they were not explicitly identified in statute [E1].

- **Section 2015(c)**: Revises DWSRF provisions on financial assistance for disadvantaged communities. It raises the share of a state's capitalization grant that may be dedicated to additional subsidization from 30% to 35%, conditionally requires states to use at least 6% of the grant for these subsidies, and extends the amortization period for loans to disadvantaged communities from 30 to 40 years [E2].

- **Section 2015(d)**: Extends the repayment and amortization period for all DWSRF-financed projects: the initial principal and interest payment is due 18 months after project completion (instead of within one year), and the amortization period for DWSRF-assisted projects can be extended from 20 to 30 years [E3].

- **Section 2015(e)**: Requires EPA to evaluate and include, to the extent practicable, the cost to replace lead service lines in the drinking water infrastructure needs survey (conducted every four years), which EPA uses to allot the DWSRF appropriation among states; this may affect some states' allotments of DWSRF capitalization grants [E4].

- **Section 2015(f)**: Removes the fiscal-year limitation on source water assessment funding, authorizing states to use a portion of their DWSRF capitalization grant to fund source water assessments or update an existing one [E5].

- **Section 2015(g)**: Requires EPA to gather specified information on DWSRF administration from state drinking water administrators and to report to Congress on best practices for implementing the DWSRF, to facilitate the application process and improve DWSRF financial management and sustainability [E6].
