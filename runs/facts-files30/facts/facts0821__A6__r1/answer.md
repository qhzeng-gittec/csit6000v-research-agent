The document states that "AWIA makes the most substantial revisions to the DWSRF provisions of SDWA since the program was authorized in 1996" (doc.txt). The parts of Section 2015 are:

- **Section 2015(a)** — Expands eligible DWSRF uses: it "amends SDWA to expressly state that DWSRF funds can be used for projects to replace or rehabilitate aging treatment, storage, or distribution systems" (doc.txt). The document notes that under EPA guidance these projects had been eligible if needed to protect public health, and that "this category of projects accounts for 66.1% of the estimated drinking water infrastructure need" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(c)** — Revises financial assistance for disadvantaged communities: it "increases that proportion to 35% while conditionally requiring states to use at least 6% of their capitalization grant for these subsidies" (up from 30% before AWIA), and "amends the SDWA DWSRF provisions to extend the amortization period for loans made to disadvantaged communities from 30 to 40 years" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(d)** — Extends repayment and amortization for all DWSRF-financed projects: it "extends the date of that initial payment to 18 months after project completion" (previously one year) and "authorizes the extension of the amortization period for projects that receive DWSRF assistance from 20 to 30 years" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(e)** — Requires EPA to "evaluate and include the cost to replace lead service lines in the drinking water infrastructure needs survey, which EPA completes every four years" (doc.txt). Since EPA "uses the needs survey to allot the DWSRF appropriation among the states," this "may potentially affect some states' allotments of DWSRF capitalization grants" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(f)** — Relates to source water: it "removes this fiscal year limitation and accordingly authorizes states to use a portion of their capitalization grant to fund these source water assessments or update an existing source water assessment" (doc.txt). The prior limitation had allowed states to fund source water assessment activities from 10% of their DWSRF capitalization grant only for FY1996 and FY1997 (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(g)** — Requires EPA to "gather specified information on DWSRF administration from state drinking water administrators and report to Congress on best practices for implementing the DWSRF to facilitate the application process and to improve DWSRF financial management and sustainability" (doc.txt).

In short, every part of Section 2015 in the document concerns drinking water: (a) and (d) broaden and extend DWSRF financing for water system infrastructure, (c) increases subsidization for disadvantaged communities, (e) adds lead service line replacement costs to the needs survey, (f) removes the fiscal-year limit on funding source water assessments, and (g) directs EPA to report on DWSRF administration best practices.
