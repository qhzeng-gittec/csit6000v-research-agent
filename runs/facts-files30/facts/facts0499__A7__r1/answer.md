## What a VBAC is
"A vaginal birth after cesarean or VBAC is when you give birth through your vagina after previously having a C-section (cesarean section) delivery." (doc.txt)

## Benefits
The document lists the following benefits of VBAC compared to a C-section:
- "Faster recovery and shorter hospital stay." (doc.txt)
- "No abdominal surgery." (doc.txt)
- "Avoiding or lowering C-section risks like heavy bleeding and infection." (doc.txt)
- "Vaginal birth helps clear amniotic fluid from your baby's lungs as it passes through the birth canal. This better prepares your baby to breathe oxygen after birth." (doc.txt)
- "Your baby gets an immune system boost as it travels through the birth canal during a vaginal delivery." (doc.txt)
- "Less risk of complications from repeat surgeries like scarring or injuries to nearby organs." (doc.txt)
- "Personal preference. Some people want the experience of a vaginal birth." (doc.txt)

## Risks
- "Because a C-section leaves a scar on your uterus, the pressure of labor could cause your uterus to open (rupture) along your C-section scar." (doc.txt)
- "Although rare, a uterine rupture is a serious complication that can have life-threatening results." (doc.txt)
- "The risk of uterine rupture if you've had a C-section with a transverse cut (sideways across the lower part of the uterus) is about 0.9% or slightly less than 1 in 100." (doc.txt)
- Other risks of attempting a VBAC: "Blood loss. Infection. Emergency C-section (which is riskier than a planned C-section)." (doc.txt)
- The type of incision matters: "A low transverse incision has the lowest risk of uterine rupture," while "A low vertical incision (up and down cut) has a higher risk of rupture," and "A high vertical incision ('classical' incision) in the upper part of your uterus has the highest risk of uterine rupture." (doc.txt)

## Success rate
- "Studies show that up to 75% of people who had one C-section went on to have a successful vaginal birth in their next pregnancy. But your chance of having a successful VBAC drops to about 50% if you've had two or more prior C-section deliveries." (doc.txt)
- "If you've already had a successful VBAC — without complications such as ruptured uterus — you're more likely to have successful future vaginal deliveries." (doc.txt)

## Criteria that favor a successful VBAC
Your chances may be higher if you:
- "Have had previous C-section deliveries (one or two) with low transverse incisions." (doc.txt)
- "Have had a vaginal birth before." (doc.txt)
- "Haven't had any other uterine surgeries (such as fibroid removal, also called myomectomy)." (doc.txt)
- "Have no history of uterine rupture." (doc.txt)
- "Don't have a medical condition (like fibroids or problems with the location of your placenta) that makes a vaginal delivery risky." (doc.txt)
- "Go into labor on your own before your due date." (doc.txt)

Conversely, chances may be lower if you:
- "Had a prior C-section due to labor not progressing." (doc.txt)
- "Are older than 35." (doc.txt)
- "Have obesity or overweight." (doc.txt)
- "Are giving birth to a large fetus or expecting multiples." (doc.txt)
- "Are past your due date." (doc.txt)
- "Had a baby within the last 18 months (short time between pregnancies)." (doc.txt)
- "Have preeclampsia. (Healthcare providers may prefer a C-section delivery.)" (doc.txt)
