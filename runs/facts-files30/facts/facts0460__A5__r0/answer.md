According to doc.txt, changing marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would have the following effects:

1. **Marketing of medical marijuana products**: Rescheduling "would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market their products consistent with federal law" [E1]. However, this would not fully legalize medical marijuana: "Rescheduling thus would not automatically legalize medical marijuana in the United States" [E1]. Any such products "must still comply with other federal legal requirements, such as the FD&C Act, before they could be legally marketed in the United States" [E1]. Under the FD&C Act, new drugs "must be approved by FDA before they can be marketed and sold in the United States" [E2], and since "Marijuana itself is not an approved drug" [E3], it would still need to go through the FDA approval process (e.g., an NDA or BLA) before it could be legally marketed.

2. **Medical research controls**: "Generally, rescheduling a drug from Schedule I to Schedule III loosens the controls on medical research involving that drug," and "Researchers and manufacturers of Schedule III substances are subject to less-onerous registration requirements than researchers and manufacturers of Schedule I substances" [E4].

The 2022 Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research Expansion Act "created separate requirements for marijuana researchers and manufacturers to expedite registration with the DEA, and these separate requirements would not be affected by rescheduling without additional congressional action" [E6].

In short, per the document, rescheduling to Schedule III would allow the medical marijuana industry to market products consistent with federal law (subject to FDA approval and other federal requirements) and would generally loosen research controls, but it would not automatically legalize medical marijuana, and marijuana/CBD researchers and sponsors would not actually benefit from the looser registration requirements without further congressional action.
