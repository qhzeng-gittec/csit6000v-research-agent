**What is monkeypox?**
Mpox (formerly known as monkeypox) is a disease caused by infection with the Monkeypox virus, which is part of the same family as the virus that causes smallpox. It is a zoonotic disease (it can spread between animals and people) and is endemic in parts of Central and West Africa, where the virus has been found in small rodents, monkeys, and other mammals. It is not related to chickenpox. The virus was discovered in 1958, and the first human case was recorded in 1970.

**Direct effects and how dangerous is it?**
- People with mpox often get a rash, along with other symptoms; the rash goes through several stages, including scabs, before healing.
- There are two virus types:
  - **Clade I:** Historically caused higher numbers of severe illnesses, with up to 10% of people dying from it; recent outbreaks have seen much lower death rates of about 1–3.3%.
  - **Clade II:** Infections are generally less severe; more than 99.9% of people survive.
- Although cases are not life-threatening, some people are more likely to get severely ill: people with severely weakened immune systems, children younger than 1, people with a history of eczema, and people who are pregnant.
