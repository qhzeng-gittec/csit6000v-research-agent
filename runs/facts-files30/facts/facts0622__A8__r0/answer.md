**What the Apgar score is**

The document defines it as follows: "The Apgar score is based on five components (skin color, heart rate, reflex irritability, muscle tone, and respiration). Each item is scored from 0 to 2 with a total score of 7–10 as normal and the highest score of 10 representing the optimal condition." It is "used worldwide as a vitality index for almost every newborn immediately after birth."

**Significance to health outcomes**

The document reports findings from a large Danish population-based cohort study (2,213,822 births, 1978–2015) linking 5-minute Apgar scores to later mental health:

- **Even "normal" scores carry elevated risk:** "individuals with even clinically 'normal' Apgar score range of 7–9 still had higher risks of overall mental disorders and some specific diagnoses: organic disorders and a series of neurodevelopmental disorders (intellectual disability, pervasive developmental disorders, childhood autism, and ADHD)." The authors note that "Current guidelines recommend Apgar scores of 7 or higher to be reassuring," yet "even reassuring Apgar scores of 7–9 are associated with higher risks of neonatal mortality, neonatal morbidity, and adverse long-term neurological outcomes, compared with an Apgar score of 10." They also found "a dose-response increasing the overall risk of mental disorders with decreasing Apgar score of 9 toward 7."

- **Compromised scores:** "compromised Apgar scores were at elevated risks of developing organic disorders and neurotic disorders, which were reported for the first time." The study observed "3~5-fold risks of intellectual disability in childhood (until 18 years of age)" and found that "compromised 5-min Apgar scores were associated with childhood autism."

- **Early adulthood:** "During early adulthood, compromised 5-min Apgar scores were not found to be associated with mental disorders," which the authors say "may be attributed to incomplete records of Apgar scores during the initial establishment of the Danish Medical Birth Register (MBR)."

- **Interpretation:** The authors caution that "Apgar scores are not clear on any causal pathway of pathogenesis," but "less-than-optimal Apgar scores at birth may be a potential sign of the cumulative effect of those adverse prenatal events," and "the clinically reassuring but suboptimal score range 7–9 may indicate subtle but still detrimental intrauterine insults which will act negatively on fetal brain development." They also note that "a distressed infant will receive resuscitation well before the 5-min Apgar score is assigned, so the score 7–9 could not well reflect severe conditions prior to the assessment."
