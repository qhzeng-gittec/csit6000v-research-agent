<answer>
Based only on doc.txt, here is what it says about VBAC (vaginal birth after cesarean):

**Benefits of VBAC compared to a C-section** [E1]:
- Faster recovery and shorter hospital stay.
- No abdominal surgery.
- Avoiding or lowering C-section risks like heavy bleeding and infection.
- Vaginal birth helps clear amniotic fluid from your baby's lungs as it passes through the birth canal, better preparing your baby to breathe oxygen after birth.
- Your baby gets an immune system boost as it travels through the birth canal.
- Less risk of complications from repeat surgeries like scarring or injuries to nearby organs.
- Personal preference — some people want the experience of a vaginal birth.

**Risks** [E2, E3, E4, E5]:
- The main risk is uterine rupture: because a C-section leaves a scar on the uterus, the pressure of labor could cause the uterus to open (rupture) along the scar. Although rare, a uterine rupture is a serious complication that can have life-threatening results, and if you're at high risk for it, your provider will typically not attempt a VBAC.
- The risk of uterine rupture after a C-section with a transverse (sideways, lower-uterus) cut is about 0.9%, or slightly less than 1 in 100.
- Other risks of attempting a VBAC: blood loss, infection, and an emergency C-section, which is riskier than a planned C-section.
- The document notes that recent studies show it's possible to have a safe VBAC after a C-section in most cases.

**Success rate** [E6]:
- Up to 75% of people who had one C-section went on to have a successful vaginal birth in their next pregnancy.
- The chance drops to about 50% if you've had two or more prior C-section deliveries.

**Criteria that may make VBAC more successful** [E7, E9]:
- Previous C-section deliveries (one or two) with low transverse incisions — the low transverse incision has the lowest risk of uterine rupture.
- Having had a vaginal birth before.
- No other uterine surgeries (such as fibroid removal/myomectomy).
- No history of uterine rupture.
- No medical condition (like fibroids or problems with placenta location) that makes vaginal delivery risky.
- Going into labor on your own before your due date.
- A prior successful VBAC without complications (such as ruptured uterus) makes future successful vaginal deliveries more likely.

**Factors that may lower your chances** [E8]:
- A prior C-section due to labor not progressing (not dilating or effacing).
- Being older than 35.
- Obesity or overweight.
- Giving birth to a large fetus or expecting multiples.
- Being past your due date.
- Having had a baby within the last 18 months (short time between pregnancies).
- Having preeclampsia (providers may prefer a C-section).

The document also advises that the type and location of your uterine incision should be in your medical records, and that when discussing VBAC safety with your provider, they should look at your medical records and the reasons for any prior C-sections [E10].
