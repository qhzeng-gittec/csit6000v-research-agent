

# Sippy Cup Types (per `doc.txt`)

The document describes four types of sippy cups:

1. **Soft spout** – Closest to a bottle, with a nipple spout that still allows sucking. (You've excluded this since your daughter already has them.)
2. **Hard spout** – Encourages your child to transition from sucking to tilting and sipping. The document suggests introducing it after a child has mastered the soft spout — which fits your daughter's situation well.
3. **Straw** – Uses a straw rather than a spout. Some feel a straw is preferable for speech development, and it helps your child get used to drinking from a straw and using a cup.
4. **No spout / flat lid (360 cups)** – Spoutless with a flat top; liquid flows from all edges of the opening to mimic a real cup while still using a lid. The document notes they typically lack no-spill valves, "and that's a good thing."

The document also notes that at 6–12 months, options include spout cups, spoutless cups, and straw cups, and that handles are helpful since the cup may be too heavy for one-handed holding. It advises supervising cup use until at least age 1.

# Best Options (excluding soft spout)

Since your daughter is 11 months, the 6–12 month picks are most on-target, but I've included the 12–18 month picks too since she's close to that stage. I've highlighted how each stacks up on your two concerns — **durability** and **travel without spills**.

## 1. ZoLi BOT Straw Sippy Cup (Best straw sippy cup, 6–12 months) — $$$
- **Pros:** Weighted straw makes it easier to get the last of the liquid out no matter how the cup is oriented; dishwasher safe; BPA-free; replacement straws are available; simple to assemble with handles that are easy for babies to hold.
- **Cons:** One of the more expensive options; **not the thickest straw and can be bitten through** (a durability concern); **prone to leaks if the top isn't screwed on correctly, and can leak if the straw is damaged from biting or normal wear and tear** (a travel concern).

## 2. Munchkin Miracle 360 Trainer Cup (Best spoutless sippy cup) — $
- **Pros:** Budget-friendly; dishwasher safe (top rack); only three main pieces; comes in a variety of sizes and colors; suitable for 6 months and over.
- **Cons:** **The top's design can allow for big spills** — some parents report their babies figured out they can pour the liquid by pressing the center of the top (a travel concern); the design can be hard for some children to figure out.

## 3. First Essentials by NUK Fun Grips Hard Spout Sippy Cup (Best for toddlers, 12–18 months) — $
- **Pros:** **100% spill-proof, leak-proof, and break-proof guarantee** (the strongest durability and travel claims in the document); dishwasher safe; made in the U.S. from BPA-free plastic; simple two-part design; hourglass shape is easy to grip without handles.
- **Cons:** **The wide base won't fit in standard cup holders or diaper bag pockets** (a travel inconvenience); recommended for 12 months and older, so she's just under the stated age.

## 4. Nuby No-Spill Cup with Flex Straw (Best straw sippy cup, 12–18 months) — $
- **Pros:** Budget-friendly; **silicone straw is sturdy enough to stand up to occasional biting** (good durability); **built-in valve prevents spills and leaks** (good for travel); contoured design offers a secure grip without handles; BPA-free.
- **Cons:** The 10-ounce size might be larger than some children can easily handle; the straw's valve requires a "squeeze and suck" action that some toddlers find difficult to master (though many parents say the spill protection is worth the effort).

## Bottom line based on your concerns
- **Best for durability:** The NUK Fun Grips Hard Spout Cup is the only one with an explicit break-proof guarantee, and the Nuby's silicone straw is noted as sturdy against biting. The ZoLi's thin, bite-through straw is the weakest on durability (though replacement straws are sold).

The Nuby's built-in valve is also designed specifically to prevent spills and leaks. The Munchkin 360 and ZoLi both have documented spill/leak complaints.
