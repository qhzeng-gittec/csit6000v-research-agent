**Section 1: The EI system itself**

EI is an insurance-based program designed to protect individuals who have lost their job for reasons outside their control, while they look for new employment or upgrade their skills; it may also help unemployed individuals across the country find employment, and it provides temporary financial assistance to workers who are sick, pregnant, caring for a newborn or newly adopted child, or caring for a family member with a serious medical condition [E1]. The program was implemented in 1940 and marked its 75th year in 2015; its last major reform occurred in 1996, when changes were introduced to better reflect its primary objective of promoting employment and to emphasize that access to the program is linked to significant work attachment [E2].

The system is administered by several bodies: Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) is the Government of Canada department responsible for developing, managing and delivering programs and services [E3]; the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) resolves questions about the insurability of employment or earnings (Part IV) and collects EI premiums (Part VII) [E4]; Service Canada, a federal institution within ESDC, oversees all legislative provisions of the act, determines benefit eligibility and entitlement, and issues Part I benefits directly to claimants [E5]; provinces and territories deliver the majority of Part II Employment Benefits and National Employment Services through Labour Market Development Agreements (LMDA) funded by the Government of Canada [E6]; the Canada Employment Insurance Commission (CEIC), a departmental corporation of ESDC, administers the program, makes regulations with the approval of the Governor-in-Council, reviews and approves policies, sets EI premium rates, and produces the annual EI Monitoring and Assessment Report [E7]; and the Social Security Tribunal of Canada (SST), an independent administrative body with a General Division and an Appeal Division, makes decisions on appeals related to the Act [E8].

On funding and eligibility, all workers in insurable employment must pay EI premiums, and whether a worker can or cannot qualify for benefits is not a criterion for premium payability, since it is the employment, not the individual, that is insured [E9]. The fundamental requirement for insurable employment is an employer-employee relationship (i.e., the person is not self-employed), and CRA decides whether an employment is insurable and whether premiums are payable [E10]. In terms of purpose, Part I of the program provides temporary income support to workers who lost their job for reasons outside their control while they look for new employment or upgrade their skills, plus temporary financial assistance to workers who are sick, pregnant, caring for a newborn or newly adopted child, or caring for a family member with a serious medical condition [E11], while Part II's purpose is to provide employment assistance to eligible insured participants through various programs and services [E12].

**Section 2: The actual benefits**

- **Regular benefits** are available to individuals who lose their jobs for reasons outside their control and who are available for and actively seeking employment [E13].
- **Special benefits** are available to insured workers and self-employed individuals who are too sick to work, are pregnant or have recently given birth, are providing care to a newborn or newly adopted child, or must be away from work temporarily to provide care or support to a family member with a serious medical condition [E14]. These include:
  - **Maternity benefits** for biological mothers who are pregnant or have given birth, covering the period surrounding the child's birth (up to 15 weeks) [E15].
  - **Parental benefits** for any legal parent to care for their newborn or newly adopted child or children; one parent can receive them or they can be shared (up to 40 weeks of standard parental benefits, or 69 weeks of extended parental benefits when sharing), though one parent cannot receive more than 35 weeks of standard or 61 weeks of extended parental benefits [E16].
  - **Sickness benefits** for people who cannot work due to injury, illness, or the need to be isolated in quarantine because they may be carrying a disease (up to 26 weeks) [E17].
  - **Compassionate Care benefits** for people who must be away from work temporarily to provide care or support to a family member who is gravely ill with a significant risk of death within 26 weeks; one family member can receive them or they can be shared (up to 26 weeks) [E18].
  - **Family Caregiver benefits** for family members who must be away from work temporarily to care for or support a critically ill or injured family member; one family member can receive them or they can be shared (up to 35 weeks for caring for a child; up to 15 weeks for caring for an adult) [E19].
  - In certain circumstances, various types of special benefits may be combined up to a maximum of 102 weeks of benefits within a maximum 104-week benefit period [E20].
- **Fishing benefits** are for eligible self-employed fishers who do not meet the eligibility requirements for EI regular benefits in their region [E21].
- **Job creation partnerships, work sharing, and training benefits** are for clients participating in developmental programs [E22].
