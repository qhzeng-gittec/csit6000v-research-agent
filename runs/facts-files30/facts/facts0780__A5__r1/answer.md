According to the document, three ISPs have moved into the edge-provider space, each through acquisitions and/or launching their own streaming or content services:

- **AT&T**: In 2018, AT&T acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, as well as other cable channels (the DOJ unsuccessfully attempted to block the merger). AT&T also announced plans to introduce a new edge provider, HBO Max, to stream video programming for no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers, while other customers would reportedly be charged a subscription fee [E1].

- **Comcast**: In 2011, Comcast became the majority owner of NBCUniversal and thereby obtained minority ownership of Hulu, an edge provider that streams video programming to subscribers (in 2019, Disney obtained "full operational control" of Hulu, but Comcast retained its 33% financial stake). Comcast also announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, Peacock, with three subscription options: a free ad-supported option, a premium version with more programming for a fee, and a premium no-ads version for a higher fee, with the premium version to be offered for free to subscribers of Comcast and Cox Communications [E2].

- **Verizon**: In 2015, Verizon acquired AOL (an ISP and edge provider), and in 2016 it acquired the core business of Yahoo (an edge provider). In 2017, it combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—to create Oath [E3].

In short, the document describes ISPs transitioning into edge providers by acquiring content/edge businesses (Time Warner, AOL, Yahoo, and a stake in Hulu) and by launching their own streaming services (HBO Max, Peacock) or consolidating acquired edge products (Oath) [E1, E2, E3].
