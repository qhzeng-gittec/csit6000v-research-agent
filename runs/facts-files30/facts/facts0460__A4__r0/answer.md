<answer>
According to doc.txt, rescheduling marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would change the following:

1. **Medical marketing becomes possible in principle.** Because substances other than Schedule I have accepted medical uses and may be lawfully dispensed by prescription under the CSA, rescheduling marijuana to Schedule III "would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market their products consistent with federal law" [E1].

2. **But it would not automatically legalize medical marijuana.** Any such products "must still comply with other federal legal requirements, such as the FD&C Act, before they could be legally marketed in the United States" — for example, FDA approval via a new drug application or biologics license application [E1].

3. **Loosened controls on medical research.** Rescheduling "loosens the controls on medical research involving that drug," since researchers and manufacturers of Schedule III substances "are subject to less-onerous registration requirements than researchers and manufacturers of Schedule I substances" [E2].

4. **However, marijuana researchers would not actually get those looser requirements without congressional action.** Medical researchers and drug sponsors of marijuana- or CBD-containing drugs "would not benefit from these looser restrictions associated with rescheduling without congressional action," because the 2022 Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research Expansion Act created separate, expedited DEA registration requirements for marijuana researchers and manufacturers, and those "would not be affected by rescheduling without additional congressional action" [E3].

5. **Other THC compounds would be unaffected.** Other tetrahydrocannabinols "remain Schedule I substances" and, being scheduled separately from marijuana, "are not included in the DOJ's proposed rule rescheduling marijuana" [E4].
</answer>
