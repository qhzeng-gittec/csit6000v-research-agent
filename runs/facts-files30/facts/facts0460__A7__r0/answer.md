

Based on `doc.txt`, changing marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would have the following effects:

**1. It would open the door for the medical marijuana industry, but not automatically legalize medical marijuana.**
- "rescheduling marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market their products consistent with federal law" (doc.txt)
- However, "Any such products must still comply with other federal legal requirements, such as the FD&C Act, before they could be legally marketed in the United States" (doc.txt)
- "Rescheduling thus would not automatically legalize medical marijuana in the United States" (doc.txt)
- Products would still need FDA approval: "the drug sponsor must submit a new drug application (NDA) with 'substantial evidence' that the drug is safe and effective for its proposed use" (doc.txt), and for a biologic, "a sponsor must submit a biologics license application (BLA) demonstrating that the product is safe, potent, and pure" (doc.txt). The document notes that "Certain chemicals related to or derived from marijuana may be considered small-molecule drugs, while other components of the cannabis plant—including marijuana itself—may be considered a biologic" (doc.txt).

**2. It would generally loosen controls on medical research, but marijuana researchers would not actually benefit without further congressional action.**
- "Generally, rescheduling a drug from Schedule I to Schedule III loosens the controls on medical research involving that drug" (doc.txt)
- "Researchers and manufacturers of Schedule III substances are subject to less-onerous registration requirements than researchers and manufacturers of Schedule I substances" (doc.txt)
- But: "medical researchers and drug sponsors of marijuana or CBD containing drugs would not benefit from these looser restrictions associated with rescheduling without congressional action" (doc.txt). The 2022 Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research Expansion Act (P.L. 117-215) "created separate requirements for marijuana researchers and manufacturers to expedite registration with the DEA, and these separate requirements would not be affected by rescheduling without additional congressional action" (doc.txt).

The document does not address other potential consequences of such a rescheduling (e.g., effects on recreational use, taxation, or state law).
