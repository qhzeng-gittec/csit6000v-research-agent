**Types of sippy cups (excluding soft spout, which you've already used):**

- **Hard spout** — "Hard-spout sippy cups encourage your child to transition from sucking to tilting and sipping. It's often best to introduce it after they've mastered the soft spout." [E1]
- **Straw** — "Straw sippy cups, as you may have guessed, employ a straw rather than a spout. Some feel that a straw is preferable for speech development over a spout. They can also help your child get used to drinking from a straw and using a cup." [E2]
- **No spout or flat lid (360 cup)** — "These sippy cups are spoutless with a flat top (sometimes referred to as 360 cups). They allow for water to flow from all edges of the cup opening to resemble the action of a real cup while still using a lid. They typically lack any no-spill valves, and that's a good thing." [E3]

The document notes that for the 6–12 month range, "the options get more varied and include: spout cups, spoutless cups, straw cups," and that "The key to success is choosing cups that are appropriate for your child's age and stage of development." [E4, E5]

**Best options (non-soft-spout), with pros and cons:**

1. **ZoLi BOT Straw Sippy Cup** (straw; $$$; 9 months+) [E6]
   - Pros: "Weighted straw makes it easier to get the last of the liquid out; dishwasher safe."
   - Cons: "One of the more expensive cup options; not the thickest of straws and can be bitten through."
   - Durability/travel: "it can also be difficult to screw the top on correctly, making it prone to leaks. The cup can also leak if the straw becomes damaged from biting or normal wear and tear." (Replacement straws are available.)

2. **Munchkin Miracle 360 Trainer Cup** (spoutless/360; $; 6 months+) [E7]
   - Pros: "Budget-friendly option; dishwasher safe; comes in a variety of sizes and colors."
   - Cons: "The top's design can allow for big spills; the design can be hard for some children to figure out how to drink from."
   - Durability/travel: "Some parents complain that, while the cup is spill-proof, their smart babies figured out they can pour the liquid by simply pressing on the center of the top."

3. **First Essentials by NUK Fun Grips Hard Spout Sippy Cup** (hard spout; $; 12 months+) [E8]
   - Pros: "Made in the United States; dishwasher safe; hourglass shape is easier to hold without needing handles."
   - Cons: "The cup's wide base won't fit in standard cup holders."
   - Durability/travel: "This cup features a 100 percent spill-proof, leak-proof, break-proof guarantee." However, "some reviewers say the cup's base is too wide and that it doesn't fit easily into standard cup holders or diaper bag pockets."

4. **Nuby No-Spill Cup with Flex Straw** (straw; $; listed under 12–18 months) [E9]
   - Pros: "Budget-friendly option; contoured design offers secure grip without handles; thicker straw."
   - Cons: "10-ounce size might be larger than some children can easily handle; valve in the straw requires a 'squeeze and suck' action."
   - Durability/travel: "The silicone straw has a built-in valve to prevent spills and leaks, and it's sturdy enough to stand up to occasional biting."

**How this maps to your concerns:**
- **Durability:** The NUK Fun Grips is the only one with an explicit "break-proof" guarantee [E8]. The Nuby's straw is "sturdy enough to stand up to occasional biting" [E9], while the ZoLi's straw "can be bitten through" [E6].

**Age note:** The document lists the NUK Fun Grips and the Nuby under the "12 to 18 months old" section, with the NUK described as "easy for toddlers ages 12 months and older to grab" [E8, E9].
