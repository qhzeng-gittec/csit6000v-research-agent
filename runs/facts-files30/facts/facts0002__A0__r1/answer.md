

Based on `doc.txt` ("Money Management Tips: 55 Ways to Save Money"), here are some tips on saving money, grouped by category:

**Recreation and Entertainment**
- Instead of paying for a fitness club membership, buy some weights or go to the ARC.
- Don't smoke — cigarettes are expensive, the money adds up quickly, and you'll be fined if you smoke near school facilities.
- Wait until after half-time at sport events and get in for free.
- When eating out, look for coupons or special deals, and order water instead of overpriced drinks.
- Grab coupon books that local businesses give out at the beginning of the semester.
- Take advantage of hundreds of free campus activities — join clubs, attend student concerts, or go to church-sponsored events (often with food).
- Attend "Illinites" student activities at the Illini Union every Friday night for free.
- Attend shows at Krannert — student tickets are $10 or less, sometimes free.
- If you're throwing a party, have guests pay a little or bring things to offset your cost.
- Don't buy a book unless you really want to keep it — you can check books out free at libraries.
- Rent movies with a group of friends, or go to second-run theaters for $1–$2 a ticket.
- Bring your student ID to movies — most theaters give student discounts.

**Food and Basic Needs**
- Be a savvy consumer: research product quality (e.g., through Consumer Reports) before major purchases.
- Sometimes the cheaper product works just as well as the expensive one.
- Ask for generic medications at the pharmacy.
- Get your hair done at a cosmetology school instead of a salon.
- Buying in bulk is usually good, but shop by per-unit price — the biggest option isn't always the best value.
- Scout garage/yard sales for housewares and furniture; the YMCA holds a "dump and run" at the start of each semester.
- Make things for gifts — cheaper, and the time invested shows you care.
- Buy holiday and birthday gifts throughout the year to take advantage of sales.
- Get a job at a place where you already spend a lot of money to get employee discounts.
- Use mail-in rebates or coupons for groceries and health/beauty items.
- Don't buy bottled water — buy a water filtration pitcher.
- Don't buy something just because it's on sale — consider whether you really need it.
- Apply for your favorite store's discount card if they have one.
- Make home-cooked meals — often cheaper than a fast food binge.
- Pack a lunch instead of eating out.

**Clothing**
- Buy clothes at the end of the season when they're on sale.
- Take clothes you no longer wear to a consignment shop or sell them online.
- Share dresses and tuxes with friends for special occasions.
- If you buy more than one of something (e.g., 2–3 shirts), always ask for a discount.
- Invest in durable clothes and shoes rather than many cheap pairs.

**Budgeting / Spending Plan**
- Set goals for your spending and saving.
- Keep track of your spending to avoid overspending (there are apps for that).
- Don't use a credit card if it leads you to make more purchases — on average, people with credit cards spend 34% more.
- Before going out to spend, set a limit for yourself and stick to it.
- Wait at least two hours before making a big purchase to be sure it's something you really need.

**Transportation**
- Obey traffic laws — speeding tickets cost more than the ticket itself by raising insurance premiums.
- Keep your tires inflated properly for better gas mileage.
- Get good grades — insurance companies offer lower rates to students with a 3.0+ GPA.
- Carpool with friends.
- Search for dependable cars that offer good gas mileage.
- Drive an older car — insurance payments and taxes will be less.
- Walk, bike, or ride to school to save on gas.
- Look around for the cheapest gas price before filling up (there are apps for that).

**Savings**
- Only use ATMs of your own bank — other banks' ATM fees add up.
- Always put part of your paycheck into a savings account.
- Spare change adds up — use a piggy bank or change jar.
- Volunteer — if you're busy, you can't spend money (and it boosts your resume).
- Use plastic grocery bags as trash can liners.

**Conserving Resources**
- Turn off the water while brushing your teeth.
- Unplug electronics when not in use — even turned off, they still use costly energy.
- Use items like shampoo, toothpaste, and paper towels sparingly — enough to do the job without waste.
- Pay your bills online to save paper and money on stamps.
- Ask your landlord to seal gaps between doors and windows to prevent heat leaks over winter.
