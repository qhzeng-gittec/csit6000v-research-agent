

Based on `doc.txt`, the following would change (and not change) if marijuana were rescheduled from Schedule I to Schedule III:

**Medical marijuana marketing**
- "rescheduling marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market their products consistent with federal law" (doc.txt).
- However, "Any such products must still comply with other federal legal requirements, such as the FD&C Act, before they could be legally marketed in the United States" (doc.txt), and "Rescheduling thus would not automatically legalize medical marijuana in the United States" (doc.txt).
- FDA approval would still be required: a sponsor must submit a new drug application (NDA) with "substantial evidence" that the drug is safe and effective, or a biologics license application (BLA) demonstrating the product is safe, potent, and pure (doc.txt).

**Medical research**
- "Generally, rescheduling a drug from Schedule I to Schedule III loosens the controls on medical research involving that drug. Researchers and manufacturers of Schedule III substances are subject to less-onerous registration requirements than researchers and manufacturers of Schedule I substances" (doc.txt).
- But this benefit would not apply to marijuana researchers: "medical researchers and drug sponsors of marijuana or CBD containing drugs would not benefit from these looser restrictions associated with rescheduling without congressional action" (doc.txt). The 2022 Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research Expansion Act (P.L. 117-215) created separate requirements "to expedite registration with the DEA, and these separate requirements would not be affected by rescheduling without additional congressional action" (doc.txt).

The document does not address other potential effects of rescheduling (e.g., on criminal penalties, state laws, or non-medical use).
