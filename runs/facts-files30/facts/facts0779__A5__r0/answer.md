<answer>
Similarities: Both are repaid through regular installment payments rather than a single lump sum on a maturity date [E1]. Each payment includes an interest charge, and both are amortized — "the process of spreading out a loan into a series of fixed payments" — with a portion of each payment applied to interest and a portion to principal [E1, E2]. The document notes "amortizing a mortgage is similar to amortizing other loans" [E5].

Differences: A mortgage is "a long term loan that is used for the purchase of a house," with the house as collateral: "if the mortgage holder is unable to make the payments the lender can take possession of the house" [E3]. Mortgages "generally tend to be for longer time periods than an installment loan," and "the terms of the mortgage will often change over the course of the mortgage" — for example, a twenty-year mortgage might be signed for a five-year term, after which a new agreement is required and conditions usually change [E4]. Mortgage amortization "generally involves many more payment periods," while installment loans "can vary in length from a few years to perhaps twenty years or more (in the case of real estate)" [E5, E1].

When to choose which: Installment loans are for "major purchases such as vehicles or furniture" when "the average consumer cannot afford to pay $25000 or more for a new vehicle" and may not want to wait three or four years to save [E6]. A mortgage is the loan used for the purchase of a house [E3]. The document gives no further guidance on choosing between them.
