All of the provisions of AWIA Section 2015 in `doc.txt` concern drinking water, specifically revisions to the Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF) program under the Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA). The document describes the following parts:

- **Section 2015(a)** — Expands eligible DWSRF uses: "AWIA Section 2015(a) amends SDWA to expressly state that DWSRF funds can be used for projects to replace or rehabilitate aging treatment, storage, or distribution systems" (doc.txt). The document notes that "this category of projects accounts for 66.1% of the estimated drinking water infrastructure need" and that "Prior to AWIA, these activities were not previously explicitly identified in statute" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(c)** — Increases financial assistance for disadvantaged communities: it "increases that proportion to 35% while conditionally requiring states to use at least 6% of their capitalization grant for these subsidies" (up from 30% before AWIA), and "extend[s] the amortization period for loans made to disadvantaged communities from 30 to 40 years" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(d)** — Extends repayment terms for all DWSRF-financed projects: it "extends the date of that initial payment to 18 months after project completion" (previously one year) and "authorizes the extension of the amortization period for projects that receive DWSRF assistance from 20 to 30 years" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(e)** — Requires EPA to "evaluate and include the cost to replace lead service lines in the drinking water infrastructure needs survey, which EPA completes every four years," which "may potentially affect some states' allotments of DWSRF capitalization grants" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(f)** — Concerns source water assessment: it "removes this fiscal year limitation and accordingly authorizes states to use a portion of their capitalization grant to fund these source water assessments or update an existing source water assessment" (doc.txt).

- **Section 2015(g)** — Requires EPA to "gather specified information on DWSRF administration from state drinking water administrators and report to Congress on best practices for implementing the DWSRF to facilitate the application process and to improve DWSRF financial management and sustainability" (doc.txt).

In short, every part of Section 2015 described in the document relates to drinking water: 2015(a), (c), and (d) expand and ease DWSRF financing for water system infrastructure and disadvantaged communities; 2015(e) adds lead service line replacement costs to EPA's needs survey; 2015(f) funds source water assessments; and 2015(g) directs reporting on DWSRF administration.
