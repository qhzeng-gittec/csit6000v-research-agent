**1. It would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market products under federal law — but not automatically legalize medical marijuana.**
- "rescheduling marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market their products consistent with federal law." (doc.txt)
- However, "Any such products must still comply with other federal legal requirements, such as the FD&C Act, before they could be legally marketed in the United States." (doc.txt)
- "Rescheduling thus would not automatically legalize medical marijuana in the United States." (doc.txt)
- Under the FD&C Act, new drugs (including small-molecule drugs and biologics) must be approved by FDA before they can be marketed and sold, requiring a new drug application (NDA) with "substantial evidence" of safety and effectiveness, or a biologics license application (BLA) demonstrating the product is "safe, potent, and pure." (doc.txt)

**2. It would generally loosen controls on medical research — but marijuana researchers would not actually benefit without further congressional action.**
- "Generally, rescheduling a drug from Schedule I to Schedule III loosens the controls on medical research involving that drug." (doc.txt)
- "Researchers and manufacturers of Schedule III substances are subject to less-onerous registration requirements than researchers and manufacturers of Schedule I substances." (doc.txt)
- However, "medical researchers and drug sponsors of marijuana or CBD containing drugs would not benefit from these looser restrictions associated with rescheduling without congressional action." (doc.txt)
- This is because the 2022 Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research Expansion Act (P.L. 117-215) "created separate requirements for marijuana researchers and manufacturers to expedite registration with the DEA, and these separate requirements would not be affected by rescheduling without additional congressional action." (doc.txt)

**3. Related substances would not be affected.**
- "Other tetrahydrocannabinols remain Schedule I substances, and, because they are scheduled separately from marijuana, they are not included in the DOJ's proposed rule rescheduling marijuana." (doc.txt)

In short, per the document, the main practical change would be that the medical marijuana industry could market products consistent with federal law (subject to FD&C Act requirements), while the expected loosening of research controls would not apply to marijuana researchers absent additional congressional action.
