<answer>
According to doc.txt, chewing gum does not inherently cause tooth decay; the effect depends on the type of gum:

- **Chewing gum in general** stimulates salivary flow, which helps dilute and neutralize acids produced by plaque bacteria that can otherwise damage enamel and lead to decay [E1]. Stimulated saliva flow has several mechanisms that may protect against dental caries [E2].

- **Sugar-containing chewing gum** can contribute to decay: its fermentable carbohydrates (monosaccharides and disaccharides) are metabolized by oral bacteria, which produce dental biofilm and acid that can lead to enamel demineralization and caries [E3]. The degree of cariogenicity depends on the gum's physical consistency, oral retention time, chewing frequency, and the sequence of consumption [E4].

- **Sugar-free chewing gum** does not cause decay: its sweeteners are noncariogenic because they are metabolized slowly or not at all by cariogenic plaque bacteria [E6], and clinical trials found decreased caries incidence in subjects who chewed sugar-free gum for 20 minutes after meals [E5].
