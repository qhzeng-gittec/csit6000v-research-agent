## What the Apgar score is

- "The Apgar score is based on five components (skin color, heart rate, reflex irritability, muscle tone, and respiration). Each item is scored from 0 to 2 with a total score of 7–10 as normal and the highest score of 10 representing the optimal condition." (doc.txt)
- "The Apgar score has been used worldwide as a vitality index for almost every newborn immediately after birth." (doc.txt)
- The article focuses specifically on the **5-min Apgar score**, which is "routinely available in contemporary neonatal settings." (doc.txt)

## The study behind the findings

The article describes "a population-based cohort study using data from Danish national registers," with "2,213,822 births" identified from 1978–2015 in the Danish Medical Birth Registry. (doc.txt)

## Significance for health outcomes

**Childhood mental and neurodevelopmental disorders:**
- "we found individuals with even clinically 'normal' Apgar score range of 7–9 still had higher risks of overall mental disorders and some specific diagnoses: organic disorders and a series of neurodevelopmental disorders (intellectual disability, pervasive developmental disorders, childhood autism, and ADHD)." (doc.txt)
- "compromised Apgar scores were at elevated risks of developing organic disorders and neurotic disorders, which were reported for the first time." (doc.txt)
- The authors "observed 3~5-fold risks of intellectual disability in childhood (until 18 years of age)." (doc.txt)
- There was a gradient: "We found a dose-response increasing the overall risk of mental disorders with decreasing Apgar score of 9 toward 7." (doc.txt)

**Early adulthood:**
- "During early adulthood, compromised 5-min Apgar scores were not found to be associated with mental disorders." (doc.txt) The authors note this "may be attributed to incomplete records of Apgar scores during the initial establishment of the Danish Medical Birth Register," and that "the maximum attained age in our study was only up to 39 years," so "the follow-up between 19 and 39 years was not long enough to detect some late-onset mental disorders (e.g., dementia)." (doc.txt)

**Comparison with guidelines:**
- "Current guidelines recommend Apgar scores of 7 or higher to be reassuring," but "recent studies showed that even reassuring Apgar scores of 7–9 are associated with higher risks of neonatal mortality, neonatal morbidity, and adverse long-term neurological outcomes, compared with an Apgar score of 10." (doc.txt)
- The authors conclude that "5-min Apgar scores routinely available in contemporary neonatal settings, even within the normal range 7–9, are not totally reassuring." (doc.txt)

## Possible explanations (as discussed in the article)

- "less-than-optimal Apgar scores at birth may be a potential sign of the cumulative effect of those adverse prenatal events," such as "gestational diabetes mellitus, preterm, and restricted fetal growth." (doc.txt)
- "the clinically reassuring but suboptimal score range 7–9 may indicate subtle but still detrimental intrauterine insults which will act negatively on fetal brain development." (doc.txt)
- "Increased risk of organic disorder with low Apgar score implies adverse prenatal insults (e.g., hypoxia-ischemia, white matter injury, reduced blood flow, malnutrition) exert a long-lasting impact on brain function in later life." (doc.txt)
- For neurotic disorders, "SES factors at least partially mediate the observed association between compromised Apgar scores and neurotic disorder," since "individuals with compromised Apgar scores tended to be born in families with worse SES (e.g., mothers live alone and have a low education level)." (doc.txt)

## Important caveats stated in the article

- "Although Apgar scores are not clear on any causal pathway of pathogenesis, less-than-optimal Apgar scores at birth may be a potential sign of the cumulative effect of those adverse prenatal events." (doc.txt)
- "In clinical settings, a distressed infant will receive resuscitation well before the 5-min Apgar score is assigned, so the score 7–9 could not well reflect severe conditions prior to the assessment." (doc.txt)

In short, according to this article, the Apgar score is a 0–10 newborn vitality index, and while scores of 7 or higher are considered reassuring by current guidelines, the study found that even "normal" scores of 7–9 (and especially lower scores) are associated with higher risks of childhood mental and neurodevelopmental disorders, with no clear association found in early adulthood.
