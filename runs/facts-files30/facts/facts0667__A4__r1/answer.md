<answer>
What monkeypox is: Monkeypox, now officially called mpox, is a disease caused by infection with the Monkeypox virus, which is in the same virus family as the smallpox virus, and it is not related to chickenpox [E1]. It is a zoonotic disease, meaning it can spread between animals and people, and it is endemic (found regularly) in parts of Central and West Africa, where the virus has been found in small rodents, monkeys, and other mammals [E2].

Is it currently spreading: According to the document, mpox spread around the world in 2022, whereas before that cases outside endemic regions were rare and usually linked to travel or imported animals [E3]. The document also states that clade I of the virus "is responsible for the current rise of cases in Central and Eastern Africa" [E5]. Both virus types spread through close contact (including intimate contact) with a person with mpox, direct contact with contaminated materials, and direct contact with infected animals [E4].

Direct effects: People with mpox often get a rash along with other symptoms, and the rash goes through several stages, including scabs, before healing [E1].

How dangerous it is: The document says cases of mpox are not life-threatening, but some people are more likely to get severely ill, including people with severely weakened immune systems, children younger than 1, people with a history of eczema, and people who are pregnant [E7]. Clade I historically caused higher numbers of severe illnesses, with up to 10% of people dying from it, though recent outbreaks have seen much lower death rates of about 1–3.3% [E5]. Clade II, which caused the global outbreak that began in 2022, is generally less severe, with more than 99.9% of people surviving [E6].

Ways to prevent it: The document lists several ways to protect yourself and others: getting vaccinated (check with your healthcare provider whether the mpox vaccine is recommended for you); avoiding close, skin-to-skin contact with people who have a rash that looks like mpox; avoiding contact with materials a person with mpox has used, including sharing eating utensils and cups and handling their bedding or clothing; if you do get mpox, isolating at home and covering lesions and wearing a mask if you must be around others; and, if you are in an area of Western or Central Africa where mpox occurs regularly, avoiding contact with live or dead wild animals [E8].
