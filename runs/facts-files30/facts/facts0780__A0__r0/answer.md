

According to `doc.txt`, the section "Examples of ISPs Becoming Edge Providers" describes the following actions taken by three ISPs:

**AT&T**
- In 2018, acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, along with other cable channels (the DOJ unsuccessfully attempted to block the merger).
- Announced plans to introduce a new edge provider—HBO Max—to stream video programming at no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers, while other customers would reportedly pay a subscription fee.

**Comcast**
- In 2011, became the majority owner of NBCUniversal (which owns television networks and broadcast stations), thereby obtaining a minority ownership stake in Hulu, an edge provider that streams video programming. In 2019, Disney obtained "full operational control" of Hulu, but Comcast retained its 33% financial stake.
- Announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, Peacock, with three subscription options: a free ad-supported option, a paid premium version with more programming, and a higher-priced ad-free premium version. The premium version is to be offered free to subscribers of Comcast and Cox Communications.

**Verizon**
- In 2015, acquired AOL, an ISP and edge provider.
- In 2016, acquired the core business of Yahoo, an edge provider.
- In 2017, combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—to create Oath.

In short, the document shows ISPs transitioning into edge providers primarily through **acquisitions of content/edge businesses** (AT&T–Time Warner/HBO, Comcast–NBCUniversal/Hulu, Verizon–AOL/Yahoo) and by **launching their own streaming/content services** (HBO Max and Peacock).
