

Based on `doc.txt`, rescheduling marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would have the following effects:

**1. It would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market products**
- "rescheduling marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III would open the door for the medical marijuana industry to market their products consistent with federal law" (doc.txt)
- However, "Any such products must still comply with other federal legal requirements, such as the FD&C Act, before they could be legally marketed in the United States" (doc.txt)

**2. It would not automatically legalize medical marijuana**
- "Rescheduling thus would not automatically legalize medical marijuana in the United States" (doc.txt)
- FDA approval would still be required: "the drug sponsor must submit a new drug application (NDA) with 'substantial evidence' that the drug is safe and effective for its proposed use" (doc.txt), and for a biologic, "a sponsor must submit a biologics license application (BLA) demonstrating that the product is safe, potent, and pure" (doc.txt). The document notes that "marijuana itself—may be considered a biologic" (doc.txt).

**3. It would loosen controls on medical research, but with a caveat**
- "Generally, rescheduling a drug from Schedule I to Schedule III loosens the controls on medical research involving that drug. Researchers and manufacturers of Schedule III substances are subject to less-onerous registration requirements than researchers and manufacturers of Schedule I substances" (doc.txt)
- However, "medical researchers and drug sponsors of marijuana or CBD containing drugs would not benefit from these looser restrictions associated with rescheduling without congressional action" (doc.txt). The 2022 Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research Expansion Act (P.L. 117-215) "created separate requirements for marijuana researchers and manufacturers to expedite registration with the DEA, and these separate requirements would not be affected by rescheduling without additional congressional action" (doc.txt)

The document does not address other potential changes (e.g., to criminal penalties, state laws, or non-medical use) beyond these points.
