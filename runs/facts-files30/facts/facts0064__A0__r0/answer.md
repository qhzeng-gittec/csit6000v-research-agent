**When the offending party is a minor (underage DUI):**
The criteria change to a **zero-tolerance policy** for drivers under 21. Under this policy, **any detectable amount of alcohol** can result in a DUI charge — meaning a minor does not need to reach a specific blood-alcohol level to be charged. The document also notes that underage DUIs carry stricter penalties and potential long-term consequences on educational and employment opportunities.

**Felony DUI vs. misdemeanor DUI:**
- **Misdemeanor:** A first-offense DUI in California is a misdemeanor (typically punished by a $390+ fine, 6–16 months license suspension, up to 6 months jail, and 3 months of alcohol treatment).
- **Felony:** The document does not give a single bright-line threshold, but states that the enhanced felony penalties are "direct outcomes of either **prior DUI convictions, inflicting bodily harm, or other aggravating factors**." Felony DUI penalties can include fines of $1,015–$5,000 plus restitution, license suspension up to 5 years, jail time of 16 months to 16 years, and 18 or 30 months of alcohol treatment.

In short, per the document, a DUI escalates from a misdemeanor to a felony when it involves prior DUI convictions, bodily harm, or other aggravating factors, while a minor (under 21) faces a zero-tolerance standard where any detectable alcohol can trigger a DUI charge.
