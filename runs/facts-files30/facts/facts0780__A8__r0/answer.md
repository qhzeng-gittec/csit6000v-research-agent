According to `doc.txt`, the section "Examples of ISPs Becoming Edge Providers" describes the following actions:

**AT&T**
- "In 2018, AT&T acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, as well as other cable channels."
- "AT&T has announced plans to introduce a new edge provider—HBO Max—to stream video programming for no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers; other customers will reportedly be charged a subscription fee."

**Comcast**
- "In 2011, Comcast became the majority owner of NBCUniversal, which owns television networks and broadcast stations, and thus obtained minority ownership of Hulu, an edge provider that streams video programming to subscribers."
- "Comcast also announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, Peacock," with three planned subscription options: "a free option supported by ads, a premium version with more programming for a fee, and the premium version with no ads for a higher fee," with "The premium version is to be offered for free to subscribers of Comcast and Cox Communications."

**Verizon**
- "In 2015, Verizon acquired AOL, an ISP and edge provider, and in 2016, it acquired the core business of Yahoo, an edge provider."
- "It combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—in 2017 to create Oath."

In short, the document presents three approaches: acquiring existing content/edge businesses (AT&T's acquisition of Time Warner; Comcast's majority ownership of NBCUniversal; Verizon's acquisitions of AOL and Yahoo), and launching or combining their own edge products (HBO Max, Peacock, and Oath).
