<answer>
According to doc.txt, ISPs have transitioned into edge providers mainly through acquisitions and by launching their own streaming/content services:

- **AT&T**: In 2018, AT&T (a Tier 1 ISP) acquired Time Warner, a content creator that owns HBO and its affiliated edge provider HBO NOW, as well as other cable channels, after the DOJ unsuccessfully attempted to block the merger [E1]. AT&T then announced plans to introduce a new edge provider, HBO Max, to stream video programming for no extra charge to AT&T customers who are also HBO subscribers, while other customers would reportedly be charged a subscription fee [E1].

- **Comcast**: In 2011, Comcast became the majority owner of NBCUniversal (which owns television networks and broadcast stations) and thereby obtained minority ownership of Hulu, an edge provider that streams video programming to subscribers [E2]. In 2019, Walt Disney obtained "full operational control" of Hulu, but Comcast retained its 33% financial stake [E2]. Comcast also announced plans to launch its own video streaming service, Peacock, with three subscription options: a free ad-supported option, a premium version with more programming for a fee, and a premium no-ads version for a higher fee, with the premium version to be offered for free to subscribers of Comcast and Cox Communications [E2].

- **Verizon**: In 2015, Verizon (a Tier 1 ISP) acquired AOL, an ISP and edge provider, and in 2016 it acquired the core business of Yahoo, an edge provider [E3]. In 2017, Verizon combined the edge provider products from these acquisitions—such as Yahoo Finance, Huffington Post, TechCrunch, and Engadget—to create Oath [E3].

The document also notes that edge providers associated with Tier 1 ISPs may have additional competitive advantages through the ISPs' ability to send content to any part of the internet for free, whereas edge providers associated with other ISPs may have to pay or barter with Tier 1 or other ISPs to access certain destinations [E4].
