

Based on `doc.txt`, AWIA Section 2015 makes the most substantial revisions to the Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF) provisions of the Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA) since 1996. Its water-related parts are:

- **Section 2015(a)** — Amends SDWA to expressly state that DWSRF funds can be used for projects to replace or rehabilitate aging drinking water treatment, storage, or distribution systems. These projects had been eligible under EPA guidance if needed to protect public health (they account for 66.1% of the estimated drinking water infrastructure need), but were not previously explicitly identified in statute.

- **Section 2015(c)** — Revises DWSRF financial assistance for disadvantaged communities: it increases the portion of a state's capitalization grant that may be dedicated to additional subsidization from 30% to 35% (while conditionally requiring at least 6% be used for these subsidies) and extends the amortization period for loans to disadvantaged communities from 30 to 40 years.

- **Section 2015(d)** — Extends repayment and amortization periods for all DWSRF-financed projects: the initial principal and interest payment deadline moves from one year to 18 months after project completion, and the amortization period may be extended from 20 to 30 years.

- **Section 2015(e)** — Requires EPA to evaluate and include the cost to replace lead service lines in the drinking water infrastructure needs survey (conducted every four years), which EPA uses to allot DWSRF appropriations among states; this may affect some states' allotments.

- **Section 2015(f)** — Removes the fiscal year limitation on source water assessments, authorizing states to use a portion of their DWSRF capitalization grant to fund source water assessments or update existing ones (previously limited to FY1996–FY1997).

- **Section 2015(g)** — Requires EPA to gather specified information on DWSRF administration from state drinking water administrators and report to Congress on best practices for implementing the DWSRF to facilitate the application process and improve DWSRF financial management and sustainability.

Overall, these provisions expand eligible uses of DWSRF assistance, give states more administrative flexibility, and make DWSRF assistance more accessible to public water systems.
