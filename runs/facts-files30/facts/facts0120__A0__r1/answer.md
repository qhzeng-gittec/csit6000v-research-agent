**1. In vitro inactivation of coronavirus (Clark et al., 1998)**
- Theaflavins extracted from black tea were shown to neutralize bovine coronavirus (as well as rotavirus) infections.
- Crude black tea extract and various theaflavin fractions were tested individually and in combination. The combination of theaflavin fractions (TF1 + TF2a + TF2b + TF3) was more active than the sum of the individual fractions, indicating synergism among the theaflavin components.
- Theaflavin and theaflavin gallate derivatives showed in vitro inactivation activity against both rotavirus and coronavirus, and the crude black tea extract was also able to neutralize the coronavirus.

**2. Virtual screening against SARS-CoV-2 (Lung et al., 2020, Journal of Medical Virology)**
- In the context of the COVID-19 pandemic, 83 compounds used in the Chinese medicine system were screened for potential efficacy against SARS-CoV-2 by assessing their binding to the virus's RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp), an enzyme important for viral RNA replication.
- Three-dimensional models of the RdRp of SARS-CoV-2 (2019), SARS-CoV (2002), and MERS-CoV (2012) were generated, and the compounds were virtually screened.
- Theaflavin emerged as the best compound based on its idock score (predicted binding affinity), hydrophobic interactions, and additional hydrogen bonds with amino acids near the active site of RdRp, confirmed by lower binding energy when docked into the catalytic pocket of SARS-CoV-2 RdRp.
- The authors suggested theaflavins could serve as a lead compound for developing a SARS-CoV-2 inhibitor targeting RdRp. Theaflavins are present in black tea, with the highest theaflavin contents found in Assam black teas. The authors note that further in vivo, animal, and clinical trials are required.

**3. Inhibition of SARS-CoV 3C-like protease (Chen et al., 2005, Evidence Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine)**
- A study from Taiwan demonstrated that Theaflavin-3,3′-digallate (TF3) inhibits the 3C-like (chymotrypsin-like, 3CLPro) protease activity of SARS-CoV (2002 epidemic).
- Puer and black tea extracts were more potent than green or oolong tea extracts in inhibiting 3CLPro.
- A docking approach screened a natural product library of 720 compounds; tannic acid and TF2b (Theaflavin-3-gallate) were found active against 3CL protease.
- Water extracts of TF2b, TF3, and tannic acid were the most effective 3CLPro inhibitors, with inhibitory concentrations (IC50) of less than 10 μM.

**Overall:** The document presents black tea's theaflavins (and related compounds like tannic acid) as promising antiviral agents against coronaviruses — with direct in vitro inactivity/neutralization of coronavirus (Clark et al., 1998), computational evidence that theaflavin binds well to SARS-CoV-2 RdRp (Lung et al., 2020), and experimental evidence that black tea theaflavins inhibit the SARS-CoV 3CL protease (Chen et al., 2005). The document also notes that these findings still require further in vivo, animal, and clinical trials.
