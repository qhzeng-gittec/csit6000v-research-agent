

Based on `doc.txt`, the government's strategies to encourage compliance in the tax system are set out mainly in Chapter 2, "Tackling non-compliance," and include the following:

**Overall approach and past measures (2.1)**
- The government has "introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC to tackle non-compliance in the tax system – this has secured and protected over £250 billion which would otherwise have gone unpaid" (doc.txt).
- Significant reforms include "the introduction of the General Anti-Abuse Rule; Accelerated Payments; the implementation of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action Plan; and a range of measures to clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance" (doc.txt).
- The stated aim is "for everyone to pay the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are" (doc.txt).

**Modernising tax debt collection (2.2)**
- A call for evidence on "how HMRC can modernise its collection of tax debts to reflect the changing nature of the economy," including e-commerce and business conducted in the UK "without having a presence or physical assets here" (doc.txt).
- It also seeks views on "the small minority of business taxpayers who do not engage with HMRC and hold off paying their tax for as long as they can, forcing HMRC to resort to costly and time-consuming enforcement action" (doc.txt).
- The proposals "will not affect taxpayers who are in temporary financial difficulty and need support from HMRC to get back onto a sustainable financial footing" (doc.txt).

**Mandatory Disclosure Rules (2.3)**
- The government "would implement the OECD's Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules" and is "issuing a technical consultation and draft regulations" (doc.txt).
- These rules "require advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) to report information to the tax authorities on certain prescribed arrangements and structures, including those that could circumvent existing tax transparency reporting rules known as the Common Reporting Standard" (doc.txt).
- The information "will be exchanged with relevant tax authorities to help deter non-compliance, assist in identifying and challenging evasion, and support the development of tax policy" (doc.txt).

**Transfer pricing documentation (2.4)**
- The government "will legislate in 2022 to require businesses that are in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action 13, and a supporting summary audit trail," taking effect "from April 2023" (doc.txt).
- It "will continue to review the case for companies to submit an International Dealings Schedule" (doc.txt).

**Tax conditionality on licences (2.5)**
- Extending tax conditionality to certain licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland "from April 2023," which "makes the renewal of certain licences conditional on applicants completing checks that confirm they are appropriately registered for tax" (doc.txt).
- Licensing bodies "will have to obtain confirmation that an applicant has completed the check before deciding on their renewal application, making it more difficult for traders to operate in the hidden economy" (doc.txt). This is "consistent with reforms which come into force in England and Wales in April 2022" (doc.txt).

**ISA compliance and penalties (2.6)**
- A call for evidence "on proposals to enhance ISA compliance, which will help determine how the current approach can be strengthened and modernised to encourage ISA managers and investors to get things right first time," applying to "all types of ISA – Cash, Stocks and Shares, Junior, Lifetime and Innovative Finance" (doc.txt).

**Making SDLT reliefs fairer (2.7)**
- A consultation on SDLT reliefs for "purchases of mixed property and multiple dwellings," which "are being exploited by some purchasers to unfairly reduce their tax liability," to "make the system fairer and reduce the scope for misuse and incorrect claims" (doc.txt).

**Tackling offshore non-compliance (2.8)**
- An "HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents to explore ideas to tackle offshore tax non-compliance," part of implementing "HMRC's 'No Safe Havens' strategy" (doc.txt).
- Discussion documents sought views on how to help taxpayers reduce offshore non-compliance in relation to "income, gains and assets, and how to reduce and prevent international tax debt." (doc.txt).

**VAT split payment (2.9)**
- Exploring "VAT split payment, an alternative method of VAT collection where the tax element of a digital payment could be remitted directly to HMRC," to assess its potential "both for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation," including through "the Industry Working Group established in 2018" (doc.txt).

**Transparency on marketed avoidance schemes (2.10)**
- HMRC is "publishing its annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes in the UK," setting out "the numbers and characteristics of taxpayers using avoidance schemes in the tax year 2019-2020," including "data on occupations, locations, ages and the average declared incomes of those involved in avoidance" (doc.txt).

Related context in the document: the government is also reviewing the Office of Tax Simplification (OTS) and keeping the Capital Gains Tax system "under review to ensure it is simple and efficient" (doc.txt), and states that "government continues to take action to level the playing field and increase transparency" (doc.txt).
