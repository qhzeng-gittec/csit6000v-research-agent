

Based on `doc.txt`, satisfaction with customer service interactions declines during the holiday season for several interrelated reasons:

1. **A sudden surge in volume.** Retailers bring in 20–40% of their annual sales in the weeks before Christmas, creating a "sudden and temporary increase in volume of sales transactions." As one manager put it, "Q4 is our busiest time of year, and every parameter that impacts a successful customer experience is strained."

2. **Customers behave differently and are harder to please.** The document notes that holiday customers:
   - Are "more unpleasant because of stress," requiring agents to "up the positive energy to calm them down" — while not being able to spend more time with them since customers are just as busy.
   - "Make purchase decisions less thoughtfully," leading to "more purchase regrets and returns that we have to deal with."
   - Have "less experience with retail processes such as coupons and returns" — many "just aren't shoppers" who don't know basics like finding the return label in a box or using a coupon before paying. This forces agents to "spend more time on education and misunderstandings at a time when we're busy anyway."

3. **Workload per agent is the measurable driver.** The Zendesk Benchmark tracked the trend and found "a clear correlation between the drop in satisfaction and the number of tickets per agent. As tickets per agent increased, satisfaction decreased."

4. **Budget constraints.** All of this must be managed "while dealing with the reality of budget constraints," which limits how much staffing or tooling a team can add.

In short, the "holiday dip" is caused by the combination of sharply higher ticket volumes per agent and a customer base that is more stressed, less experienced, and more prone to regret-driven returns — all under budget pressure.
