

Congratulations on your pregnancy! Based solely on the article "Pregnancy nutrition: Foods to avoid during pregnancy" (by Mayo Clinic Staff) in `doc.txt`, here is an explanation of the items on your doctor's list and why each is not acceptable during pregnancy.

**1. Seafood high in mercury**
The article explains that while seafood is a great source of protein and the omega-3 fatty acids in many fish can help your baby's brain and eye development, some fish and shellfish contain levels of mercury that could be harmful. Too much mercury could damage your baby's growing nervous system. The article notes that the bigger and older the fish, the more mercury it's likely to contain. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) says not to eat the following while pregnant: bigeye tuna, king mackerel, marlin, orange roughy, swordfish, shark, and tilefish.

**2. Raw, undercooked, or tainted seafood**
To avoid harmful bacteria or viruses in seafood, the article instructs you not to eat raw fish and shellfish. Examples of raw or undercooked foods to avoid include sushi, sashimi, ceviche, and raw oysters, scallops, or clams. You should also not eat uncooked seafood that's refrigerated, such as seafood labeled nova style, lox, kippered, smoked, or jerky. (Smoked seafood is OK if it's in a casserole or other cooked dish, and canned and shelf-stable versions are safe.) The article also advises watching for local fish advisories if you eat fish from local waters, especially when water pollution is a concern, and if you're not sure about the safety of fish you've already eaten, don't eat any more fish that week. Seafood should be cooked well: fish to an inside temperature of 145 F (63 C), done when it flakes with a fork and looks milky white throughout; shrimp, lobster, and scallops until milky white; and clams, mussels, and oysters until their shells open, throwing away any that don't open.

**3. Undercooked meat, poultry, and eggs**
During pregnancy, you're at a higher risk of food poisoning from bacteria, called foodborne illness. The article explains that how your body reacts to food poisoning when you're pregnant might be worse than if you weren't pregnant, and although it's rare, food poisoning may affect the baby, too. To prevent foodborne illness: fully cook all meats and poultry before eating, using a meat thermometer; cook hot dogs and lunch meats until they're steaming hot, or don't eat them at all, because they can be sources of a rare but serious foodborne illness known as a listeria infection; don't eat pates and meat spreads that are stored in a refrigerator (canned and shelf-stable versions are OK); cook eggs until the yolks and whites are firm, since raw eggs can have harmful bacteria; and don't eat foods that may be made with raw or partly cooked eggs, such as homemade eggnog, raw batter and dough, tiramisu, freshly made or homemade hollandaise sauce, homemade Caesar salad dressing, and homemade ice cream. The article also says not to eat ready-made meat salads or seafood salads, including ham salad, tuna salad, and chicken salad.

**4. Unpasteurized foods**
The article states that you should not eat or drink anything that contains milk that hasn't gone through a process called pasteurization, because products with unpasteurized milk could cause foodborne illness. Avoid soft cheeses, such as brie, feta, and blue cheese, unless the label says they're pasteurized or made with pasteurized milk. Also, don't drink juice or cider that isn't pasteurized.

**5. Unwashed fruits and vegetables, and raw sprouts**
To get rid of harmful bacteria, the article says to wash all raw fruits and vegetables well. It also instructs not to eat any raw sprouts, including alfalfa, clover, radish, and mung bean, because they might have harmful bacteria. Be sure to fully cook sprouts.

**6. Too much caffeine**
Caffeine can cross to the baby, but the effects on the baby aren't clear. To be safe, your health care professional might tell you not to have caffeine while pregnant, or you may be told to limit caffeine to less than 200 milligrams (mg) a day. For reference, an 8-ounce cup of brewed coffee has about 95 mg of caffeine, an 8-ounce cup of brewed tea about 47 mg, and a 12-ounce cola with caffeine about 33 mg.

**7. Herbal tea**
Not much is known about the effects of certain herbs on fetuses. As a result, the article advises not to drink herbal tea unless your health care professional says it's OK — that includes the types of herbal tea made for pregnancy.

**8. Alcohol**
No amount of alcohol has been proved safe during pregnancy, so to be safe, don't drink any alcohol. The article explains that drinking alcohol during pregnancy leads to a higher risk of miscarriage and stillbirth, and may also result in fetal alcohol syndrome, which can cause the face to form oddly and cause lower intelligence. If you're worried about alcohol you drank before you knew you were pregnant, or if you think you need help to stop drinking, talk to your health care professional.

In short, the common thread running through the article is that these foods and drinks can expose you or your baby to harmful bacteria, viruses, mercury, or substances whose effects on a developing fetus are unknown or proven dangerous — which is why your doctor's office has listed them as things to avoid.
