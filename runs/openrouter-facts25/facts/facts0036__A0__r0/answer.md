Based solely on `doc.txt`, here is a summary of the vaccine types discussed and their characteristics:

**1. Live-attenuated vaccines**
- Stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like receptors (TLRs)
- Provide long-term immunity
- May develop hypersensitivity
- Main drawback: costly safety and efficacy assessments

**2. Inactivated viral vaccines**
- Poorly provoke cellular immune responses, which mitigates their efficacy
- Example: an inactivated COVID-19 vaccine manufactured by Sinovac and Wuhan Institute of Biological Products (Sinopharm) in April 2020

**3. Subunit vaccines**
- Safe
- Defects: low immunogenicity, requirement for boosters or adjuvants, and high cost

**4. Nucleic acid-based vaccines (next-generation)**
- Developed based on sequence information; include DNA or mRNA sequences of antigens
- Strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses
- Advantages: fast production; they were the earliest COVID-19 vaccines in clinical trials
- **DNA-based vaccines:** a noticeable advantage is their stability in various storage conditions
- **RNA-based vaccines:** received more attention from companies such as Pfizer/Biontech and Moderna; unlike DNA vaccines, they stimulate an effective humoral immune response as a TLR ligand without adjuvant, and their sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation

**Relationship of Toll-like receptors (TLRs) to vaccines:**
According to the document, TLRs are involved in two ways:
- Live-attenuated vaccines stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses *by inducing TLRs*, which contributes to their long-term immunity.
- RNA-based vaccines act *as a TLR ligand* themselves, enabling them to stimulate an effective humoral immune response without the need for an adjuvant.

In short, TLRs serve both as targets induced by live-attenuated vaccines and as receptors engaged directly by RNA-based vaccines to drive immune responses.
