## 1. Steps to Apoptosis

According to the "Apoptosis Steps" section, apoptosis occurs in a gentle and controlled manner with no negative impact on surrounding cells:

1. **Self-generated trigger** – Apoptosis is usually triggered by self-generated signals within a cell, and is mediated by **caspases**, enzymes found in all cells that cleave specific proteins to initiate cell death.
2. **Drying out and condensation** – The cell expels moisture in order to dry out and condense until fragmentation occurs.
3. **No release into the extracellular environment** – Because the cell does not release into the extracellular environment, there are no morphological changes.
4. **Formation of apoptotic bodies** – Small vesicles called apoptotic bodies form to transport the contents of the cell elsewhere, enabling cells to die gently and without causing inflammation.
5. **Chromatin condensation** – Apoptosis is signaled by chromatin condensation in the cell's nucleus, which allows scientists to determine whether a cell is dying naturally or unnaturally.

## 2. Differentiating Factors of Necrosis vs. Apoptosis

The document lists the following distinctions:

- **Process** – Apoptosis is characterized by the shrinking of the cytoplasm, resulting in condensation of the nucleus. Necrosis occurs when the cytoplasm and mitochondria swell, resulting in cell lysis or a rupture in the cell membrane.
- **Membrane Integrity** – Blebbing is a defining feature of apoptosis (the cytoskeleton degrades and the membrane bulges outward; this has no effect on the membrane's integrity). During necrosis, membrane integrity is loosened and significantly reduced, if not completely lost.
- **Organelle Behavior** – Organelles can continue to function even after a cell has died due to apoptosis. During necrotic cell death, organelles swell and disintegrate, and after necrosis they are no longer functional.
- **Scope of Affected Cells** – Apoptosis is a localized process that only destroys individual or single cells. Necrosis can spread to affect adjacent cell groups, causing damage beyond the initial site.
- **Bodily Influence** – Apoptosis is involved in controlling cell numbers and is frequently beneficial (though it can cause diseases if abnormal in either direction). Necrosis is always harmful and, if left untreated, can be fatal.
- **Nature of the process** – Apoptosis is regarded as a naturally occurring, controlled and timely process, making it predictable and beneficial to the host. Necrosis is regarded as a pathological process, often unregulated and caused by toxins, infections, or traumas.

## 3. Different Ways a Cell Can Be Affected by Necrosis

The document describes necrosis as unintentional cell death occurring when cells are exposed to extreme conditions, with damage to the internal environment. The specific ways/causes listed are:

- **Bacterial infection** – Bacteria entering through an airway or open wound can cause infections linked to unplanned cell death.
- **Fungal infection (mycosis)** – A fungus infiltrating human tissues can result in skin or internal system diseases, as well as unprogrammed cell death.
- **Pancreatitis** – Inflammation of the pancreas, a gland that helps regulate hormones and digestion.
- **Protein denaturation** – Weak bonds in proteins break down, causing the cells' ability to function properly to suffer.
