Based on `doc.txt`, here's what the document says about hunger and thirst during a fast:

**Hunger:** Not necessarily more than you might expect. The document states that "fasting decreases hunger hormones and increases fullness." While you may feel hungry when you first try fasting, "many people actually report reduced hunger once they are used to it." It also notes that missing a meal may lead to slightly more food at the next meal, but total energy intake is still lower. Some of the initial hunger may just be habit — your body expecting food at certain times — and most people adapt quickly.

**Thirst:** Yes, you may need to pay more attention to fluids. The document explains that when you eat, you get some water from your food, so during a fast "you are missing out on some of the fluids you normally get," which "can lead to you becoming dehydrated, causing side effects such as tiredness and headaches." It recommends the best option for staying hydrated is water (optionally flavoured with lemon, orange, lime, or cucumber), and that black or naturally flavoured coffee, tea, or broth are also suitable.

**One important overlap:** The document warns that "dehydration can also be mistaken for hunger, which can make it more difficult to stick to your planned fast." So if you feel hungrier during a fast, it may partly be thirst rather than true hunger.

In short: hunger tends to decrease as you adapt, but you do need to actively drink fluids (and possibly get some salt, e.g., via salted water or bone broth) since you're no longer getting water from food.
