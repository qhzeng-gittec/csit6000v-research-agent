

Based on `doc.txt`, a quick correction to the premise: the document never mentions Elon Musk. It's about **Jared Isaacman**, a tech entrepreneur and the founder/CEO of the Shift4 credit-card-processing company, who led the "Polaris Dawn" mission with SpaceX.

**His experience with space trips:**
- This was Isaacman's **second chartered flight with SpaceX**, with two more still planned under his personally financed space exploration program named **Polaris** (after the North Star).
- His first spaceflight was in **2021**, for which he paid an undisclosed sum; he took along contest winners and a pediatric cancer survivor and raised more than **$250 million for St. Jude Children's Research Hospital**.
- He became only the **264th person ever to perform a spacewalk** (SpaceX's Sarah Gillis was the 265th), and until now all spacewalks had been done by professional astronauts.

**Specs of the trip:**
- A **five-day trip** that ended with a splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico near Florida's **Dry Tortugas** (about 70 miles/113 km west of Key West) in the predawn darkness on Sunday, Sept. 15, 2024.
- The crew of four: Isaacman, two SpaceX engineers (Anna Menon and Scott "Kidd" Poteet), and a former Air Force Thunderbird pilot (Sarah Gillis).
- They orbited nearly **460 miles (740 km) above Earth** — higher than the ISS and Hubble — and hit a **peak altitude of 875 miles (1,408 km)** after Tuesday's liftoff.
- They performed the **first private spacewalk** on Thursday; the Dragon capsule's hatch was open barely a half-hour, and the spacewalk lasted **less than two hours** (most of that time was spent depressurizing and re-pressurizing the cabin). Isaacman emerged only to his waist to test SpaceX's brand-new spacesuit, followed by Gillis, who went knee-high.
- It was the first time SpaceX aimed for a splashdown near the Dry Tortugas, a change prompted by two weeks of poor weather forecasts.

**How the trip benefited the company (SpaceX):**
- SpaceX considers the brief spacewalk a **starting point to test spacesuit technology for future, longer missions to Mars**.
- It also marked a new operational capability: the first splashdown targeted near the Dry Tortugas, giving SpaceX an alternative landing site when Florida weather is unfavorable.

One note: the document says Isaacman **shared the cost** of the Polaris Dawn mission with SpaceX and won't disclose how much he spent — so the "rich people flexing" framing is only partially supported; the doc frames it as a privately financed program that also raised significant charity money on his first flight.
