## Overall approach (para 2.1)
Since 2010 the government has introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC, securing and protecting over £250 billion that would otherwise have gone unpaid. Significant reforms cited include:
- the **General Anti-Abuse Rule**
- **Accelerated Payments**
- implementation of the **OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action Plan**
- a range of measures to **clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance**

The stated aim is "for everyone to pay the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are."

## Specific strategies

**1. Modernising tax debt collection (2.2)** – A call for evidence on how HMRC can modernise debt collection for a changing economy, including e-commerce businesses operating in the UK without a physical presence. It also targets the small minority of business taxpayers who do not engage with HMRC and delay payment, forcing costly enforcement. Taxpayers in temporary financial difficulty (including those affected by the pandemic) are excluded.

**2. OECD Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules (2.3)** – The government is issuing a technical consultation and draft regulations. Advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) must report prescribed arrangements and structures to tax authorities, including those that could circumvent the Common Reporting Standard. Information is exchanged with relevant tax authorities to deter non-compliance, help identify and challenge evasion, and support tax policy development.

**3. Improving transfer pricing documentation (2.4)** – Legislation in 2022 will require businesses in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD BEPS Action 13, plus a supporting summary audit trail, effective April 2023. The government will continue reviewing the case for an International Dealings Schedule.

**4. Tax conditionality on licences (2.5)** – From April 2023, renewal of certain licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland will be conditional on applicants completing checks confirming they are appropriately registered for tax (consistent with reforms in England and Wales from April 2022). Licensing bodies must obtain confirmation before deciding renewals, making it harder for traders to operate in the hidden economy. The government remains committed to exploring wider application of tax conditionality.

**5. Strengthening ISA compliance and penalties (2.6)** – A call for evidence on enhancing compliance across all ISA types (Cash, Stocks and Shares, Junior, Lifetime and Innovative Finance), including checks on Innovative Finance ISA managers and the penalties regime, to encourage ISA managers and investors to "get things right first time."

**6. Making SDLT reliefs fairer (2.7)** – A consultation on reliefs for purchases of mixed property and multiple dwellings, which are being exploited to unfairly reduce tax liability, with potential changes to reduce misuse and incorrect claims.

**7. Tackling offshore non-compliance (2.8)** – An HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents, part of HMRC's "No Safe Havens" strategy, to reduce offshore non-compliance on non-UK income, gains and assets and to reduce and prevent international tax debt.

**8. Exploring VAT split payment (2.9)** – An alternative VAT collection method where the tax element of a digital payment is remitted directly to HMRC, being assessed for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation, developed collaboratively with the payments industry (Industry Working Group, established 2018).

**9. Transparency on marketed avoidance schemes (2.10)** – HMRC publishes an annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes, including the numbers and characteristics (occupations, locations, ages, average declared incomes) of taxpayers using them.

## Supporting measures (Chapters 1 and 3)
- **Office of Tax Simplification (1.10–1.12)**: A first five-year review of the OTS with eight recommendations; a response to the OTS's Capital Gains Tax review (five technical recommendations accepted, with CGT kept under review to ensure it is simple and efficient); and a response to the Inheritance Tax review (no design changes at this point).
- **Broader policy framing (3.1)**: The government emphasises levelling the playing field and increasing transparency, with new consultations and next steps on reform work already underway.

In short, the document describes a mix of deterrence and enforcement (anti-abuse rules, accelerated payments, penalties, debt collection), transparency and information exchange (mandatory disclosure, transfer pricing documentation, annual avoidance-scheme reporting), structural barriers to non-compliance (tax conditionality on licences, VAT split payment), and simplification and stakeholder engagement (OTS reviews, stakeholder forums, consultations and calls for evidence).
