

Based on `doc.txt`, the difference between hard and soft information is as follows:

**Hard information**
- Consists of conventional financial metrics and documentation, such as sales fluctuations, costs of inputs, and specific industry factors.
- Is used in automated and statistical underwriting methodologies to price loans.
- Is already quite uniform (standardized and digitized), so large lenders may already have automated technological systems that can handle large volumes of it, making reporting less expensive per applicant.
- Is typically collected by large lenders, who lend to borrowers with more conventional financial metrics and documentation.

**Soft information**
- Is gathered through relationship lending, in which small lenders develop close familiarity with their customers.
- Contains circumstantial details about factors such as non-standardized business risks, insufficient collateral, or weak or thin (business) credit histories.
- Because of soft information, the underwriting process to determine more customized loan products and loan pricing is generally less algorithmic and more labor intensive.
- Is more unique to applicant circumstances, infrequent, and localized, so standardizing it for electronic collection and reporting is challenging.
- Carries a higher reporting cost per applicant, especially for small lenders that lack the volume of applications to justify the costs of converting soft information to digital and secure formats.

In short, hard information is standardized, conventional financial data suited to automated underwriting, while soft information is idiosyncratic, relationship-based, circumstantial detail that makes underwriting more labor intensive and the data harder and costlier to standardize and report.
