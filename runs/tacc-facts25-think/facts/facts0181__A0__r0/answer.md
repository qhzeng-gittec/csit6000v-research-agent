

Based solely on `doc.txt`, the government's strategies to encourage compliance in the tax system are set out mainly in Chapter 2, "Tackling non-compliance." The document states that since 2010 the government has introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC, securing and protecting over £250 billion that would otherwise have gone unpaid, with the overarching aim that "everyone [pays] the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are." The specific strategies include:

**1. Anti-avoidance and anti-evasion legislation (2.1)**
Significant past reforms that form the base for current measures include:
- The General Anti-Abuse Rule
- Accelerated Payments
- Implementation of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action Plan
- A range of measures to clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance

**2. Modernising tax debt collection (2.2)**
The government is publishing a call for evidence on how HMRC can modernise collection of tax debts to reflect the changing economy, where e-commerce has enabled business to be conducted in the UK without a physical presence or assets. It also seeks views on HMRC's approach to the small minority of business taxpayers who do not engage with HMRC and delay paying as long as possible, forcing costly enforcement action. The proposals explicitly will not affect taxpayers in temporary financial difficulty (including those affected by the pandemic) who need support to get back on a sustainable footing.

**3. Mandatory Disclosure Rules (2.3)**
Following the Spring Budget 2021 announcement, the government is issuing a technical consultation and draft regulations to implement the OECD's Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules. These require advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) to report information to tax authorities on certain prescribed arrangements and structures, including those that could circumvent the Common Reporting Standard. The information will be exchanged with relevant tax authorities to deter non-compliance, help identify and challenge evasion, and support tax policy development.

**4. Transfer pricing documentation (2.4)**
The government will legislate in 2022 to require businesses in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD BEPS Action 13, plus a supporting summary audit trail, taking effect from April 2023. It will continue reviewing the case for companies to submit an International Dealings Schedule.

**5. Tax conditionality on licences (2.5)**
From April 2023, renewal of certain licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland will be conditional on applicants completing checks confirming they are appropriately registered for tax (consistent with reforms in England and Wales from April 2022). Licensing bodies must obtain confirmation before deciding on renewal, making it harder for traders to operate in the hidden economy. The government remains committed to exploring wider application of tax conditionality.

**6. Strengthening ISA compliance and penalties (2.6)**
Following the December 2020 announcement on checks of Innovative Finance ISA managers and the penalties regime, the government is publishing a call for evidence on enhancing ISA compliance across all ISA types (Cash, Stocks and Shares, Junior, Lifetime and Innovative Finance) to encourage ISA managers and investors to "get things right first time."

**7. Making Stamp Duty Land Tax reliefs fairer (2.7)**
A consultation is being published on SDLT reliefs for purchases of mixed property and multiple dwellings, which are being exploited by some purchasers to unfairly reduce their tax liability. Potential changes aim to make the system fairer and reduce the scope for misuse and incorrect claims.

**8. Tackling offshore non-compliance (2.8)**
The government has established an HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents to explore ideas for tackling offshore tax non-compliance, following two spring 2021 discussion documents under HMRC's "No Safe Havens" strategy. These sought views on reducing offshore non-compliance in relation to non-UK income, gains and assets, and on reducing and preventing international tax debt.

**9. Exploring VAT split payment (2.9)**
The government is exploring VAT split payment, an alternative collection method where the tax element of a digital payment could be remitted directly to HMRC. Though at an exploratory stage, it is being assessed for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation, worked on collaboratively with the payments industry through the Industry Working Group established in 2018.

**10. Transparency on marketed avoidance schemes (2.10)**
HMRC is publishing its annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes in the UK, setting out the numbers and characteristics (occupations, locations, ages, average declared incomes) of taxpayers using avoidance schemes in 2019–2020.

In addition, Chapter 1 of the document shows the government supporting compliance indirectly through simplification: publishing its first five-year review of the Office of Tax Simplification (with eight recommendations), accepting five technical recommendations from the OTS's Capital Gains Tax review to keep CGT simple and efficient, and responding to the OTS's Inheritance Tax reviews (deciding not to proceed with design changes at this point).
