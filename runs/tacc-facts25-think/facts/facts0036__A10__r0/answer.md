

Based on `doc.txt`, the following vaccine types are described:

**Live-attenuated vaccines** — "stimulate the innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs) with long-term immunity and may develop hypersensitivity. The main drawback of these vaccines is their costly safety and efficacy assessments" (doc.txt).

**Inactivated viral vaccines** — "poorly provoke cellular immune responses which mitigate their efficacy" (doc.txt). An inactivated COVID-19 vaccine was manufactured by Sinovac and Wuhan Institute of Biological Products (Sinopharm) in April 2020 (doc.txt).

**Subunit vaccines** — "safe, with some defects including low immunogenicity, booster or adjuvant requirement, and high cost" (doc.txt).

**Nucleic acid-based vaccines** (DNA or mRNA) — "include DNA or mRNA sequences of antigens that strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses" (doc.txt).
- DNA-based: advantages include "fast production" and "their stability in various storage conditions" (doc.txt).
- RNA-based: "stimulate effective humoral immune response as TLR ligand without adjuvant, and its sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation" (doc.txt); they received more attention from companies like Pfizer/Biontech and Moderna (doc.txt).

**Relation of Toll-like receptors to vaccines:** The document links TLRs to vaccines in two ways. First, live-attenuated vaccines work "by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs)," which is how they stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses (doc.txt). Second, RNA-based vaccines act "as TLR ligand," meaning they stimulate an effective humoral immune response through TLRs without needing an adjuvant (doc.txt).

The document does not describe the molecular mechanisms of TLRs themselves beyond these two roles.
