1. **For specific purposes (Article 6(1)(a))** – Processing is lawful where "the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes."

2. **Freely given (Article 7(4))** – When assessing whether consent is freely given, "utmost account shall be taken of whether, inter alia, the performance of a contract, including the provision of a service, is conditional on consent to the processing of personal data that is not necessary for the performance of that contract."

3. **In a written declaration (Article 7(2))** – If consent is given in the context of a written declaration which also concerns other matters, the request for consent must be "clearly distinguishable from the other matters, in an intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language." Any part of such a declaration that infringes the Regulation is not binding.

4. **With prior information about withdrawal (Article 7(3))** – Prior to giving consent, the data subject must be informed that they have the right to withdraw consent at any time, and that withdrawal will not affect the lawfulness of processing before its withdrawal. It must be as easy to withdraw as to give consent.

5. **By children (Article 8)** – In relation to information society services offered directly to a child, processing is lawful where the child is at least 16 years old (i.e., the child can give consent). Where the child is below 16, consent must be given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility (Member States may set a lower age, but not below 13).

6. **Explicit consent for special categories (Article 9(2)(a))** – A person may give "explicit consent to the processing of those personal data for one or more specified purposes" (e.g., data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, health data, etc.), except where Union or Member State law provides that the prohibition may not be lifted by the data subject.

Additionally, the document notes (Article 7(1)) that where processing is based on consent, the controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject has consented, and (Article 9(2)(c)) that a data subject who is "physically or legally incapable of giving consent" cannot give consent, in which case processing may be based on protecting vital interests instead.
