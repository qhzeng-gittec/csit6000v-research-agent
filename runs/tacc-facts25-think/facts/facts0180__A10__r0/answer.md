

Based on `doc.txt`, the two evaluations differ in the following ways:

**Sideline evaluation (NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment / "Sideline Survey")**
- It is the immediate assessment a player must undergo when a sign or symptom of concussion is exhibited or reported: "the player must undergo the entire NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment" (doc.txt).
- It is conducted "inside the medical evaluation tent on the Sideline (or in the Locker Room if the Club medical staff elects to conduct it in the Locker Room)" and "performed using the tablet or other technology assigned by the NFL" (doc.txt).
- At a minimum it consists of the items following: "the entire NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment1 which, at a minimum, must consist of the following:" (doc.txt), including "All Maddocks' questions;" (doc.txt).
- It can end with return to play: "If, upon completing the Sideline Survey, the Club physician, after consultation with the sideline UNC, concludes that the player did notsustain a concussion, then the playermay return to play." (doc.txt).
- It serves as a gate to the locker room exam: "If any elements of the sideline assessment are positive, inconclusive, or suspicious for the presence of a concussion, the player must be escorted to the locker room immediately for the complete NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment" (doc.txt).

**Locker room evaluation (NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment / "Locker Room Exam")**
- It is "the standardized acute evaluation tool that has been developed by the NFL's Head, Neck and Spine Committee to be used by Clubs' medical staffs and designated UNCs to evaluate potential concussions during practices and on game day" (doc.txt).
- It is "based on the Standardized Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT 5) published by the International Concussion in Sport Group (McCrory, et al., 2017), modified for use in the NFL" (doc.txt).
- Unlike the sideline survey, it "can be used to aid in the diagnosis of concussion even if there is a delayed onset of symptoms" (doc.txt).
- It is tied to baseline comparison and records: "The ongoing use of the Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment in conjunction with the preseason baseline and post-injury testing provides detailed data regarding each athlete's injury and recovery course," and "Being able to compare the results... to the baseline information obtained in the preseason improves the value of this instrument" (doc.txt).
- It carries the diagnostic decision: "The Club physician shall be responsible for determining whether the player is diagnosed as having a concussion" (doc.txt), and "The athlete may have a concussion despite being able to complete the NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment "within normal limits" compared to baseline," (doc.txt).

In short, the sideline survey is the immediate, tablet-based on-field screening (including video review and a focused neurological exam) that can clear a player to return to play, while the locker room exam is the more comprehensive, SCAT 5-based standardized assessment used for diagnosis (including delayed-onset cases), baseline comparison, and documentation.
