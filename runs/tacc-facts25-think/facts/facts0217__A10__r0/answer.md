**Point 6 — the right to information**
- "Every child and young person has a right to information, in a form that is understandable to them." (doc.txt)
- It covers information "about their health and healthcare. This includes information about the choice of health care services available." (doc.txt)
- It requires that "the language and format used are appropriate to the child's or young person's abilities and level of understanding" and that "they have understood the information given and had every opportunity to participate in the conversations about their health and care." (doc.txt)
- It includes "the right of tamariki and rangatahi to have access to information in Te Reo Māori and for those from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds to have access to information in their own language." (doc.txt)
- It requires that "health professionals talk directly to children and young people, as well as to their families/whānau, even if the child or young person may seem unable to comprehend." (doc.txt)
- It entitles children "to know what is going to happen to them before a procedure occurs and to be given honest information about their condition and treatment outcomes, and to be helped to select and practice strategies for coping." (doc.txt)
- It notes that "Advance preparation for hospitalisation, healthcare procedures or impending surgery provides children and young people with a sense of mastery over the healthcare environment and helps them to cope more effectively with potentially stressful situations." (doc.txt)

**Point 7 — the right to participate in decision-making**
- "Every child and young person has a right to participate in decision-making and, as appropriate to their capabilities, to make decisions about their care." (doc.txt)
- It includes "the right to be involved in decisions about the use, return or disposal of any bodily parts or substances removed, changed or added in the course of health care." (doc.txt)
- It states that "they are always entitled to a second opinion" and that "If their views cannot be acted on, they are entitled to an explanation." (doc.txt)
- It requires that "Informed consent is to be sought from children, young people and their families/whānau before they are involved in teaching or research," that participants "must have the opportunity to withdraw at any time without having to give a reason," and that "The decision not to participate in teaching or research must not alter access to treatment." (doc.txt)
- It extends beyond the individual: "It includes encouraging and supporting children and young people as groups to be involved in consultation on the development, implementation and evaluation of the services, policies and strategies that have an impact on them." (doc.txt)
- It adds that "Ethical oversight by a Human Research Ethics Committee of all research projects conducted in child healthcare services is part of protecting the children and young people involved." (doc.txt)

**Key differences**
- Point 6 is about *receiving* information: understandable form, appropriate language (including Te Reo Māori and other languages), direct communication from health professionals, and advance preparation before procedures.
- Point 7 is about *taking part in and making* decisions: involvement in decisions about care (including decisions about bodily parts or substances), the right to a second opinion, an explanation when views cannot be acted on, informed consent, group consultation on services and policies, and the rules governing participation in teaching or research (withdrawal without reason, no effect on treatment access, and Human Research Ethics Committee oversight).
