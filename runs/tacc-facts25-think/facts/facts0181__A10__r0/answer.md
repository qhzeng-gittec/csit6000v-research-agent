

Based on `doc.txt`, the government's strategies to encourage compliance in the tax system are set out in Chapter 2, "Tackling non-compliance":

**Overall approach and past measures (2.1)**
- "Since 2010 the government has introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC to tackle non-compliance in the tax system – this has secured and protected over £250 billion which would otherwise have gone unpaid."
- "Significant reforms include: the introduction of the General Anti-Abuse Rule; Accelerated Payments; the implementation of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action Plan; and a range of measures to clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance."
- The stated aim is "for everyone to pay the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are."

**Modernising tax debt collection (2.2)**
- The government is "publishing a call for evidence on how HMRC can modernise its collection of tax debts to reflect the changing nature of the economy," including e-commerce and "the conduct of business in the UK without having a presence or physical assets here."
- It also seeks views on "HMRC's approach to the small minority of business taxpayers who do not engage with HMRC and hold off paying their tax for as long as they can, forcing HMRC to resort to costly and time-consuming enforcement action."

**Mandatory Disclosure Rules (2.3)**
- The government "announced that it would implement the OECD's Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules" and is "issuing a technical consultation and draft regulations."
- These rules "require advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) to report information to the tax authorities on certain prescribed arrangements and structures," and the information "will be exchanged with relevant tax authorities to help deter non-compliance, assist in identifying and challenging evasion, and support the development of tax policy."

**Transfer pricing documentation (2.4)**
- The government "will legislate in 2022 to require businesses that are in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action 13, and a supporting summary audit trail," taking effect from April 2023.

**Tax conditionality on licences (2.5)**
- The government is extending "tax conditionality to certain licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland from April 2023," making "the renewal of certain licences conditional on applicants completing checks that confirm they are appropriately registered for tax."
- This makes "it more difficult for traders to operate in the hidden economy," and the government "remains committed to exploring further the wider application of tax conditionality."

**ISA compliance and penalties (2.6)**
- The government is "publishing a call for evidence on proposals to enhance ISA compliance," to strengthen and modernise the approach "to encourage ISA managers and investors to get things right first time," applying to all types of ISA.

**Stamp Duty Land Tax reliefs (2.7)**
- A consultation on SDLT reliefs for mixed property and multiple dwellings, which "are being exploited by some purchasers to unfairly reduce their tax liability," sets out "potential changes to make the system fairer and reduce the scope for misuse and incorrect claims."

**Offshore tax non-compliance (2.8)**
- The government "has established an HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents to explore ideas to tackle offshore tax non-compliance," as part of implementing HMRC's "'No Safe Havens' strategy," seeking views on "how to help taxpayers reduce offshore non-compliance in relation to non-UK income, gains and assets, and how to reduce and prevent international tax debt."

**VAT split payment (2.9)**
- The government is "committed to exploring further VAT split payment, an alternative method of VAT collection where the tax element of a digital payment could be remitted directly to HMRC," assessing its potential "both for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation."

**Transparency on marketed avoidance schemes (2.10)**
- "HMRC is publishing its annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes in the UK," setting out "the numbers and characteristics of taxpayers using avoidance schemes in the tax year 2019-2020," including "data on occupations, locations, ages and the average declared incomes of those involved in avoidance."
