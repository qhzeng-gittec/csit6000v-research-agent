**Types of vaccines and their characteristics**

1. **Live-attenuated vaccines** – Stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like receptors (TLRs); provide long-term immunity but may develop hypersensitivity. Main drawback: costly safety and efficacy assessments.

2. **Inactivated viral vaccines** – Poorly provoke cellular immune responses, which mitigates their efficacy. An inactivated COVID-19 vaccine was manufactured in April 2020 by Sinovac and the Wuhan Institute of Biological Products (Sinopharm).

3. **Subunit vaccines** – Safe, but have defects including low immunogenicity, requirement for boosters or adjuvants, and high cost.

4. **Nucleic acid-based vaccines (next-generation)** – Developed from sequence information; contain DNA or mRNA sequences of antigens that strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses.
   - **DNA-based** – Advantages include fast production, being among the earliest COVID-19 vaccines in clinical trials, and stability in various storage conditions.
   - **RNA-based** – Received more attention from companies such as Pfizer/Biontech and Moderna. Unlike DNA vaccines, they stimulate an effective humoral immune response as a TLR ligand without adjuvant, and their sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation.

**How Toll-like receptors relate to vaccines**

According to the document, TLRs are involved in two ways:
- Live-attenuated vaccines stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses *by inducing TLRs*, which underlies their long-term immunity.
- RNA-based vaccines act as a *TLR ligand*, enabling them to stimulate an effective humoral immune response without the need for an adjuvant.

In short, TLRs serve as molecular targets/ligand-recognition points through which certain vaccine platforms (live-attenuated and RNA-based) trigger immune activation.
