**Why 2025 instead of 2024?**

Nintendo has not officially announced any follow-up to the current Switch, so everything here is rumor-based. The document notes that the next console is "most likely arriving in 2025" rather than 2024. In February 2024, the Japanese publication Nikkei suggested the proposed Switch 2 would launch in March 2025 at the earliest, contradicting previous reports. According to the article, the 2025 date was likely due to Nintendo stockpiling consoles to avoid scalping at launch — the piece recalls how difficult it was to secure a Switch at its 2017 launch, when inflated aftermarket pricing from resellers was a problem. Adding to the intrigue, Nintendo confirmed it won't be exhibiting at this year's Gamescom in Cologne, Germany, which raises questions about what to expect in the latter half of 2024, though the document says only time will tell. Notably, Nintendo president Shuntaro Furukawa confirmed in a Nikkei interview that Nintendo will support the current-gen Switch until at least March 2025, which is consistent with a 2025 transition.

**What will it look like?**

There isn't a lot of confirmed information. VGC reported that development kits for the potential new console are already with partner studios, and sources cited some returning features: the new Switch could keep the portability of its predecessor and feature a cartridge slot for physical games. However, the document cautions that patents don't necessarily translate directly to future products, so there's no guarantee the Switch 2 will feature updated Joy-Cons.

There's no official word on backwards compatibility. Nintendo of America president Doug Bowser declined to comment on the rumored console but discussed the Nintendo Account system, which players' digital libraries are currently tied to. He said the Nintendo Account will allow Nintendo to communicate with players "if and when we make a transition to a new platform, to help ease that process." This could suggest that your favorite Switch games may work on the next console, but the document advises taking this with a pinch of salt. It's also unclear whether your current accessories and controllers will be compatible with the new model.
