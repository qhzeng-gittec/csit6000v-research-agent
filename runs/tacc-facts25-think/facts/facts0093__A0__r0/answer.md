

Based on `doc.txt`, satisfaction with customer service interactions declines around the holidays for several interrelated reasons:

1. **Sudden surge in volume:** Retailers bring in 20–40% of their annual sales in the weeks leading up to Christmas, with peak days like Black Friday and Cyber Monday. This creates a sudden, temporary spike in sales transactions that strains every parameter of the customer experience.

2. **Customers are different and more difficult:** During the holiday rush, customers tend to be more unpleasant because of stress, make purchase decisions less thoughtfully (leading to more purchase regrets and returns), and have less experience with retail processes such as coupons and returns. As one manager noted, many holiday customers "just aren't shoppers" — they don't know things like finding the return label in the box or using a coupon before paying, so agents must spend more time on education and misunderstandings.

3. **Workload per agent increases:** The Zendesk Benchmark found a clear correlation between the drop in satisfaction and the number of tickets per agent — as tickets per agent increased, satisfaction decreased.

4. **Budget constraints:** Teams must handle the increased volume while maintaining satisfaction levels, all under budget constraints, and they can't take more time with each customer because customers are just as busy as the agents.

In short, the "holiday dip" is driven by the combination of dramatically higher ticket volumes (more tickets per agent) and a harder-to-serve customer base that is more stressed, less experienced, and more prone to returns and misunderstandings.
