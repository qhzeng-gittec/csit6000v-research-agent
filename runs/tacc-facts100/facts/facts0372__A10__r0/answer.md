**Introduction**
- *Staphylococcus aureus* is a major bacterial human pathogen causing a wide variety of clinical manifestations.
- Infections are common in community-acquired and hospital-acquired settings; treatment is challenging due to multi-drug resistant strains such as MRSA.
- *S. aureus* is found in the environment and in normal human flora, on skin and mucous membranes (most often the nasal area) of most healthy individuals.
- It does not normally cause infection on healthy skin, but if it enters the bloodstream or internal tissues, it may cause potentially serious infections.
- Transmission is typically from direct contact, though some infections involve other methods.

**Epidemiology**
- *S. aureus* (including MRSA) is found on skin and mucous membranes; humans are the major reservoir.
- Up to half of all adults are colonized; approximately 15% of the population persistently carry *S. aureus* in the anterior nares.
- Some populations have higher colonization rates (up to 80%): health care workers, persons who use needles regularly (diabetics and IV drug users), hospitalized patients, and immunocompromised individuals.
- *S. aureus* can be transmitted person-to-person by direct contact or by fomites.
