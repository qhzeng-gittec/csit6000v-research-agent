

# How COVID-19 Affected Behavioral Health (per `doc.txt`)

**Overall trend:** Data from multiple sources suggest that mental health symptoms and substance use increased since the start of the pandemic. Symptoms include emotional distress and anxiety, depression, and trauma-related conditions. Although comprehensive 2020 national data were not yet available, most 2020 data from rapid surveys and short-term reporting suggest increased behavioral health morbidity in the United States.

**Stress and mental health:**
- A SHADAC (Robert Wood Johnson Foundation) survey in April 2020 found over 90% of U.S. adults reported additional stress caused by the pandemic.
- A Kaiser Family Foundation survey found a rising share of Americans reporting pandemic-related stress negatively affecting their mental health: 32% in March 2020, 45% in April, and 53% in July 2020.
- Stress reactions can include fear, tension, irritability, sadness, or feeling overwhelmed. Coping strategies can be adaptive (social connections, physical activity, hobbies, good sleep) or maladaptive (substance use, excessive screen time, risky behaviors); maladaptive strategies may reduce stress in the moment but worsen problems long term.

**Mental health disorders:**
- NCHS/Census Bureau Household Pulse Survey data indicate the percentage of Americans experiencing symptoms of a mental health disorder increased during the pandemic.
- CDC analysis showed socioeconomic and psychosocial-related emergency department (ED) visits increased in April 2020 versus April 2019, even as total ED visits fell over 40%. Other research suggests ED visits for mental health conditions may have decreased in the first months, but less than overall ED visits.

**Suicide:**
- One CDC analysis found roughly twice as many U.S. adults reported serious consideration of suicide in the prior 30 days compared with 2018 (10.7% vs. 4.3%).
- The Disaster Distress Helpline saw a 335% increase in calls in the first five months of the pandemic, though the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline did not report increased call volume.
- Effects on suicide attempts and deaths are unclear: ED visits for suicide attempts decreased March–October 2020 vs. 2019 (but less than overall ED visits), and preliminary 2020 data suggest suicide deaths may have decreased overall. Regional and demographic differences exist—e.g., ED visits for suspected suicide attempts began rising among adolescents (especially girls) in May 2020, and Maryland researchers found suicide mortality increased for Black residents but decreased for White residents from March to May 2020.

**Substance use and overdoses:**
- Preliminary ONDCP (ODMAP) data show an 11% increase in fatal overdoses and a 19% increase in nonfatal overdoses from March–May 2020 vs. the same months in 2019, with nearly 62% of participating counties reporting increases. However, overdose trends were already rising before the pandemic, making attribution difficult.
- CDC data show overdose deaths were already increasing before the pandemic but accelerated after it began, with notable rises in deaths involving synthetic opioids, as well as commonly prescribed opioids and heroin (both previously declining).
- CDC also found more drug overdoses (including opioid overdoses) in ED visits between March and October 2020 vs. 2019.
- People with substance use disorders may face higher risk of contracting SARS-CoV-2 (unstable housing, high incarceration rates, difficulty with physical distancing) and higher risk of complications, since substance use can suppress the immune system or impair respiratory function.
