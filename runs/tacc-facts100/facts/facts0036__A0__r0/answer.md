**Types of vaccines and their characteristics**

1. **Live-attenuated vaccines** – Stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs), providing long-term immunity. They may develop hypersensitivity, and their main drawback is the costly safety and efficacy assessment.

2. **Inactivated viral vaccines** – Poorly provoke cellular immune responses, which mitigates their efficacy. An inactivated COVID-19 vaccine was manufactured in April 2020 by Sinovac and the Wuhan Institute of Biological Products (Sinopharm).

3. **Subunit vaccines** – Safe, but have defects including low immunogenicity, the requirement of a booster or adjuvant, and high cost.

4. **Nucleic acid-based vaccines (next-generation)** – Developed from sequence information; they contain DNA or mRNA sequences of antigens that strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses. Advantages include fast production and being the earliest COVID-19 vaccines in clinical trials.
   - **DNA-based vaccines** – Notably stable in various storage conditions.
   - **RNA-based vaccines** – Received more attention from companies such as Pfizer/Biontech and Moderna. Unlike DNA vaccines, they stimulate an effective humoral immune response as a TLR ligand without adjuvant, and their sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation.

**Relation of Toll-like receptors to vaccines**

According to the document, TLRs are linked to vaccines in two ways: (1) live-attenuated vaccines stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing TLRs, and (2) RNA-based vaccines act as TLR ligands, enabling them to stimulate an effective humoral immune response without the need for an adjuvant.
