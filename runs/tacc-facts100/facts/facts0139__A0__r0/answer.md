**Hunger:**
- You may feel hungry when you first try fasting, and if you're used to a set eating routine, your body expecting food at certain times can trigger feelings of hunger.
- However, fasting **decreases hunger hormones and increases fullness**, so many people actually report **reduced hunger** once they are used to it. Most people adapt quite quickly and find it increasingly easy to fast.
- Missing a meal may result in slightly more food being eaten at the next meal, but total energy intake is still less than if you had eaten both meals.

**Thirst:**
- Yes, you can become thirstier/dehydrated: when you eat, you get some water from your food, so during a fast you miss out on some of the fluids you normally get. This can lead to dehydration, with side effects such as tiredness and headaches.
- The document notes that dehydration can even be **mistaken for hunger**, making it harder to stick to the fast.
- To stay hydrated, the best option is water (which can be flavoured with lemon, orange, lime, or cucumber); black or naturally flavoured coffee, tea, or broth are also suitable options.

So in short: hunger tends to decrease as you adapt, but you do need to actively drink fluids during a fast since you're no longer getting water from food.
