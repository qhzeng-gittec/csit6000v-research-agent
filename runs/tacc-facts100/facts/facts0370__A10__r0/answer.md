

To apply, you should "apply for EI right away, even if you do not have your ROE yet," since "you will need an ROE for every employer you worked for in the past 12 months" or, if you lack it, "proof of your employment, such as pay stubs and T4 slips." If "you do not apply within 4 weeks after your employment ends, you will probably get fewer EI payments." When you apply, "you have to answer questions on a special form if you quit or were fired"—for example, why you quit or were fired, whether you tried to fix the problem, and whether you tried to get other work—after which "Service Canada may interview you" and "may also contact your employer" before deciding whether you quit without just cause or were fired for misconduct. "Just cause" for quitting means "you had to quit because you had no other reasonable choice," with examples including discrimination, an employer or supervisor who "was unfair or hostile to you for no good reason" or "was doing something that was against the law," dangerous conditions, or significant wage cuts, though "even if one of the examples applies to you, that might not be enough to be just cause." For fired employees, "misconduct usually means doing something wrong on purpose" and is "more than not being able to do the job well," with examples including "threatening someone or being violent," "destroying company property on purpose," "being away from work without permission," and "not obeying an order from your employer." "Service Canada decides if you were fired for misconduct, not your employer. So you should apply, even if you were fired." If Service Canada disqualifies you, "they will tell you in a letter that you cannot get regular EI benefits," and the letter "will also tell you that you have the right to ask them to reconsider their decision," which "you must ask for this in writing within 30 days of finding out about Service Canada's decision."
