

Based on `doc.txt`, the negatives of the near-zero biodiversity in European coffee plantations (all descended from the same Arabian mother plant) were:

1. **Devastating vulnerability to disease**: In the last decades of the nineteenth century, the fungal pathogen *Haemilia vestatrix* severely infected coffee plantations in Sri Lanka, India, Java, Sumatra, and Malaysia. Because the plants were essentially clones, the rust disease "destroyed the coffee plantations one by one."

2. **Loss of plantations and forced conversion**: As a consequence of the rust disease, some plantations had to be converted into tea plantations (as in the case of Sri Lanka), while others had to be replanted with *Coffea canephora* (syn. *Coffea robusta*), a different species with natural resistance to rust.

In short, the lack of genetic diversity meant that a single pathogen could wipe out plantations across multiple regions, forcing their abandonment or conversion to other crops.
