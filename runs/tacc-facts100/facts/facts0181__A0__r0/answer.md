

Based solely on `doc.txt`, the government's strategies to encourage compliance in the tax system are set out mainly in Chapter 2, "Tackling non-compliance." The document states that since 2010 the government has introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC, securing and protecting over £250 billion that would otherwise have gone unpaid, with the overarching aim that "everyone [pays] the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are." The specific strategies include:

**1. Anti-avoidance and anti-evasion legislation (2.1)**
Significant past reforms that form the base for current measures include the General Anti-Abuse Rule, Accelerated Payments, implementation of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action Plan, and a range of measures to clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance.

**2. Modernising tax debt collection (2.2)**
A call for evidence on how HMRC can modernise collection of tax debts to reflect the changing economy, including e-commerce and businesses operating in the UK without a physical presence or assets. It also seeks views on HMRC's approach to the small minority of business taxpayers who do not engage with HMRC and delay paying, forcing costly enforcement action. The proposals explicitly do not affect taxpayers in temporary financial difficulty (including those affected by the pandemic) who need support to get back on a sustainable footing.

**3. Mandatory Disclosure Rules (2.3)**
Implementing the OECD's Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules (announced at Spring Budget 2021), with a technical consultation and draft regulations issued. These rules require advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) to report information to tax authorities on certain prescribed arrangements and structures, including those that could circumvent the Common Reporting Standard. The information is exchanged with relevant tax authorities to deter non-compliance, help identify and challenge evasion, and support tax policy development.

**4. Transfer pricing documentation (2.4)**
Legislation in 2022 (effective April 2023) requiring businesses in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD BEPS Action 13, plus a supporting summary audit trail. The government will continue reviewing the case for an International Dealings Schedule.

**5. Tax conditionality on licences (2.5)**
Extending tax conditionality to certain licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland from April 2023 (consistent with reforms in England and Wales from April 2022). Renewal of certain licences becomes conditional on applicants completing checks confirming they are appropriately registered for tax; licensing bodies must obtain this confirmation before deciding on renewal, making it harder for traders to operate in the hidden economy. The government remains committed to exploring wider application of tax conditionality.

**6. Strengthening ISA compliance and penalties (2.6)**
A call for evidence on proposals to enhance and modernise compliance for all types of ISA (Cash, Stocks and Shares, Junior, Lifetime and Innovative Finance), following the Treasury's review of checks on Innovative Finance ISA managers and the penalties regime, to encourage ISA managers and investors to "get things right first time."

**7. Making SDLT reliefs fairer (2.7)**
A consultation on Stamp Duty Land Tax reliefs for purchases of mixed property and multiple dwellings, which are being exploited by some purchasers to unfairly reduce their tax liability. Potential changes aim to make the system fairer and reduce the scope for misuse and incorrect claims.

**8. Tackling offshore non-compliance (2.8)**
Establishing an HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents to explore ideas for tackling offshore tax non-compliance, following two spring 2021 discussion documents under HMRC's "No Safe Havens" strategy. These sought views on reducing offshore non-compliance in relation to non-UK income, gains and assets, and on reducing and preventing international tax debt.

**9. Exploring VAT split payment (2.9)**
Exploring VAT split payment, an alternative method of VAT collection where the tax element of a digital payment could be remitted directly to HMRC. Though at an exploratory stage, the government will continue assessing its potential for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation, working collaboratively with the payments industry through the Industry Working Group established in 2018.

**10. Transparency on marketed avoidance schemes (2.10)**
HMRC publishes an annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes in the UK, setting out the numbers and characteristics of taxpayers using them (occupations, locations, ages, and average declared incomes), as in the report for tax year 2019–2020.

In addition, Chapter 1 of the document shows the government supporting compliance indirectly through simplification: publishing its first five-year review of the Office of Tax Simplification (OTS) with eight recommendations, accepting five of the OTS's technical recommendations on Capital Gains Tax to keep the system simple and efficient, and keeping CGT under review (while deciding not to proceed with Inheritance Tax design changes at this time).
