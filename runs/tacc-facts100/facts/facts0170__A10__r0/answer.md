

According to `doc.txt`, the difference lies in how they are used:

- **Methods** are "special functions called string methods" that come bundled with strings. They are written with a leading dot (e.g., `.lower()`), and "The dot (.) tells Python that what follows is the name of a method." They "must be used in conjunction with a string. They do not exist independently."

- **Functions** like `len()` are "a stand-alone function" that you "call the len() function directly" (e.g., `len(name)`), without needing to attach it to a string.

The document states: "Aside from the different results of these functions, the important distinction here is how they're used."
