**Overall severity**
- Bacterial meningitis is described as "a rare but potentially fatal disease" that can cause stroke, hearing loss, and permanent brain damage.
- Both meningitis and encephalitis "can progress quickly and have the potential to cause severe, irreversible neurological damage," so suspected cases should receive immediate, aggressive medical treatment.

**By type**
- **Pneumococcal meningitis** (caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae*) is the most common and most serious form of bacterial meningitis, with about 6,000 U.S. cases per year. Survivors often suffer neurological damage ranging from deafness to severe brain damage. Children under 12 and adults with weakened immune systems are at particular risk.
- **Meningococcal meningitis** (caused by *Neisseria meningitidis*) is highly contagious, with about 2,600 U.S. cases per year. Between 10–15% of cases are fatal, and another 10–15% cause brain damage and other serious side effects. High-risk groups include infants under 1, people with suppressed immune systems, travelers to endemic areas, and military recruits/dormitory residents.
- **Haemophilus influenzae meningitis** was once the most common form, but the Hib vaccine has greatly reduced cases in the U.S.
- **Other bacterial forms** include Listeria, E. coli (most common in elderly adults and newborns), and tuberculosis meningitis (rare).
- **Viral (aseptic) meningitis**, usually caused by enteroviruses, is "rarely life threatening and no specific treatment is needed."
- **Fungal meningitis** (most commonly *Cryptococcus neoformans*) can smolder for weeks; although treatable, it often recurs in nearly half of affected persons.
- **Amoebic meningitis** is rare but "can be rapidly fatal."

**Prognosis and complications**
- Outcome depends on the infectious agent, severity of illness, and how quickly treatment is given.
- In most cases, people with very mild meningitis can make a full recovery; those with only headache, fever, and stiff neck may recover in 2–4 weeks.
- Bacterial meningitis patients typically show relief 48–72 hours after initial treatment but are more likely to experience complications.
- In more serious cases, meningitis can cause hearing and/or speech loss, blindness, permanent brain and nerve damage, behavioral changes, cognitive disabilities, lack of muscle control, seizures, and memory loss, and affected individuals may need long-term therapy, medication, and supportive care.

**Mitigating factors**
- Appropriate antibiotic treatment for most types of meningitis can greatly reduce the risk of dying from the disease.
- Effective vaccines are available to prevent Haemophilus influenzae, pneumococcal, and meningococcal meningitis, and preventative antibiotics are recommended for close contacts of someone diagnosed with meningococcal meningitis.
