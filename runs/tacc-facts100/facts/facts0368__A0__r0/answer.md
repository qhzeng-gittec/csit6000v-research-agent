

Based solely on `doc.txt` ("Gaming PC vs. Console: Which Should You Buy in 2024?"), here's how the comparison breaks down:

## Raw graphical performance: PC wins
- **Consoles:** The PS5 and Xbox Series X can *technically* output 8K at 120 FPS, but the article calls that "marketing bullshit." In practice, the most graphically demanding games are "lucky to upscale to 4K at 60 FPS," and many titles include a 30 FPS "graphics"/"visuals" mode.
- **PC:** 60 FPS is considered the bare minimum, with 120+ FPS preferable. Native resolution beyond 4K is possible — e.g., the Samsung Neo G9 57-inch super-ultrawide at 7,680 x 2,160, which is only playable at native resolution on a PC.
- **Quality presets:** Console versions of games generally run at settings similar to the "Medium" or "High" preset of the PC release.
- **Ray tracing (relevant to your Cyberpunk interest):** This is "another win for the PC." Consoles can handle ray tracing, but game support is "underwhelming" — even big next-gen exclusives like Starfield lack it. Cross-platform titles that do support it, **like Cyberpunk 2077, usually stick to a quality level a notch or two below the maximum available on PC.**
- **Caveats for PC:** Nvidia's tendency to limit video memory is catching up to cards with less than 16 GB of VRAM (the amount the PS5 and Xbox Series X have), and some PC ports have optimization problems. The article concludes that PC beats console on both framerate and visual quality, and that "this gap will only increase in the coming years as the current console generation ages relative to new PC graphics cards."

## Game availability: PC wins
- Consoles have a "remarkable" library — most games are cross-platform, and PS5/Xbox Series X|S backwards compatibility boosts each library into the thousands.
- But compared to PC, "those numbers look absolutely adorable." The PC library is estimated at **over 100,000 titles** (over 12,000 games were released to Steam in 2022 alone), and a PC can also emulate most older game libraries and, in some cases, even modern games.

## Price point for a solid PC
- A **mid-range, off-the-shelf desktop or gaming laptop** with performance comparable to an Xbox Series X or PS5: **$1,500–$2,000** (basic Dell/HP desktops at the lower end; boutique builders like Origin PC or Digital Storm at the higher end).
- **DIY route:** You can build a "console-slaying" desktop for **under $1,500** with an AMD Ryzen 5600X and a budget GPU like a Radeon RX 7800 XT.
- Warning: a PC priced like an Xbox Series X or PS5 will have an outdated graphics card that can't handle games designed for modern consoles, and PCs at entry-console prices usually lack a proper GPU entirely.

**Bottom line for your use case:** If you prioritize high-quality visuals, ray tracing, and high FPS in games like Cyberpunk 2077, the document clearly sides with PC — at a cost of roughly $1,500–$2,000 for a prebuilt, or under $1,500 if you build it yourself.
