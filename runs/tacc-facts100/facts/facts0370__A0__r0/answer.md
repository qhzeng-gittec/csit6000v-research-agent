

To apply for EI after quitting or being fired, you should apply right away even if you don't yet have your Record of Employment (ROE), though you need an ROE for every employer you worked for in the past 12 months (if your employer sends it online, you don't need a copy, but you'll need proof of employment such as pay stubs and T4 slips), and if you don't apply within 4 weeks after your employment ends you will probably get fewer EI payments. When you apply, you must answer questions on a special form: if you quit, you must say why you quit, whether you tried to fix the problem at work before leaving, and whether you tried to get other work before quitting; if you were fired, you must say why you were fired, whether your employer gave you warnings, and whether you tried to fix the problem. Service Canada may then interview you and contact your employer before deciding whether you quit without just cause or were fired for misconduct. "Just cause" for quitting means you had to quit because you had no other reasonable choice, and examples include being discriminated against, an employer or supervisor who was unfair, hostile, or did something against the law, being pressured to leave, dangerous working conditions, or significant wage cuts or major changes to your duties. For fired employees, misconduct usually means doing something wrong on purpose—more than not being able to do the job well—and examples include threatening or being violent, deliberately destroying company property, being away from work without permission, or not obeying an employer's order; Service Canada (not your employer) decides whether you were fired for misconduct, so you should apply even if you were fired. If Service Canada disqualifies you, they will send a letter saying you cannot get regular EI benefits, and that letter tells you you have the right to ask them to reconsider their decision, which you must request in writing within 30 days of finding out about the decision.
