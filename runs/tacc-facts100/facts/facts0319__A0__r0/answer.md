

Based on `doc.txt`, the challenges to Hokkaido's economy described in the article are:

1. **Unbalanced industrial structure** – Hokkaido's economy is still biased toward natural resources supply industry. Manufacturing has not developed enough: its share of total Hokkaido GDP is less than 10%, compared with more than 20% of the Japanese average. Within manufacturing, food processing dominated in 2005, and the share of assembly industry is only 12%, compared with 48% in the Japanese average.

2. **Over-reliance on infrastructure investment** – The basic development strategy has been characterized by heavy infrastructure investment, which developed an excessive construction industry that now requires continuous infrastructure investment. This situation has not provided good conditions for diversifying the industrial structure.

3. **Decreasing natural resources** – Hokkaido faces declining natural forestry and fishery resources.

4. **Decline of the coal industry** – Since the 1960s, the energy revolution and the shift from coal to oil caused the coal industry to decline. Hokkaido once produced 20 million tons of coal annually, but the last coal mine closed in 2002. Areas that depended on coal mining were greatly affected and still struggle with economic decline and collapsing communities; there are many impoverishing areas that used to depend on coal mining.

5. **Damage to the fishery industry** – The introduction of the 200-mile economic zone system internationally in 1976 greatly damaged Japanese northern sea fishery, which had been a main profit source for Hokkaido's fishery industry.

6. **Severe global competition in agriculture** – Although Hokkaido ranks No.1 in Japan for producing many agricultural products, global competition has become severe, requiring efforts to differentiate its products from foreign and other regions' products.

7. **Economic recession and fiscal strain** – Under the long-lasting economic recession, Hokkaido's regional economy has been weakened, and the financial condition of many local self-governments is facing very critical conditions.
