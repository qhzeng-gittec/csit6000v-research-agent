

# New OTC Eczema Probiotic Treatment — Key Features

**The Treatment**
- A new over-the-counter topical eczema probiotic, called **Defensin**, formulated by Skinesa, is now available.
- It is based on **Roseomonas mucosa (R. mucosa)**, a commensal bacterium that occurs naturally on healthy skin as part of the typical skin microbiome.
- The product is the result of **seven years of research** at NIAID's Laboratory of Clinical Immunology and Microbiology (LCIM), part of the National Institutes of Health.
- It is intended to simplify or complement current eczema management, when used in consultation with a healthcare provider.

**What the Probiotic Does for the Skin**
- R. mucosa **reduces eczema-related skin inflammation** in both adults and children.
- It **enhances the skin's natural barrier function**, which normally retains moisture and keeps out allergens.
- It **helps restore skin lipids (oils)** that people with eczema are deficient in, helping correct microbiome imbalances.
- By strengthening the barrier, it may reduce vulnerability to bacterial, viral, and fungal skin infections.

**Clinical Study Results**
- In Phase 1/2 open-label and Phase 2 blinded, placebo-controlled studies, **most people experienced greater than 75% improvement in eczema severity** after applying R. mucosa.
- Improvement was seen on **all treated skin sites**, including inner elbows, inner knees, hands, trunk, and neck.
- Most participants **needed fewer corticosteroids**, experienced **less itching**, and reported a **better quality of life**.
- Benefits **persisted after treatment ended** — therapeutic R. mucosa strains remained on the skin for **up to eight months** in participants observed that long.

**Background on Eczema**
- Eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition affecting about **20% of children and 10% of adults** worldwide.
- It causes dry, itchy skin that compromises the skin barrier, increasing infection risk.

**Future Plans**
- NIAID will conduct an **additional clinical trial** to generate further evidence on R. mucosa's efficacy.
- Those data could support an **FDA application** to have the product regulated as a **nonprescription drug**, making it accessible to a broader population.
- **Study results are expected in 2024.**

**Research Team**
- The work was led by **Dr. Ian Myles**, chief of the LCIM Epithelial Research Unit, who and his colleagues isolated and cultured R. mucosa, conducted preclinical (laboratory/animal) and clinical (human) studies, and made the bacteria available for commercial, non-therapeutic development.
