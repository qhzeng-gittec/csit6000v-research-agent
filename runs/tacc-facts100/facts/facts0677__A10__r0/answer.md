

The document describes two mergers/acquisitions and their consequences:

**Amazon's acquisition of Whole Foods Market (2017, ~$13.2 billion)**

- Integration of offline and online services: "By acquiring Whole Foods Market, Amazon obtained brick-and-mortar grocery store locations that it was able to integrate with its online services" (doc.txt). Specifically, "shoppers with an Amazon Prime membership52 are eligible for discounts and free pickup or delivery of Whole Foods Market groceries in selected zip codes,53 and Amazon Hub Lockers—where consumers can pick up products purchased on Amazon's website—are often located in Whole Foods Markets.54" (doc.txt).
- Increased competitive pressure on grocery retailers: "Amazon's acquisition of Whole Foods Market may have increased competition in the grocery retail market" (doc.txt), and "other grocery retailers have responded by implementing changes in response to competitive pressure from Amazon" (doc.txt).
- Rival online delivery services: "Competitive pressure from Amazon may have incentivized other grocery retailers to start offering online delivery services" (doc.txt) — Walmart launched one in 2017, Kroger in 2018, and Walmart+ in 2020. "Consumers may have benefited from food retailers offering their own online delivery services, particularly as many of these stores offer free delivery on orders over $35" (doc.txt).
- Pressure on third-party services: "These changes may have also increased pressure on other online grocery delivery services, such as Instacart...; the service launched in 2012 and stopped delivering groceries from Whole Foods in 2019" (doc.txt).
- Growing dominance in e-commerce: the acquisition "raised concern about its growing dominance in the retail industry, particularly in e-commerce" (doc.txt); Amazon "had the greatest share of e-commerce sales at 38.7% in 2020" (doc.txt).
- Data and bargaining power: "Through its acquisition of Whole Foods, Amazon gained access to additional consumer data, strengthening its bargaining power with suppliers" (doc.txt).
- Vertical integration: Amazon "has integrated vertically, such as by offering products under its private label AmazonBasics and by creating its own delivery system," investing "$60 billion since 2014 in its delivery network" (doc.txt). "By integrating vertically, Amazon may be able to further strengthen its position in e-commerce; if, for example, it is able to provide faster delivery,67 consumers could benefit even if it becomes more difficult for other companies to compete." (doc.txt).

**Facebook's acquisition of Instagram (2012, $1 billion)**

- Legal consequences: the FTC initially "closed the investigation without taking action" (doc.txt), but on December 9, 2020, "the FTC filed a lawsuit against Facebook, alleging that "Facebook has maintained its monopoly position by buying up companies that present competitive threats,"" (doc.txt), and a coalition of 46 state attorneys general "filed a parallel lawsuit against Facebook, also alleging that Facebook acquired companies to eliminate competitive threats" (doc.txt). "Both lawsuits72 specifically mention Facebook's acquisitions of Instagram and WhatsApp," (doc.txt).
- Intent to eliminate a competitor: Zuckerberg's internal email — "Instagram has become a large and viable competitor to us on mobile photos, which will increasingly be the future of photos" — "has been used to support the claim that Facebook acquired Instagram with the intention of eliminating a potential competitor" (doc.txt).
- Instagram's growth and profitability: Instagram "grew rapidly thereafter, from about 100 million monthly active users (MAUs) in February 2013 to 500 million MAUs in June 2016 and 1 billion MAUs in June 2018" (doc.txt), and "was able to use Facebook's resources, such as its advertising services and its infrastructure...These have been key to the profitability of Instagram" (doc.txt).
- Counterfactual: "It is possible that without the merger, Instagram would have been among the platforms that have struggled to compete in digital markets because of resource constraints" (doc.txt), as happened with Friendster, which "eventually closed down" (doc.txt).
