

**Introduction**
- *Staphylococcus aureus* is a major bacterial human pathogen causing a wide variety of clinical manifestations.
- Infections occur in both community-acquired and hospital-acquired settings; treatment is challenging due to multi-drug resistant strains such as MRSA.
- *S. aureus* is found in the environment and as normal human flora on skin and mucous membranes (most often the nasal area) of most healthy individuals.
- It does not normally infect healthy skin, but if it enters the bloodstream or internal tissues, it can cause potentially serious infections.
- Transmission is typically by direct contact, though some infections involve other methods.

**Epidemiology**
- *S. aureus* (including MRSA) is found on skin and mucous membranes; humans are the major reservoir.
- Up to half of all adults are colonized; ~15% of the population persistently carry it in the anterior nares.
- Colonization rates can reach up to 80% in certain groups: health care workers, regular needle users (diabetics, IV drug users), hospitalized patients, and immunocompromised individuals.
- Transmission occurs person-to-person via direct contact or fomites.
