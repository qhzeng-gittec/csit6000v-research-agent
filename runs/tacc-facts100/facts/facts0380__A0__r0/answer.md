

Based solely on `doc.txt`, here are four customer-awareness strategies your new loss prevention manager can implement quickly (all are low-cost, signage/positioning-based, and can be done before you open):

**1. Put up anti-theft signs around the store**
The document states that "signage around your store can help keep losses at a minimum" — these are "small reminders for potential shoplifters that tell them not to steal from your store," and they "can help deter people who don't want to pay for items, especially if they know they are on camera." Since you already have three CCTV cameras, pair the signs with that fact.

**2. Post signs reminding customers of your security policy / code of conduct**
The document notes that over 93% of retailers have a security policy or "code of conduct," and that "to remind customers about the rules, you can also post signs around the store." For customers, the document says the policy may include:
- Guidelines for respecting other customers and employees
- Directions for reporting potential theft to store staff
- Rules against stealing or damaging items

**3. Use your CCTV as a visible deterrent at your entry/exit**
The document explains that CCTV "can watch entry points into the store, like the customer entrance," and that "using CCTV also acts as a deterrent for potential thieves. It gives the appearance of strong security and shows you take losses seriously." With one entry and exit and three cameras, position them to cover that single entry/exit point so customers see the surveillance.

**4. Use conspicuous surveillance with a "You're being monitored" reminder**
The document describes Target's approach to anti-shoplifting: placing a camera "right at eye level" so the customer sees "your own face staring back at you," with a reminder that "'You're being monitored.'" This is a customer-awareness tactic that makes shoppers aware they are being watched, and it can be done with your existing cameras.

A note from the document: it also recommends training employees on the security policy (meetings/trainings) and using your POS sales and inventory reports to identify trends in missing items, gift card sales, and returns — but those are staff-facing and analytical rather than customer-awareness strategies, so I've kept the four above as the quick, customer-facing actions you can have in place before opening.
