

Based on `doc.txt` (a scoping review of clinical prediction models to diagnose neonatal sepsis in low- and middle-income countries), here is a summary of the predictors used in the included models:

**Overall predictor counts**
- A total of **135 predictors** of sepsis were included across all 44 distinct models.
- **82 (61%)** were clinical parameters (signs, symptoms or risk factors) and **53 (39%)** were laboratory parameters.
- The **median number of predictors per model was 6** (range 2 to 110, IQR 4).

**Composition of models by predictor type**
- **14 models (32%)** included only clinical parameters.
- **12 models (27%)** included only laboratory parameters.
- **18 models (41%)** included both clinical and laboratory parameters.

**Most common laboratory parameters**
- White cell count: **17 models (39%)**
- C-reactive protein (CRP): **16 models (36%)**
- Platelet count: **15 models (34%)**

**Most common clinical parameters**
- Neonatal fever: **13 models (30%)**
- Gestational age: **11 models (25%)**

**Model development context (related to predictor selection)**
- Most models were developed using logistic regression (**16 models, 36%**, often with stepwise selection to select predictors) or consisted of a scoring system based on univariable predictor performance or literature review and expert opinion (**10 models, 23%**).

Note: the document states that the full list of predictors is in the supplementary appendix, so the specific predictors named above are only the most common ones reported in the main text.
