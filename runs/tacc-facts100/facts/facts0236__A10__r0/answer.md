

Based on `doc.txt`, having lupus complicates pregnancy and the health of the baby for several reasons:

**The disease itself**
- "Lupus is a chronic condition that results from a malfunctioning immune system." (doc.txt)
- "in the case of lupus, your immune system mistakenly attacks one or many different types of tissue in the body, such as the skin, joints, muscles, nerves, kidneys, heart or lungs. The result of this damage is ongoing inflammation and pain." (doc.txt)
- "All pregnancies will need careful medical monitoring because of the risk of complications." (doc.txt)

**Problems that can occur during pregnancy**
The document lists that "Some of the problems that can occur during pregnancy for women with lupus include:" (doc.txt):
- "flares of your lupus symptoms may occur during pregnancy or immediately after you deliver" (doc.txt)
- "high blood pressure (hypertension)" (doc.txt)
- "your baby may be born with low birth weight" (doc.txt)
- "pre-eclampsia – symptoms include high blood pressure and excessive amounts of protein lost through your urine" (doc.txt)
- "premature labour" (doc.txt)
- "increased risk of blood clots in the legs or lungs" (doc.txt)
- "increased risk of miscarriage" (doc.txt)
- "increased risk of emergency caesarean section" (doc.txt)
- "increased risk of excessive bleeding after delivery." (doc.txt)

**Antibodies affecting the placenta and the baby**
- "About one third of women with lupus have antibodies that may cause blood clots and interfere with the proper functioning of the placenta." (doc.txt)
- "The placenta isn't able to supply the baby with sufficient nourishment and the baby's growth is slowed. This may require early delivery via caesarean section." (doc.txt)
- "Around one third of women with lupus have antibodies that may cause lupus-like symptoms in their baby once it's born. This is known as neonatal lupus. Symptoms may include skin rash, unusual blood count and, rarely, heartbeat irregularities." (doc.txt)

**Pre-eclampsia risk**
- "Pre-eclampsia is a condition that involves increased blood pressure, fluid retention and protein in the urine. It occurs in one in five women with lupus. If left untreated it can endanger the life of both the woman and her baby." (doc.txt)

**Medication concerns**
- "Some medication taken for lupus can cross the placenta and pose a threat to your baby." (doc.txt)
- "Some medications are safe to take while you're pregnant however others, like methotrexate, shouldn't be taken. You may need to stop taking some medications months before trying to get pregnant as they can be harmful to your baby." (doc.txt)

**Monitoring difficulty**
- "Sometimes, it can be difficult to distinguish between a lupus flare and normal pregnancy symptoms." (doc.txt)

The document also notes that "Most women with lupus are able to have a healthy baby" (doc.txt), and that planning, stable condition before conception, and close medical care help reduce these risks.
