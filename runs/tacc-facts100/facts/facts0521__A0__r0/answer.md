**The Internet** is the underlying network infrastructure. According to the document:
- It began in 1969 as ARPANET, a U.S. Department of Defense project designed to be a decentralized nationwide computer network that could withstand major disasters.
- It is defined as "a collection of computer networks," where a network is a group of two or more computers connected together with cables that allow them to share information.
- Computers "on the Internet" all use the same protocols to send information back and forth, so they can communicate regardless of their hardware or software.
- It is decentralized, made up of independently owned and managed networks of all sizes, with large high-speed networks called backbone providers and ISPs selling connections to consumers.
- It offers many services, including e-mail, Telnet, FTP, Usenet, IRC, and the World Wide Web.

**The World Wide Web** is one service that runs *on* the Internet, not the Internet itself:
- It was invented in 1989 by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in Geneva, Switzerland, to make research information on the Internet more organized and accessible.
- It is based on hypertext — a method of linking documents using embedded hyperlinks (which can be underlined/colored text or graphics).
- Web documents are written in HTML, stored on computers called servers, and retrieved by any Internet user with a Web browser, which communicates with servers using the HTTP protocol.
- Its documents are called Web pages, organized into Web sites, each with its own address (URL).
- Its graphical, "point-and-click" user interface is cited as a major reason for the Internet's growth explosion.

**In short:** the Internet is the global collection of interconnected computer networks that communicate using common protocols, while the World Wide Web is a hypertext-based service — pages of HTML documents linked by hyperlinks and accessed through browsers via HTTP — that operates on top of the Internet, alongside other services like e-mail, FTP, and Telnet.
