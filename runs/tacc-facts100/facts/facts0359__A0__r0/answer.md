

Based on `doc.txt`, the three vehicle types differ as follows:

**Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs)**
- Powered by an internal combustion engine (running on conventional or alternative fuel) *plus* an electric motor that uses energy stored in a battery.
- Do **not** require a plug to charge the battery; instead they charge via regenerative braking (using the electric motor as a generator) and the internal combustion engine.
- The electric motor's extra power allows a smaller engine, giving better fuel economy without sacrificing performance, while retaining the power and range of conventional vehicles.

**Plug-In Hybrid Electric Vehicles (PHEVs)**
- Also powered by conventional fuels and a battery-powered electric motor, but they have **larger battery packs than HEVs**, which makes it possible to drive using only electric power — an "all-electric range" of about 10 to 40 miles in current models.
- Batteries can be charged several ways: by an outside electric power source (plugging in), by the internal combustion engine, or through regenerative braking.
- If never plugged in, a PHEV's fuel economy is about the same as a similarly sized HEV; if fully charged and driven a distance shorter than its all-electric range, it can run on electric power only.

**All-Electric Vehicles (EVs)**
- Use **only** a battery to store the electrical energy that powers the motor — they use no other fuel at all, which helps reduce petroleum consumption.
- Batteries are charged solely by plugging the vehicle into an electric power source.
- The EPA considers EVs zero-emission vehicles because their motors produce no exhaust or emissions (though electricity production may contribute to air pollution).
- They have a shorter range per charge than most conventional vehicles per tank of gas; manufacturers typically target a minimum range of 100 miles, which the Federal Highway Administration says is sufficient for more than 90% of U.S. household vehicle trips.

**In short:** HEVs have no plug-in charging and rely on regenerative braking plus the engine to charge; PHEVs are like HEVs but with larger batteries that can be plugged in and support a short all-electric range; EVs have no internal combustion engine or other fuel at all, relying entirely on a plug-charged battery.
