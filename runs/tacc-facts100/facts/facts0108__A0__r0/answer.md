## Steps to Apoptosis
Apoptosis (programmed cell death, or "cellular suicide") is triggered by self-generated signals within a cell and is mediated by caspases — enzymes found in all cells that cleave specific proteins to initiate cell death. The process occurs in a gentle and controlled manner with no negative impact on surrounding cells:

1. The cell expels moisture in order to dry out and condense until fragmentation occurs.
2. Because the cell does not release its contents into the extracellular environment, there are no morphological changes.
3. Apoptotic bodies — small vesicles — form to transport the contents of the cell elsewhere, enabling the cell to die gently without causing inflammation.
4. Apoptosis is signaled by chromatin condensation in the cell's nucleus, which allows scientists to determine whether a cell is dying naturally or unnaturally.

## Differentiating Factors of Necrosis vs. Apoptosis
- **Nature of the process:** Apoptosis is a naturally occurring, controlled and timely process, making it predictable and beneficial to the host. Necrosis is a pathological process, often unregulated and caused by toxins, infections, or traumas.
- **Process:** In apoptosis, the cytoplasm shrinks, resulting in condensation of the nucleus. In necrosis, the cytoplasm and mitochondria swell, resulting in cell lysis or a rupture in the cell membrane.
- **Membrane integrity:** Blebbing (cytoskeleton degradation and the membrane bulging outward) is a defining feature of apoptosis and has no effect on the membrane's integrity. In necrosis, membrane integrity is loosened and significantly reduced, if not completely lost.
- **Organelle behavior:** Organelles can continue to function even after a cell has died due to apoptosis. During necrosis, organelles swell and disintegrate and are no longer functional.
- **Scope of affected cells:** Apoptosis is localized, destroying only individual or single cells. Necrosis can spread to affect adjacent cell groups, causing damage beyond the initial site.
- **Bodily influence:** Apoptosis controls cell numbers and is frequently beneficial (though abnormal in either direction can cause disease). Necrosis is always harmful and, if left untreated, can be fatal.
- **In essence:** Apoptosis is planned cell death in which the cell actively destroys itself to maintain body functionality; necrosis is unintentional/unplanned cell death caused by the cell's external environment, which impedes or interferes with body functions or health.

## Ways a Cell Can Be Affected by Necrosis
Necrosis is caused by external factors influencing cell physiology. The document lists these causes of necrotic cell death:
- **Bacterial infection** – bacteria entering through an airway or open wound can cause illnesses linked to unplanned cell death.
- **Fungal infection (mycosis)** – a fungus infiltrating human tissues can result in skin or internal system diseases, as well as unprogrammed cell death.
- **Pancreatitis** – inflammation of the pancreas, a gland that helps regulate hormones and digestion.
- **Protein denaturation** – weak bonds in proteins break down, causing the cells' ability to function properly to suffer.

Additionally, the document notes that in cell samples, the longer a cell is kept outside its preferred environment, the more likely it will die from necrosis; cells can swell and die as a result of external factors, and dead cells can clump and cause more cell death in the surrounding population when using traditional cell separation methods such as magnetic-based cell sorting.
