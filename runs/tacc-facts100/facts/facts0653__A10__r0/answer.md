**Lawrence G. Roberts**

- Roberts was an MIT researcher whom J.C.R. Licklider, the first head of the computer research program at DARPA, "convinced ... of the importance of this networking concept" (doc.txt).
- He was persuaded by Kleinrock that packet-based communication was viable: "Kleinrock convinced Roberts of the theoretical feasibility of communications using packets rather than circuits, which was a major step along the path towards computer networking" (doc.txt).
- In 1965, Roberts built the first wide-area computer network: "in 1965 working with Thomas Merrill, Roberts connected the TX-2 computer in Mass. to the Q-32 in California with a low speed dial-up telephone line creating the first (however small) wide-area computer network ever built" (doc.txt). The experiment showed that "the circuit switched telephone system was totally inadequate for the job. Kleinrock's conviction of the need for packet switching was confirmed" (doc.txt).
- In late 1966, Roberts moved to DARPA and planned the ARPANET: "In late 1966 Roberts went to DARPA to develop the computer network concept and quickly put together his plan for the "ARPANET", publishing it in 1967." (doc.txt). At the conference where he presented the paper, "Scantlebury told Roberts about the NPL work as well as that of Paul Baran and others at RAND" (doc.txt).
- After the ARPANET design was refined, "In August 1968, after Roberts and the DARPA funded community had refined the overall structure and specifications for the ARPANET, an RFQ was released by DARPA for the development of one of the key components, the packet switches called Interface Message Processors (IMP's)" (doc.txt).
- While BBN built the IMPs, "the network topology and economics were designed and optimized by Roberts working with Howard Frank and his team at Network Analysis Corporation" (doc.txt).
- In July 1972, Roberts advanced the ARPANET's first major application: "In July, Roberts expanded its utility by writing the first email utility program to list, selectively read, file, forward, and respond to messages" (doc.txt).

**Leonard Kleinrock**

- Kleinrock laid the theoretical foundation: "Leonard Kleinrock at MIT published the first paper on packet switching theory in July 1961 and the first book on the subject in 1964" (doc.txt).
- His team at UCLA prepared "the network measurement system" (doc.txt).
- "Due to Kleinrock's early development of packet switching theory and his focus on analysis, design and measurement, his Network Measurement Center at UCLA was selected to be the first node on the ARPANET" (doc.txt).
- The first node came online in September 1969: "All this came together in September 1969 when BBN installed the first IMP at UCLA and the first host computer was connected" (doc.txt).
- Kleinrock's laboratory sent the network's first host-to-host message: "One month later, when SRI was connected to the ARPANET, the first host-to-host message was sent from Kleinrock's laboratory to SRI" (doc.txt).

Together, the document presents Kleinrock as the source of the packet-switching theory and measurement work, and Roberts as the engineer and planner who built the first wide-area network experiment, designed the ARPANET at DARPA, and optimized its topology and economics.
