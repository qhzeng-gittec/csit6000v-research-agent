

# Summary of the Annual Report (doc.txt)

The document is an excerpt from the Annual Report on Form 10-K of **Timberland Bancorp, Inc.**, a Washington corporation and the holding company for **Timberland Bank**, for the fiscal year ended September 30, 2022.

## Overview
- The Bank opened in 1915 and serves consumers and businesses across Grays Harbor, Thurston, Pierce, King, Kitsap, and Lewis counties in Washington through 23 branches (main office in Hoquiam).
- At September 30, 2022, the Company had total assets of $1.86 billion, net loans receivable of $1.13 billion, total deposits of $1.63 billion, and total shareholders' equity of $218.57 million.
- The holding company's activities are limited to passive investment and oversight of the Bank, so the report focuses on the Bank's operations. The Bank is community-oriented, offering savings products and concentrating on real estate-secured lending (residential construction, one- to four-family, multi-family, and commercial real estate), adjustable-rate residential mortgages (some not eligible for secondary-market sale), commercial business loans, and consumer loans.

## Profitability Drivers
- Profitability depends primarily on **net interest income** (interest earned on loans and investments minus interest paid on deposits and borrowings) after provision for loan losses. Management aims to keep the net interest margin in the top quartile of Washington State peers.
- Since March 2022, the FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 300 basis points (to 3.00%–3.25% by September 30, 2022), then another 75 basis points in November 2022 (to 3.75%–4.00%). The Company believes its balance sheet is positioned to benefit from variable-rate asset repricing and a higher net interest margin.
- **Provision for loan losses:** $270,000 for the year ended September 30, 2022 (driven by loan portfolio growth); none for the year ended September 30, 2021 (improving economy and declining forecasted COVID-19-related losses).
- **Non-interest income** (2022) consisted mainly of deposit service charges, gains on loan sales, ATM/debit interchange fees, increased BOLI cash surrender value, escrow fees, and other operating income. **Non-interest expense** consisted mainly of salaries and benefits, premises and equipment, advertising, interchange fees, postage, core deposit intangible amortization, taxes, professional fees, FDIC premiums, loan administration/foreclosure costs, data processing, and deposit operations, with occasional reductions from gains on sales of premises/equipment and OREO.

## Operating Strategy
The goal is to run the Bank as a well-capitalized, profitable, independent, community-oriented institution serving small businesses and individuals in western Washington, by focusing on higher-yielding assets (commercial real estate, construction, commercial business loans), growing core deposits, managing problem assets, controlling expenses, and seeking expansion. Key objectives:
1. **Expand presence** in existing market areas, leveraging bank consolidation and a community-bank emphasis on responsive, personalized service.
2. **Portfolio diversification** — limiting speculative construction and land development loans in favor of lower-risk, higher risk-adjusted-return loans (owner/builder and custom construction, multi-family, commercial business, commercial real estate), plus cross-selling.
3. **Increase core deposits** through total banking relationships and technology products (business cash management, remote deposit).
4. **Manage interest-rate exposure** by retaining ARMs, short-term construction loans, and index-tied commercial loans, while selling longer fixed-rate mortgages into the secondary market.
5. **Mortgage banking revenue** — selling fixed-rate residential mortgages (and completed custom construction/owner-builder loans) into the secondary market with servicing retained.
6. **Maintain strong asset quality** — non-performing loans were 0.18% of net loans at September 30, 2022 (vs. 0.29% a year earlier); non-performing assets were 0.12% of total assets (vs. 0.18%) and fell to $2.17 million from $3.17 million, managed through collections, writedowns, modifications, and OREO sales.
