

Based on `doc.txt`, the three vehicle types—Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs), Plug-In Hybrid Electric Vehicles (PHEVs), and All-Electric Vehicles (EVs)—differ in their power sources, how their batteries are charged, their electric-only capability, and their emissions:

**Power source**
- HEVs: "HEVs are powered by an internal combustion engine or other propulsion source that runs on conventional or alternative fuel and an electric motor that uses energy stored in a battery."
- PHEVs: "PHEVs are powered by conventional fuels and by electrical energy stored in a battery."
- EVs: "EVs use a battery to store the electrical energy that powers the motor" and "Since EVs use no other fuel, they help reduce petroleum consumption."

**Battery charging**
- HEVs: "HEVs do not require a plug to charge the battery; instead, they charge using regenerative braking and the internal combustion engine."
- PHEVs: "PHEV batteries can be charged several ways: by an outside electric power source, by the internal combustion engine, or through regenerative braking."
- EVs: "EV batteries are charged by plugging the vehicle into an electric power source."

**Electric-only driving and range**
- PHEVs: "PHEVs have larger battery packs than HEVs, making it possible to drive using only electric power (about 10 to 40 miles in current models). This is commonly referred to as the all-electric range of the vehicle."
- EVs: "Currently available EVs have a shorter range per charge than most conventional vehicles have per tank of gas. EV manufacturers typically target a minimum range of 100 miles."
- HEVs: the document does not describe an all-electric range for HEVs; instead, "The energy from the battery provides extra power during acceleration and auxiliary power when idling."

**Emissions**
- HEVs: "HEVs combine the benefits of high fuel economy and low emissions with the power and range of conventional vehicles."
- PHEVs: "PHEVs can also reduce emissions, depending on the electricity source."
- EVs: "the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) considers EVs to be zero-emission vehicles because their motors produce no exhaust or emissions."

**Fuel economy**
- HEVs: "The extra power provided by the electric motor allows for a smaller engine, resulting in better fuel economy without sacrificing performance."
- PHEVs: "If a PHEV is never plugged in to charge, its fuel economy will be about the same as that of a similarly sized HEV."
