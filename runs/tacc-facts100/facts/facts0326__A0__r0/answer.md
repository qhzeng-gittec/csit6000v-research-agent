

Based on `doc.txt`, the study examined weight changes among 4.24 million US adults (ages 18–84) from January 2019 through May 2021 using a large electronic health records (EHR) database, comparing the pre-pandemic period (before March 2020) with the pandemic period (after March 2020).

**Outcome:**
- Compared with the pre-pandemic weight trend, there was only a **small increase of 0.1 kg in weight during the first year of the pandemic** (March 2020 through March 2021).
- Weight changes during the pandemic **varied by sex, age, and initial BMI**, but the **largest mean increase across these groups was less than 1.3 kg**.
- Weight increases were **generally greatest among women, adults with a BMI of 30 or 35 kg/m², and younger adults**.

In short, the study found that pandemic-related weight gain in US adults was modest — far smaller than the larger increases (e.g., 13–29 lb) reported in some earlier studies — with the excess weight gain over the pre-pandemic trend being only about 0.1 kg in the first year of the pandemic.
