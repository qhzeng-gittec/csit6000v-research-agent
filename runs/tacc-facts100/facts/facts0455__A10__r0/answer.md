**Overall increase in symptoms and substance use**
- "Data from multiple sources suggest that mental health symptoms and substance use have increased since the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic. These symptoms include emotional distress and anxiety, depression, and trauma-related conditions." (doc.txt)
- "most of the 2020 data suggest an increase in behavioral health morbidity in the United States over the course of the COVID-19 pandemic." (doc.txt)

**Stress**
- A SHADAC survey in April 2020 "found that over 90% of U.S. adults reported experiencing additional levels of stress caused by the COVID-19 pandemic." (doc.txt)
- A Kaiser Family Foundation survey found a rising share of Americans reporting pandemic-related stress affecting their mental health: "In March 2020, 32% of respondents felt that worry or stress related to coronavirus had a negative impact on their mental health. In April 2020 that number rose to 45%, and in July 2020, 53% reported that pandemic-related stress was affecting their mental health." (doc.txt)
- The document notes that without adequate coping strategies, "stress can have detrimental effects on mental health," and that maladaptive coping strategies "may exacerbate problems in the long term." (doc.txt)

**Mental health disorders and emergency department visits**
- "The percentage of Americans experiencing symptoms of a mental health disorder appears to have increased during the COVID19 pandemic," per NCHS data from the Household Pulse Survey. (doc.txt)
- CDC analysis showed that "socioeconomic and psychosocial-related visits increased during April 2020 (compared with April 2019), while total ED visits decreased over 40%." (doc.txt)

**Suicide**
- "approximately twice as many U.S. adults reported serious consideration of suicide in the previous 30 days compared with 2018 (10.7% versus4.3%)." (doc.txt)
- The Disaster Distress Helpline "experienced a 335% increase in calls during the first five months of the pandemic." (doc.txt)
- However, "it appears that suicide mortality has decreased compared with previous years," and "An increase in suicidal thoughts does not necessarily equate to an increase in suicide attempts or suicide deaths." (doc.txt) There were also regional and demographic differences, e.g., "suicide mortality rates increased for Black residents from March 2020 to May 2020, while decreasing for White residents over that same time." (doc.txt)

**Substance use and overdoses**
- ODMAP "reported an increase of 11% in fatal overdoses and a 19% increase in nonfatal overdoses from March through May 2020 compared with the same months in 2019." (doc.txt)
- CDC data "showed the rate of overdose deaths accelerating after the pandemic began," with the rise "particularly notable for deaths involving synthetic opioids." (doc.txt)
- "Put together, the ODMAP and CDC data suggest that drug-related overdoses and overdose deaths have increased during the COVID-19 pandemic." (doc.txt)
- The document also notes that "Individuals with substance use disorders may be at higher risk of contracting SARS-CoV-2" and "may be at higher risk for complications of COVID-19 because substance use can often suppress the immune system or inhibit respiratory functioning." (doc.txt)
