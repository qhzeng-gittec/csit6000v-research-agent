**Important caveats noted in the document:**
- The evidence is "less clear on the benefit of puppy classes specifically." Most studies are **epidemiological (correlational)** in nature, correlating adult dog behavior with demographic, owner, and environmental factors, and there is debate over whether they can establish true causal relationships.
- The studies sometimes show **contradictory results** (e.g., male dogs showed fewer behavioral problems in one study, while desexed females showed less aggression in another), possibly due to sample differences or unaccounted variables.
- Puppy classes may not always help because there are often **no accreditation procedures**, classes may be run by people lacking skills or experience, and puppies may find the experience **too stressful** to benefit — since mild stress is a component of socialization, but too much stress means the benefits are not conferred.

In short, the document concludes that socialization classes appear to correlate with better adult behavior (less fear, aggression, and behavioral problems; more sociability), but they are "one piece of the puzzle" in developing adult dog behavior, and the correlation does not necessarily prove a singular causal effect.
