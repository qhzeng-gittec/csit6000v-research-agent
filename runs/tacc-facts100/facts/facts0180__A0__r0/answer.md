**Sideline evaluation (NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment / "Sideline Survey")**
- **When/why:** Triggered immediately when a player exhibits or reports a sign or symptom of concussion, spinal cord neuropraxia, or a concern is raised by gameday medical personnel. The player is removed to the sideline (or stabilized on the field), his helmet is taken away, and he must undergo the entire survey.
- **Components (minimum):** (i) review of the "No-Go" criteria (loss of consciousness, ataxia, confusion, amnesia); (ii) inquiry into the history of the event (before, during, and after the suspected mechanism of injury); (iii) review of concussion signs and symptoms; (iv) all Maddocks' questions; (v) complete video review of the injury, including discussion with the Booth UNC; and (vi) a focused neurological exam (cervical spine exam, speech evaluation, gait/coordination/balance testing, and eye movements/pupillary exam).
- **Setting and method:** Conducted inside the medical evaluation tent on the sideline (or in the locker room if the Club medical staff elects), performed using the tablet or other technology assigned by the NFL, with completion of each component confirmed on that device.
- **Outcome:** If any element is positive, inconclusive, or suspicious, the player must be escorted to the locker room immediately for the complete Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment. If the Club physician (after consulting the sideline UNC) concludes the player did not sustain a concussion, the player may return to play.

**Locker room evaluation (NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment / "Locker Room Exam")**
- **Nature:** A standardized acute evaluation tool developed by the NFL's Head, Neck and Spine Committee, based on the SCAT 5 (International Concussion in Sport Group, McCrory et al., 2017) and modified for the NFL (Attachment A).
- **Purpose:** Used by Clubs' medical staffs and designated UNCs to evaluate potential concussions during practices and on game day; it can aid diagnosis even with delayed onset of symptoms, and its ongoing use with preseason baseline and post-injury testing provides detailed data on each athlete's injury and recovery course (comparison to baseline improves its value).
- **Content notes:** Its neurocognitive assessment is brief and does not replace a more comprehensive neurological evaluation or formal neurocognitive testing; the modified Balance Error Scoring System (mBESS) is an important, validated component.
- **Documentation:** Clubs must maintain and upload all exams to the EMR, with a copy given to both the player and the team medical staff.
- **Role of physician:** The Club physician, in conjunction with the sideline UNC, is responsible for determining whether the player is diagnosed with a concussion; a player may still have a concussion even if the assessment is "within normal limits" compared to baseline.

**Key differences in summary:** The sideline survey is the immediate, on-the-spot screening (No-Go criteria, history, Maddocks questions, video review, focused neuro exam) done in the sideline tent on NFL-assigned technology, whose main function is to decide whether the player can return to play or must go to the locker room. The locker room exam is the more comprehensive, standardized (SCAT 5-based) acute assessment performed in the locker room, used to diagnose concussion (including delayed-onset cases), track injury and recovery against preseason baseline, and generate documented records uploaded to the EMR.
