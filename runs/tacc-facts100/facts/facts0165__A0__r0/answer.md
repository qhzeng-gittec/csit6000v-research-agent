

Based on the document in `doc.txt`, Amlodipine is a medication in a class called calcium channel blockers. It is used alone or in combination with other medications to treat high blood pressure in adults and children 6 years and older. It is also used to treat certain types of angina (chest pain) and coronary artery disease (narrowing of the blood vessels that supply blood to the heart). Amlodipine lowers blood pressure by relaxing the blood vessels so the heart does not have to pump as hard, and it controls chest pain by increasing the supply of blood to the heart. If taken regularly, it controls chest pain, but it does not stop chest pain once it starts. The document notes that amlodipine helps control high blood pressure, angina, and coronary artery disease, but does not cure these conditions, so patients should continue taking it even if they feel well.

Regarding side effects, the document lists the following symptoms to report to a doctor if severe or persistent: swelling of the hands, feet, ankles, or lower legs; nausea; stomach pain; dizziness or lightheadedness; and muscle stiffness or tremors. It also warns of serious side effects that require immediate medical attention: more frequent or more severe chest pain, and rapid, pounding, or irregular heartbeat. Serious side effects may be reported to the FDA's MedWatch Adverse Event Reporting program.
