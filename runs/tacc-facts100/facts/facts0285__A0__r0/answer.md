**General guidance**
- Talk with your healthcare providers before changing or stopping your medication. Stopping suddenly can cause withdrawal in some people, and it's not known how withdrawal may affect a pregnancy. If you stop, providers may suggest slowly reducing the dose.

**Potential benefits of continuing**
- Taking it as directed is not expected to make it harder to get pregnant or increase the chance of miscarriage.
- Most studies suggest first-trimester use does not increase the chance of birth defects (background risk is 3–5% in any pregnancy). A large study of ADHD stimulants in pregnancy found no increased birth-defect risk with amphetamines.
- A Danish study (limited by grouping all ADHD medications) suggested no increase in neurodevelopmental disorders, like ADHD, in children of people who continued their ADHD medication during pregnancy versus those who stopped.
- Your providers can discuss the benefits of treating your condition versus the risks of untreated illness during pregnancy.

**Potential detriments**
- Data are limited, but use during pregnancy has sometimes been associated with a higher chance of pregnancy-related problems: poor growth (small size and/or small head size), low birth weight (under 5 lbs 8 oz / 2500 g), and preterm delivery (before week 37).
- You may experience side effects such as weight loss from decreased appetite, and changes in heart rate and blood pressure. The sheet recommends monitoring these with your provider to improve outcomes for you and your baby.
- It is not known whether taking it throughout pregnancy could cause withdrawal symptoms in the newborn; this hasn't been well studied.

**Breastfeeding (if relevant later)**
- Amphetamine and dextroamphetamine individually pass into breast milk; effects on infant behavior and brain development are not well studied. No adverse effects were reported in 4 infants (3–10 months old) whose mothers took dextroamphetamine.
- Large doses could lower milk supply in newly breastfeeding people.
- The product label recommends not using the medication while breastfeeding, but the sheet notes the benefit may outweigh possible risks. Watch for baby symptoms like trouble eating, trouble sleeping, or irritability.

The document emphasizes this information doesn't replace medical advice, so discuss your specific situation with your healthcare provider.
