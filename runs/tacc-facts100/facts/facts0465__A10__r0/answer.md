

- **Purpose and scope:** "This report generally provides an overview of Big Tech's current financial offerings, how the existing regulatory framework applies to these operations, and related policy implications." (doc.txt) The section "considers the potential evolution of financial services offered by Big Tech and the attendant policy implications," while noting "the report does not assume that any one is more or less likely or that the companies are considering pursuing any particular strategy." (doc.txt)

- **Investment advisory services:** These "refer to professionals that provide advice on securities to investors and include asset, portfolio, and wealth managers." (doc.txt) "More than 90% of wealth managers believe Big Tech companies will enter the market, with greater than 50% expecting significant disruption if it happens, according to a November 2021 survey." (doc.txt) Fintechs "have entered the investment management area, using machine learning to offer services such as" (doc.txt) robo-advising and retail stock trading. Big Tech companies "would have to register as investment advisors with the Securities and Exchange Commission" and "may also be required to register with the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority if they plan to offer brokerage services." (doc.txt)

- **Insurance:** **Amazon** and **Google** "have partnered with insurance companies, much like they did banks, to offer various insurance products" (doc.txt) "Any intention to push further without relying on a partner would invite regulation at the state level," (doc.txt) since "Insurance companies, unlike banks and securities firms, have been chartered and regulated solely by the states for the past 150 years." (doc.txt)

- **Banking:** "There are few issues at the nexus of technology and finance that generate greater interest and speculation than the prospect of Big Tech's entry into banking." (doc.txt) The "Plex" project, which **Google** "abandoned in late 2021, was the most direct step to date by the industry" (doc.txt) If companies expanded "into more bank-like activities, such as accepting deposits, their only option would likely be to pursue an industrial loan company charter" (doc.txt)

- **Legislative framework:** "Generally, U.S. law prohibits commercial enterprises (i.e., nonfinancial firms) from operating banks," a separation dating to the Glass-Steagall Act and the Bank Holding Company Act. (doc.txt) An exception is industrial loan companies (ILCs): ILCs are financial institutions charted in six states "that function much like typical banks. Unlike other banks, ILCs can be owned by nonfinancial, commercial firms." (doc.txt) ILCs are exempt from the definition of bank under the Bank Holding Company Act but "are considered state banks for the purposes of the FDIC Act," making them "eligible for deposit insurance." (doc.txt) Thus, "this patchwork legal framework creates the opportunity for a company with other commercial interests to offer banking services, obtain deposit insurance, and operate nationally without being subject to consolidated supervision by the Federal Reserve System." (doc.txt)

- **Relevant history:** In the mid-2000s, both **Walmart** and **Home Depot** "began unsuccessful attempts to establish or acquire ILCs" (doc.txt) "Both ultimately dropped their attempts in the face of public opposition," after which "the FDIC imposed an official moratorium on considering new insurance applications from ILCs between July 2006 and January 2008, and Section 603 of the Dodd-Frank Act imposed another temporary statutory moratorium between July 2010 and July 2013." (doc.txt) "the FDIC did not grant an ILC insurance application again until March 2020, when it approved two ILCs" (doc.txt) — **Nelnet** and **Square** — "for deposit insurance." (doc.txt) **Square** "offers many of the same services as the Big Techs in this report, including mobile wallet with payment and peer-to-peer transfer functions" (doc.txt)

- **Rakuten application:** "Also relevant is an outstanding application for an ILC charter by Japanese e-commerce company" (doc.txt) **Rakuten**. **Rakuten** "filed its most recent ILC application on January 15, 2021, following two previous applications that it withdrew before rulings." (doc.txt) "**Rakuten** is sometimes referred to as the 'Amazon of Japan' and has a similar business model to U.S. Big Tech companies," so "any decision by the FDIC will have regulatory arbitrage considerations" (doc.txt)

- **Potential benefit of an ILC charter:** "Theoretically, a Big Tech with an ILC charter could benefit from access to deposits and deposit insurance without the increased regulatory burden and costs of consolidated financial oversight by the Federal Reserve System" (doc.txt)
