

Based on `doc.txt`, the two points address different rights:

**Point 6 — the right to information**
Point 6 states that "Every child and young person has a right to information, in a form that is understandable to them" (doc.txt). It concerns what children are told and how:
- Information "about their health and healthcare," including "information about the choice of health care services available" (doc.txt).
- Freedom "to seek, receive and impart information and ideas, not only orally but also through other means of the child's or young person's choice, such as play and art" (doc.txt).
- Language and format "appropriate to the child's or young person's abilities and level of understanding," and ensuring "they have understood the information given" (doc.txt).
- Access to information "in Te Reo Māori" and, for those from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds, "in their own language" (doc.txt).
- Health professionals must "talk directly to children and young people, as well as to their families/whānau, even if the child or young person may seem unable to comprehend" (doc.txt).
- Children are "entitled to know what is going to happen to them before a procedure occurs and to be given honest information about their condition and treatment outcomes" (doc.txt), and "Advance preparation for hospitalisation, healthcare procedures or impending surgery provides children and young people with a sense of mastery over the healthcare environment" (doc.txt).

**Point 7 — the right to participate in decision-making**
Point 7 states that "Every child and young person has a right to participate in decision-making and, as appropriate to their capabilities, to make decisions about their care" (doc.txt). It concerns the child's role in decisions:
- Involvement "in decision-making about their healthcare, to the greatest extent possible in line with their capacities for understanding" (doc.txt).
- The right to be involved "in decisions about the use, return or disposal of any bodily parts or substances removed, changed or added in the course of health care" (doc.txt).
- Children "should be offered healthcare choices wherever possible" and "are always entitled to a second opinion" (doc.txt); if their views "cannot be acted on, they are entitled to an explanation" (doc.txt).
- Health professionals "ought to be available, trained and committed to communicating with children and young people" so families can "provide informed consent to treatment" (doc.txt).
- Participation "can include both verbal and nonverbal communication" and "opportunities to communicate through play, art and other media of the child's or young person's choice" (doc.txt).
- The right extends to groups: "encouraging and supporting children and young people as groups to be involved in consultation on the development, implementation and evaluation of the services, policies and strategies that have an impact on them" (doc.txt).
- For teaching or research, "Informed consent is to be sought from children, young people and their families/whānau," those who agree "must have the opportunity to withdraw at any time without having to give a reason," "The decision not to participate in teaching or research must not alter access to treatment," and "Ethical oversight by a Human Research Ethics Committee of all research projects conducted in child healthcare services is part of protecting the children and young people involved" (doc.txt).

**Key difference**
Point 6 is about the child's right to *receive and understand information* — in an appropriate form and language, directly from health professionals, before procedures, with honest information about condition and outcomes. Point 7 is about the child's right to *take part in and make decisions* — participating in decisions about their care (including decisions about bodily parts or substances), being offered choices and a second opinion, giving informed consent, and being involved as groups in consultation, including in teaching and research with the right to withdraw.
