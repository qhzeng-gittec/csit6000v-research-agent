**VA (Department of Veterans Affairs).** VA received Mr. Taylor's 2007 claim for service-connected disability benefits. A VA medical examiner diagnosed him with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) resulting from his time at Edgewood, which was reaggravated during his service in Vietnam. VA ultimately granted him a total disability rating with an effective date of February 28, 2007—the day VA received his claim—relying on 38 U.S.C. § 5110, which provides that, absent certain exceptions, the effective date "shall not be earlier than the date of receipt of application therefor." On remand, VA procured the secrecy oath that most participants in the Edgewood program signed, and VA and the BVA again concluded that Mr. Taylor was entitled to benefits starting February 28, 2007, not September 7, 1971. Following the Federal Circuit's 2023 decision, VA is also responsible for assessing the effective-date issue on remand in accordance with the court's decision.

**Board of Veterans' Appeals (BVA).** The BVA adjudicated Mr. Taylor's appeal of the VA determination in which he sought an effective date of September 7, 1971, the day he was discharged from service. In July 2010, the BVA held that 38 U.S.C. § 5110 prevented the effective date from being earlier than the day he filed his claim, stating that "there was nothing stopping [Mr. Taylor] from filing the claim . . . earlier." In February 2014, the BVA remanded the case to VA to ascertain the secrecy oath's language and reassess the case. In April 2017, the BVA again determined that benefits began February 28, 2007, giving three reasons: (1) Mr. Taylor could have filed a claim earlier premised on his Vietnam service experiences, which were partially responsible for his PTSD; (2) he could have divulged his Edgewood participation because he had done so on a previous occasion in pursuit of care, though the health care provider at that time did not believe him; and (3) Section 5110 delineates when VA benefits can begin and does not provide for equitable tolling.

**U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC).** The CAVC reviewed the BVA's decisions. In June 2013, a single judge held that the BVA "failed to provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases" for its decision, sought the language of the original secrecy oath, and vacated and remanded the case. In April 2019, a 2-1 panel affirmed the BVA's determination that Mr. Taylor was only entitled to benefits beginning February 28, 2007, detailing three components of its analysis: (1) Mr. Taylor was not entitled to relief under the Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause because he did not have a property interest in VA benefits prior to February 28, 2007, as individuals gain a constitutionally cognizable property interest only upon filing claims; (2) the court lacked authority to order VA to establish a process through which he and similarly situated veterans could obtain effective dates preceding their claim-filing dates; and (3) 38 U.S.C. § 5110 required the effective date to be February 28, 2007.

**Federal Circuit.** The Federal Circuit reviewed the CAVC's decision. In June 2021, a three-judge panel ruled in favor of Mr. Taylor, holding that (1) the Veterans Court may exercise equitable powers within the scope of its statutory jurisdiction to ensure all veterans entitled to benefits receive them; (2) the effective date requirement under 38 U.S.C. § 5110(a)(1) is not jurisdictional and can be subject to equitable considerations; (3) where the Government affirmatively and intentionally prevented veterans from seeking care and benefits under threat of criminal prosecution, the CAVC is within its authority to hold the Government equitably estopped from asserting that those veterans are not entitled to an earlier effective date; and (4) the government was equitably estopped from asserting § 5110(a)(1) against Mr. Taylor because the government's actions caused him to file his claim when he did. The panel reversed and remanded. In July 2021, the Federal Circuit voted to hear the case en banc, heard oral argument on February 10, 2022, and stayed all proceedings until the Supreme Court decided Arellano. In June 2023, the full Federal Circuit decided in favor of Mr. Taylor: after finding equitable estoppel unavailable under Section 5110(a)(1) in light of Supreme Court precedent, it held that the government violated his Fifth Amendment due process right of access to adjudicatory forums, that the secrecy oath and threat of criminal prosecution unconstitutionally denied him access to the VA adjudication system, and that the statutory limits on effective dates were "unconstitutional as applied" in his case. The court determined he was entitled to "the effective date he would have had if no unconstitutional denial of access had occurred," which could be September 7, 1971, pending VA's assessment on remand, and it reversed the CAVC's decision and remanded the case.

**U.S. Supreme Court.** The Supreme Court's role in the claim arose through its review of Arellano v. McDonough, a case concerning whether 38 U.S.C. § 5110(b)(1)—which mandates that the effective date of an award of disability compensation is the day following discharge if application is received within one year—is subject to equitable tolling. In January 2023, the Court held that subsection (b)(1) is not subject to equitable tolling, because "the structure of §5110 reinforces Congress's choice to set effective dates solely as prescribed in the text," and because subsection (a)(1) provides "an instruction to attend to specifically enacted language to the exclusion of general, unenacted carveouts." This decision prompted the Federal Circuit to request supplemental briefing in Mr. Taylor's case.
