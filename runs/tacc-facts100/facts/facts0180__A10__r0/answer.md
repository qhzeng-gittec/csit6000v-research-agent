**Sideline evaluation (NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment / "Sideline Survey")**
- It is the immediate on-field assessment: "the player must undergo the entire NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment" (doc.txt) when a sign or symptom of concussion is exhibited or reported.
- It is "conducted inside the medical evaluation tent on the Sideline (or in the Locker Room if the Club medical staff elects to conduct it in the Locker Room); (ii) performed using the tablet or other technology assigned by the NFL" (doc.txt).
- Its minimum components are: "the entire NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment1 which, at a minimum, must consist of the following:" (doc.txt), including "iv. All Maddocks' questions;" and "vi. Focused Neurological Exam, inclusive of the following:" (doc.txt).
- It is a screening step: "If any elements of the sideline assessment are positive, inconclusive, or suspicious for the presence of a concussion, the player must be escorted to the locker room immediately for the complete NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment" (doc.txt). If the physician concludes "the player did notsustain a concussion, then the playermay return to play." (doc.txt).

**Locker room evaluation (NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment / "Locker Room Exam")**
- It is "the standardized acute evaluation tool that has been developed by the NFL's Head, Neck and Spine Committee" (doc.txt), "based on the Standardized Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT 5)... modified for use in the NFL" (doc.txt).
- It is more comprehensive and longitudinal: "The ongoing use of the Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment in conjunction with the preseason baseline and post-injury testing provides detailed data regarding each athlete's injury and recovery course" (doc.txt), and "Being able to compare the results from the NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment to the baseline information obtained in the preseason improves the value of this instrument" (doc.txt).
- It can "aid in the diagnosis of concussion even if there is a delayed onset of symptoms" (doc.txt).
- It has documentation requirements: "Clubs shall maintain and upload to the EMR all NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment exams and a copy of the same shall be given to both the player and the team medical staff" (doc.txt).
- It has stated limits: "The neurocognitive assessment in the NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment is brief and does not replace a more comprehensive neurological evaluation or more formal neurocognitive testing" (doc.txt), and "The athlete may have a concussion despite being able to complete the NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment "within normal limits" compared to baseline," (doc.txt).

In short, the sideline survey is the immediate, tablet-based on-field screening (including video review and a focused neurological exam) that determines whether the player can return to play or must go to the locker room, while the locker room exam is the standardized, SCAT 5-based comprehensive assessment used for diagnosis (including delayed-onset cases), baseline comparison, and tracking the injury and recovery course.
