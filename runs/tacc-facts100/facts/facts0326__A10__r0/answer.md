

The study examined changes in weight among 4.24 million US adults (18–84 years old) from January 2019 through May 2021 to determine whether weight gain increased during the pandemic. Its outcome, as stated in the document:

- "Compared with the pre‐pandemic weight trend, there was a small increase (0.1 kg) in weight in the first year of the pandemic (March 2020 through March 2021)." (doc.txt)
- "Weight changes during the pandemic varied by sex, age, and initial BMI, but the largest mean increase across these characteristics was < 1.3 kg." (doc.txt)
- "Weight increases were generally greatest among women, adults with BMI of 30 or 35 kg/m2, and younger adults." (doc.txt)

The study also defined its key measure: "We refer to the difference in weight change during the pandemic and the weight change before the pandemic from these models as the excess weight gain during the pandemic. This is the weight gain during the pandemic in excess of that predicted by the pre‐pandemic trend in weight." (doc.txt)
