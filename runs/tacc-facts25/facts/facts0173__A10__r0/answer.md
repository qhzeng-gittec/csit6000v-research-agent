Periorbital hyperpigmentation, also called peri-palpebral hyperpigmentation, dark eyelids, dark eye circles, dark circles, or simply under-eye circles, is described in the document as "a mere color difference between the palpebral skin and the remaining facial skin," yet it "makes people look tired or older, which negatively affects their quality of life."

**Prevalence and inheritance.** The condition "has a higher prevalence in individuals with darker skin, hair and eyes, and affects age groups and genders equally. Nevertheless, there are a higher number of complaints from women, especially senior women." There are few studies on its etiology, but "dark eye circles with a vascular component are known to present a dominant autoso- mal family inheritance pattern."

**Causes.** Periorbital hyperpigmentation "seems to have multifactori- al causes that involve intrinsic factors (determined by the indi- vidual's genetics), and extrinsic factors (sun exposure, smoking, alcoholism and sleep deprivation, for instance)." A distinctive feature of its etiopathogeny is "the presence of melanic pigment and hemosiderotic pigment in the affected sites."

Melanic hyperpigmentation "is more frequent in brunet adults, as a consequence of excessive and cumulative exposure to the sun, which increases the production of melanin, reduces the skin's thickness and increases the dilatation of blood vessels."

Intense vascularization "is mainly found in people belong- ing to certain ethnic groups such as Arabs, Turks, Hindus, inhab- itants of the Iberian Peninsula and their respective descendants." In these ethnicities, the manifestation "tends to take place earlier, often during childhood." There is no change in skin color; "the eyelid appears darkened because the dilated vessels are visible due to the transparency of the skin." The problem is often aggravated "when the lower eyelid's vessels are more dilated (e.g., from fatigue, insom- nia, oral breathing, crying), causing dermal blood extravasation. The liberation of ferric ions takes place locally, entailing the for- mation of free radicals that stimulate the melanocytes, which generates melanic pigmentation."

Other causes noted include "post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation secondary to atopic and contact dermatitis, sleep deprivation, oral breathing, alcoholism, smoking, use of certain medications (contraceptives, chemotherapy, antipsychotic and some types of eye drops), the presence of palpebral sagging (due to aging) and of disorders that develop with hydric retention and palpebral edema (thyroid disorders, nephropathies, cardiopathies and pneumopathies) – all of which worsen the unattractive appear- ance of dark eye circles."

**Treatments.** "Various treatments have been proposed for periorbital hyperchromia, however there are few studies on their long-term efficacy." The main types are "topical application of depigmenting products, chemical peelings, dermabrasion, cryosurgery, fillings with hyaluronic acid, intense pulsed light, CO2, argon, ruby and excimer lasers."

**Anatomical background.** The eyelids are "tegumentary pleats" whose main function is to protect the eyeballs. Palpebral skin is "the thinnest in the human body (< 1 mm)," with an epidermis of only 0.4 mm. The dermis is "extremely thin in that region" and absent in the pre-tarsal skin and the medial and lateral ligaments. "The thinness of the skin, combined with the lack of fatty tissue, gives that region its characteristic translucency. As a result, the accu- mulation of melanin and/or vessel dilatation in that region can be easily seen, through transparency, as bilateral homogeneous hyperpigmentation." The palpebral skin's color results from "the combination of several factors, some of genetic-racial origin (such as the amount of melanin pigment), others of individual or regional and even gender l origins, such as the thickness of the several components and the blood volume in their vessels."

**Most affected individuals:** **individuals with darker skin, hair and eyes** (with a higher number of complaints from women, especially senior women).
