Based on `doc.txt`, the medications used in the treatment of ADHD are:

- **Methylphenidate (MPH)** — a stimulant
  - *Mechanism of action:* Increases dopamine (DA) signalling through multiple actions, including blockade of the DA reuptake transporter, amplification of DA response duration, disinhibition of the dopamine D2 receptor, and amplification of DA tone. It is also an inhibitor of noradrenaline (NE) re-uptake.
  - *Risks:* Appears to cause minor increases in blood pressure (BP) and heart rate (HR), with no strong data to suggest it increases the QT interval. Little is known about the long-term effects of stimulants on the functional organisation of the developing brain.

- **Dextroamphetamine** — a stimulant
  - *Mechanism of action:* Increases the synaptic activity of DA and NE by increasing the release of the neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft, decreasing reuptake back into the presynaptic neuron, and inhibiting their catabolism.
  - *Risks:* The document does not describe specific risks for dextroamphetamine (it is grouped with MPH as a stimulant with strong evidence of efficacy).

- **Atomoxetine (ATX)** — a non-stimulant
  - *Mechanism of action:* A selective inhibitor of synaptic re-uptake; in vivo, it specifically increases extracellular levels of DA in the prefrontal cortex but not in the striatum, probably by modulating cortical synaptic DA uptake via the NE transporter.
  - *Risks:* Limited data suggest it may increase BP and HR in the short term; in the long term it appears to only increase BP. Its effects on the QT interval remain uncertain.

- **Guanfacine**
  - *Mechanism of action:* A selective alpha2A adrenergic receptor agonist, which improves working memory by stimulating postsynaptic alpha2A adrenoceptors, strengthening the functional connectivity of prefrontal cortex networks. It has also been shown to be effective in reducing ADHD symptoms.
  - *Risks:* The document does not describe specific risks for guanfacine.

Additional context from the document:
- Both MPH and ATX significantly increase activation in key cortical and subcortical regions subserving attention and executive functions, indicating that alterations in dopaminergic and noradrenergic function are apparently necessary for clinical efficacy.
- ADHD pharmacological therapies are generally well-tolerated, but patients should always be closely monitored for both common and unusual potentially severe adverse effects.
- In Italy, only ATX and immediate-release MPH are currently approved for the treatment of ADHD, and pharmacological treatment can only be initiated after failure of cognitive behavioural therapy over 6 months or longer, with patients first enrolled in the ADHD medication registry.
