Based on `doc.txt`, the two evaluations differ as follows:

**Sideline evaluation (NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment / "Sideline Survey")**
- It is the immediate, on-the-spot assessment a player must undergo when a sign or symptom of concussion is exhibited or reported: "the player must undergo the entire NFL Sideline Concussion Assessment" (doc.txt).
- It is conducted "inside the medical evaluation tent on the Sideline (or in the Locker Room if the Club medical staff elects to conduct it in the Locker Room)" and "performed using the tablet or other technology assigned by the NFL" (doc.txt).
- At a minimum it consists of: "A review of the 'No-Go' criteria listed above," "Inquiry regarding the history of the event," "Review of concussion signs and symptoms," "All Maddocks' questions," "Complete Video Review of the injury," and "Focused Neurological Exam" (doc.txt).
- It is a screening step: "If any elements of the sideline assessment are positive, inconclusive, or suspicious for the presence of a concussion, the player must be escorted to the locker room immediately for the complete NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment" (doc.txt). If the physician concludes "the player did notsustain a concussion, then the playermay return to play" (doc.txt).

**Locker room evaluation (NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment / "Locker Room Exam")**
- It is "the standardized acute evaluation tool that has been developed by the NFL's Head, Neck and Spine Committee to be used by Clubs' medical staffs and designated UNCs to evaluate potential concussions during practices and on game day" (doc.txt).
- It is "based on the Standardized Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT 5) published by the International Concussion in Sport Group (McCrory, et al., 2017), modified for use in the NFL" (doc.txt).
- It "can be used to aid in the diagnosis of concussion even if there is a delayed onset of symptoms," and its ongoing use "in conjunction with the preseason baseline and post-injury testing provides detailed data regarding each athlete's injury and recovery course" (doc.txt).
- It is more comprehensive and documented: "Clubs shall maintain and upload to the EMR all NFL Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment exams and a copy of the same shall be given to both the player and the team medical staff" (doc.txt). Its neurocognitive assessment "is brief and does not replace a more comprehensive neurological evaluation or more formal neurocognitive testing," and it includes "modified Balance Error Scoring System (mBESS)" (doc.txt).

In short, the sideline survey is the immediate, tablet-based screening (No-Go criteria, Maddocks' questions, video review, focused neurological exam) that determines whether the player can return to play or must go to the locker room, while the locker room exam is the fuller, standardized (SCAT 5-based) acute assessment used for diagnosis, comparison to baseline, and documentation.
