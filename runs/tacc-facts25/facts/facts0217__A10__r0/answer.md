Based on `doc.txt`, Points 6 and 7 address two distinct rights of children and young people in health care:

**Point 6 — the right to information:**
- "Every child and young person has a right to information, in a form that is understandable to them" (doc.txt).
- It covers information "about their health and healthcare," including "information about the choice of health care services available" (doc.txt).
- It requires that "the language and format used are appropriate to the child's or young person's abilities and level of understanding" and that they "have understood the information given" (doc.txt).
- It includes "the right of tamariki and rangatahi to have access to information in Te Reo Māori and for those from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds to have access to information in their own language" (doc.txt).
- It requires that "health professionals talk directly to children and young people, as well as to their families/whānau, even if the child or young person may seem unable to comprehend" (doc.txt).
- It entitles children "to know what is going to happen to them before a procedure occurs and to be given honest information about their condition and treatment outcomes" (doc.txt), and notes that "Advance preparation for hospitalisation, healthcare procedures or impending surgery provides children and young people with a sense of mastery over the healthcare environment" (doc.txt).

**Point 7 — the right to participate in decision-making:**
- "Every child and young person has a right to participate in decision-making and, as appropriate to their capabilities, to make decisions about their care" (doc.txt).
- It includes "the right to be involved in decisions about the use, return or disposal of any bodily parts or substances removed, changed or added in the course of health care" (doc.txt).
- It states that children "are always entitled to a second opinion" and that "If their views cannot be acted on, they are entitled to an explanation." (doc.txt).
- It requires that "health professionals caring for them ought to be available, trained and committed to communicating with children and young people" (doc.txt), and that participation "can include both verbal and nonverbal communication" (doc.txt).
- It extends beyond individual care: it "includes encouraging and supporting children and young people as groups to be involved in consultation on the development, implementation and evaluation of the services, policies and strategies that have an impact on them" (doc.txt).
- It addresses teaching and research: "Informed consent is to be sought from children, young people and their families/whānau before they are involved in teaching or research," those who agree "must have the opportunity to withdraw at any time without having to give a reason," and "The decision not to participate in teaching or research must not alter access to treatment." (doc.txt), with "Ethical oversight by a Human Research Ethics Committee" (doc.txt).

**Key differences:** Point 6 is about receiving understandable, honest, and culturally appropriate *information* about health and care (including advance preparation and knowing what will happen before procedures), while Point 7 is about *participating in and making decisions* about care (including decisions about bodily parts or substances, second opinions, group consultation on services and policies, and consent/withdrawal for teaching and research).
