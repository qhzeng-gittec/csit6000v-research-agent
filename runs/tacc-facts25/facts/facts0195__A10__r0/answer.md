**Ellana's background in medicine**

Ellana Stinson is "a board-certified emergency medicine physician and have practiced emergency medicine for 15 years" (doc.txt). From 2013 to 2020, she "worked on staff as an emergency medicine physician at Carney Hospital, Good Samaritan and the former Quincy Hospital, all part of the Steward Health System" (doc.txt). In addition, "over the past 11 years, I have worked intermittently throughout the country at various facilities run and operated by HCA, Envision, Tenant, Team Health and CHS" (doc.txt).

**Her concerns about changes in Emergency Medicine**

She says that "so much of what I loved about Emergency Medicine has changed drastically over the years" (doc.txt). Her specific concerns include:

- **Dwindling resources:** At Steward facilities she "began to realize how resources were being dwindled down and pulled from each facility," with "Most of the facilities no longer had certain speciality services" and Quincy Hospital "taken down to bare bones before its ultimate closure." "Not having blood products, respiratory therapy at times or certain specialty services no longer felt like I was able to provide safe or quality care" (doc.txt).

- **Private equity buyouts:** She notes "the buyouts, mergers and acquisitions led by private equity firms of over 30% of hospitals in the US," and at PE sites "the level of deprivation was seemingly worse in some areas" (doc.txt).

- **Staffing and safety problems:** She describes "Increased wait times and critical shortage of staff leading to dangerous boarding levels and critically dangerous patient to nurse ratios in the emergency department, seeing upwards to 14 to 1 ratios at times," which was "exacerbated by the pandemic which resulted in increased cost of care, infection rates, mortality and even death" (doc.txt).

- **Profit-driven decision making:** "Practicing medicine in many PE led places is no longer about patient safety and quality, but about making medical decisions and judgment due corporate decision making with profit motives at the expense of patients," including "Forcing staff to see patients in the waiting room in order to have it appear wait times were being reduced," "calling codes for sepsis and strokes in order to find innovative ways to make profits," and "Increasingly daunting metrics required of physicians and other staff to meet were nearly unattainable and unsafe in many instances" (doc.txt).

- **Surprise billing:** "many sites across the country began to remove emergency physicians from in-network, resulting in higher bills for patients known as surprise billing," which "increase financial burdens on patients who do not have a choice of where they will go when they are experiencing crushing chest pain or stroke-like symptoms" (doc.txt).

- **Impact on vulnerable populations:** Most facilities she worked in served "very vulnerable populations that were mostly people of color, low income, or had limited access to other facilities or primary care services," and "most of the buyouts are of hospitals and facilities already struggling or with higher Medicaid and Medicare populations." She warns that "The financialization of medicine will continue to exacerbate health disparities" (doc.txt).

- **Toll on physicians:** These practices "often felt unsafe, which sparked many questions of medical legal risk as well as the emotional toll it takes on all of us who enter this profession to do no harm." Being on staff at a PE-backed facility "also would not qualify physicians swimming in student debt for loan forgiveness," and the profession is "now one of the least competitive fields to enter as students bear witness to the destruction of the profession," with "554 of the residency spots" unfilled in 2023 and PE-backed hospitals "opening residency programs that were not adequately equipped to provide the proper training for an emergency medicine trainee" (doc.txt).

She concludes: "We train to deal with hard stuff, but we are running out of options on how to continue providing safe and adequate care at these PE run facilities" (doc.txt).
