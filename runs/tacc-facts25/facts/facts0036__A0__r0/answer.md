Based solely on `doc.txt`, here is a brief overview of the vaccine types discussed and their characteristics:

**Traditional vaccines**

- **Live-attenuated vaccines:** Stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs), providing long-term immunity. Drawbacks: may develop hypersensitivity, and safety/efficacy assessments are costly.
- **Inactivated viral vaccines:** Poorly provoke cellular immune responses, which mitigates their efficacy. Examples: inactivated COVID-19 vaccines manufactured by Sinovac and Sinopharm (Wuhan Institute of Biological Products) in April 2020.
- **Subunit vaccines:** Safe, but have defects including low immunogenicity, requirement for boosters or adjuvants, and high cost.

**Next-generation (nucleic acid-based) vaccines**

Developed from sequence information; include DNA or mRNA sequences of antigens that strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses.

- **DNA-based vaccines:** Advantages include fast production, being among the earliest COVID-19 vaccines in clinical trials, and stability in various storage conditions.
- **RNA-based vaccines:** Received more attention from companies like Pfizer/Biontech and Moderna. Unlike DNA vaccines, they stimulate an effective humoral immune response as a TLR ligand without adjuvant, and their sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation.

**Relation of Toll-like receptors (TLRs) to vaccines**

According to the document, TLRs are involved in two ways: (1) live-attenuated vaccines stimulate innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses *by inducing* TLRs, and (2) RNA-based vaccines act *as a TLR ligand*, stimulating an effective humoral immune response without needing an adjuvant.
