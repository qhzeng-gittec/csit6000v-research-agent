Based on `doc.txt`, the government's strategies to encourage compliance in the tax system (Chapter 2, "Tackling non-compliance") include:

**Overall approach**
- Since 2010, "the government has introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC to tackle non-compliance in the tax system – this has secured and protected over £250 billion which would otherwise have gone unpaid." (doc.txt)
- Significant reforms include "the introduction of the General Anti-Abuse Rule; Accelerated Payments; the implementation of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action Plan; and a range of measures to clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance." (doc.txt)
- The overall aim is "for everyone to pay the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are." (doc.txt)

**Modernising tax debt collection**
- The government is publishing "a call for evidence on how HMRC can modernise its collection of tax debts to reflect the changing nature of the economy," including business conducted in the UK without a physical presence, and on HMRC's approach to "the small minority of business taxpayers who do not engage with HMRC and hold off paying their tax for as long as they can" (doc.txt)

**Mandatory Disclosure Rules**
- The government announced it "would implement the OECD's Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules" and is "issuing a technical consultation and draft regulations." These rules "require advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) to report information to the tax authorities on certain prescribed arrangements and structures," which "will be exchanged with relevant tax authorities to help deter non-compliance, assist in identifying and challenging evasion, and support the development of tax policy." (doc.txt)

**Transfer pricing documentation**
- The government "will legislate in 2022 to require businesses that are in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action 13, and a supporting summary audit trail," taking effect from April 2023. (doc.txt)

**Tax conditionality on licences**
- The government is extending "tax conditionality to certain licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland from April 2023," making "the renewal of certain licences conditional on applicants completing checks that confirm they are appropriately registered for tax," which makes "it more difficult for traders to operate in the hidden economy." (doc.txt)

**ISA compliance and penalties**
- The government is publishing "a call for evidence on proposals to enhance ISA compliance," to strengthen and modernise the approach "to encourage ISA managers and investors to get things right first time," applying to all types of ISA. (doc.txt)

**SDLT reliefs**
- The government is consulting on changes to "the SDLT reliefs for purchases of mixed property and multiple dwellings," which "are being exploited by some purchasers to unfairly reduce their tax liability," to "make the system fairer and reduce the scope for misuse and incorrect claims." (doc.txt)

**Offshore non-compliance**
- The government "has established an HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents to explore ideas to tackle offshore tax non-compliance," as part of implementing HMRC's "'No Safe Havens' strategy," seeking views on "how to help taxpayers reduce offshore noncompliance in relation to non-UK income, gains and assets, and how to reduce and prevent international tax debt." (doc.txt)

**VAT split payment**
- The government is "committed to exploring further VAT split payment, an alternative method of VAT collection where the tax element of a digital payment could be remitted directly to HMRC," assessing its potential "both for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation." (doc.txt)

**Monitoring marketed avoidance schemes**
- "HMRC is publishing its annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes in the UK," setting out "the numbers and characteristics of taxpayers using avoidance schemes in the tax year 2019-2020." (doc.txt)
