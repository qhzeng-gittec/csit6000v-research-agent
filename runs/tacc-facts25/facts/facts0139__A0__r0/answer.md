Based on `doc.txt`, here's what the document says about hunger and thirst during a fast:

**Hunger:**
- You may feel hungry when you first try fasting, but fasting actually *decreases hunger hormones and increases fullness*, so many people report **reduced hunger** once they get used to it (lines 36–38).
- Missing a meal may lead to slightly more food at the next meal, but total energy intake is still lower than if you'd eaten both meals (lines 34–35).
- You may struggle at first if you're not used to extended periods without food, but much of this is down to habit — your body expects food at certain times, and when it doesn't get it, that can trigger feelings of hunger. Most people adapt quickly and find it increasingly easy to fast (lines 51–57).

**Thirst:**
- Yes, you can become thirstier/dehydrated. When you eat, you get some water from your food, so during a fast you miss out on some of those fluids. This can lead to **dehydration**, with side effects such as tiredness and headaches (lines 41–43).
- Notably, dehydration can be **mistaken for hunger**, which can make it harder to stick to your fast (lines 44–45).
- The document recommends staying hydrated with water (optionally flavoured with lemon, orange, lime, or cucumber), black or naturally flavoured coffee, tea, or broth (lines 46–49).

So in short: hunger tends to decrease as you adapt, but you do risk becoming dehydrated (and thirst can even be misread as hunger), so you should drink plenty of fluids during the fast.
