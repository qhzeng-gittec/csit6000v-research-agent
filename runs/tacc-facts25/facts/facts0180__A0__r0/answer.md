**Sideline Concussion Assessment (Sideline Survey)**
- Conducted immediately on the sideline (inside the medical evaluation tent, or in the locker room if the Club medical staff elects to) when a player exhibits/reports a sign or symptom of concussion, spinal cord neuropraxia, or a concern is raised by gameday medical personnel.
- Performed using the NFL-assigned tablet or other technology, with completion of each component confirmed on the same device.
- At a minimum it consists of: (i) review of the "No-Go" criteria (loss of consciousness including impact seizure and/or "fencing posture," ataxia, confusion, amnesia) — if present, the player must be brought to the locker room immediately and shall not return to play; (ii) inquiry into the history of the event (before, during, and after the suspected mechanism of injury); (iii) review of concussion signs and symptoms; (iv) all Maddocks' questions; (v) complete video review of the injury, including discussion with the Booth UNC; and (vi) a focused neurological exam (cervical spine examination including range of motion and pain, speech evaluation, gait/coordination/balance testing, and eye movements and pupillary exam).
- If any element is positive, inconclusive, or suspicious for concussion, the player must be escorted to the locker room immediately for the complete Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment. If the Club physician (after consulting the sideline UNC) concludes no concussion occurred, the player may return to play.

**Locker Room Comprehensive Concussion Assessment (Locker Room Exam)**
- The standardized acute evaluation tool developed by the NFL's Head, Neck and Spine Committee for use by Clubs' medical staffs and designated UNCs to evaluate potential concussions during practices and on game day.
- Based on the Standardized Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT 5) published by the International Concussion in Sport Group, modified for use in the NFL (Attachment A).
- Can be used to aid in diagnosis even with delayed onset of symptoms; ongoing use in conjunction with preseason baseline and post-injury testing provides detailed data on each athlete's injury and recovery course, and comparison to preseason baseline improves its value.
- All exams must be maintained and uploaded to the EMR, with a copy given to both the player and the team medical staff.
- The Club physician must assess the player in conjunction with the sideline UNC and is responsible for determining whether the player is diagnosed with a concussion.
- A player may still have a concussion despite completing the assessment "within normal limits" compared to baseline, due to the assessment's limitations.
- Its neurocognitive assessment is brief and does not replace a more comprehensive neurological evaluation or more formal neurocognitive testing; the modified Balance Error Scoring System (mBESS) is an important, validated component.

**Key differences in summary:** The Sideline Survey is the immediate, on-the-spot screening (No-Go criteria, Maddocks' questions, video review, focused neuro exam) done in the tent/sideline with the NFL tablet, which determines whether the player can return to play or must go to the locker room. The Locker Room Exam is the more comprehensive, standardized (SCAT 5-based) acute evaluation used for diagnosis (including delayed-onset symptoms), baseline comparison, and documentation in the EMR.
