Based on `doc.txt`, the government's strategies to encourage compliance in the tax system (Chapter 2, "Tackling non-compliance") include:

**Overall approach (2.1)**
- Since 2010, the government has introduced over 150 new measures and invested over £2 billion in HMRC to tackle non-compliance, securing and protecting over £250 billion that would otherwise have gone unpaid.
- Significant reforms include: the General Anti-Abuse Rule; Accelerated Payments; implementation of the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action Plan; and measures to clamp down on promoters of tax avoidance.
- The stated aim is for everyone to pay the tax that is legally due, no matter who they are.

**Specific measures:**

1. **Modernising tax debt collection (2.2)** – A call for evidence on how HMRC can modernise collection of tax debts to reflect the changing economy (e.g., e-commerce and businesses operating in the UK without a physical presence), and on HMRC's approach to the small minority of business taxpayers who delay paying and force costly enforcement action. The proposals will not affect taxpayers in temporary financial difficulty (including those affected by the pandemic).

2. **OECD Model Mandatory Disclosure Rules (2.3)** – Announced at Spring Budget 2021, with a technical consultation and draft regulations now issued. These rules require advisers (and sometimes taxpayers) to report information to tax authorities on certain prescribed arrangements and structures, including those that could circumvent the Common Reporting Standard. The information is exchanged with relevant tax authorities to deter non-compliance, help identify and challenge evasion, and support tax policy development.

3. **Improving Transfer Pricing Documentation (2.4)** – Legislation in 2022 (effective April 2023) requiring businesses in scope of country-by-country reporting to maintain a master file and local file in line with OECD BEPS Action 13, plus a supporting summary audit trail. The government will continue reviewing the case for an International Dealings Schedule.

4. **Extending tax conditionality to licences (2.5)** – From April 2023, renewal of certain licences in Scotland and Northern Ireland will be conditional on applicants completing checks confirming they are appropriately registered for tax (consistent with reforms in England and Wales from April 2022). Licensing bodies must obtain confirmation before deciding on renewal, making it harder for traders to operate in the hidden economy. The government remains committed to exploring wider application of tax conditionality.

5. **Strengthening ISA compliance and penalties (2.6)** – A call for evidence on proposals to enhance compliance for all types of ISA (Cash, Stocks and Shares, Junior, Lifetime and Innovative Finance), following a December 2020 announcement about the sufficiency of checks on Innovative Finance ISA managers and the penalties regime, to encourage ISA managers and investors to get things right first time.

6. **Making SDLT reliefs fairer (2.7)** – A consultation on Stamp Duty Land Tax reliefs for purchases of mixed property and multiple dwellings, which are being exploited to unfairly reduce tax liability, with potential changes to reduce misuse and incorrect claims.

7. **Stakeholder forum on offshore tax non-compliance (2.8)** – An HMRC stakeholder forum with representative bodies and agents to explore ideas to tackle offshore tax non-compliance, following two spring 2021 discussion documents under HMRC's 'No Safe Havens' strategy, seeking views on reducing offshore non-compliance for non-UK income, gains and assets, and on reducing and preventing international tax debt.

8. **Exploring VAT split payment (2.9)** – An exploratory alternative method of VAT collection where the tax element of a digital payment could be remitted directly to HMRC, assessed for tackling overseas VAT non-compliance and wider tax modernisation, pursued collaboratively with the payments industry (including the Industry Working Group established in 2018).

9. **Publishing data on marketed tax avoidance schemes (2.10)** – HMRC's annual report on the use of marketed tax avoidance schemes in the UK, setting out the numbers and characteristics (occupations, locations, ages, average declared incomes) of taxpayers using avoidance schemes in 2019–2020.

The document also notes (Chapter 3, 3.1) that the government continues to take action to "level the playing field and increase transparency" so the tax system remains fit for the modern world.
