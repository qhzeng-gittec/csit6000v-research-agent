Based on `doc.txt`, the vaccine types and their characteristics are:

**Live-attenuated vaccines** — "stimulate the innate, cellular, and humoral immune responses by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs) with long-term immunity and may develop hypersensitivity." Their "main drawback... is their costly safety and efficacy assessments."

**Inactivated viral vaccines** — "poorly provoke cellular immune responses which mitigate their efficacy." An inactivated COVID-19 vaccine was made by Sinovac and Sinopharm in April 2020.

**Subunit vaccines** — "safe, with some defects including low immunogenicity, booster or adjuvant requirement, and high cost."

**Nucleic acid-based vaccines** (DNA or mRNA) — "strongly stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses in various doses." DNA-based vaccines offer "fast production" and "stability in various storage conditions." RNA-based vaccines (e.g., Pfizer/Biontech, Moderna) "stimulate effective humoral immune response as TLR ligand without adjuvant, and its sequence is modified to preclude mRNA degradation."

**Toll-like receptors (TLRs):** The document links TLRs to vaccines in two ways: live-attenuated vaccines work "by inducing Toll-like Receptors (TLRs)," and RNA-based vaccines act "as TLR ligand without adjuvant." No further detail on TLRs is given.

Note: the document does not describe the molecular mechanisms of these vaccines beyond the TLR points above.
