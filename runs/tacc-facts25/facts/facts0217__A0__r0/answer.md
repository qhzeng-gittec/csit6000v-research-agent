Based on `doc.txt`, Points 6 and 7 are two distinct rights of children and young people in health care:

**Point 6 — Right to information**
- Every child and young person has a right to information about their health and healthcare, in a form they can understand, including information about the choice of health care services available.
- Special attention and creativity are needed so children can seek, receive and impart information and ideas — orally or through other means of their choice, such as play and art.
- The language and format must match the child's abilities and level of understanding, and they must be ensured to have understood the information and had every opportunity to participate in conversations about their health and care.
- Includes the right of tamariki and rangatahi to access information in Te Reo Māori, and for those from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds to access information in their own language.
- Health professionals must talk directly to children and young people as well as to their families/whānau, even if the child seems unable to comprehend.
- Professionals and families should be as open as possible; children are entitled to know what will happen before a procedure, to be given honest information about their condition and treatment outcomes, and to be helped to select and practice coping strategies.
- Timely, accurate information helps children retain a sense of control, particularly in hospital; advance preparation for hospitalisation, procedures or surgery gives a sense of mastery and helps them cope with stressful situations.

**Point 7 — Right to participate in decision-making**
- Every child and young person has a right to participate in decision-making and, as appropriate to their capabilities, to make decisions about their care, to the greatest extent possible in line with their capacities for understanding.
- This includes the right to be involved in decisions about the use, return or disposal of any bodily parts or substances removed, changed or added in the course of health care.
- Children should be offered healthcare choices wherever possible and are always entitled to a second opinion.
- Their questions and ideas about their healthcare should be heard; if their views cannot be acted on, they are entitled to an explanation.
- Health professionals must be available, trained and committed to communicating with children; effective communication is critical because children, young people and families need appropriate information to give informed consent.
- A child must be able to talk with the staff caring for them, understand who the staff are and what they do, and question them about their condition and treatment.
- Participation can include verbal and nonverbal communication, and opportunities to communicate through play, art and other media of the child's choice; professionals must respond appropriately to nonverbal cues (e.g., from infants, very young children, or those with disabilities).
- The right extends beyond the individual: it includes encouraging and supporting children and young people as groups to be involved in consultation on the development, implementation and evaluation of services, policies and strategies that affect them.
- Informed consent must be sought from children, young people and their families/whānau before involvement in teaching or research; those who agree may withdraw at any time without giving a reason, and the decision not to participate must not alter access to treatment. Ethical oversight by a Human Research Ethics Committee of all research in child healthcare services is part of protecting them.

**Key differences:** Point 6 concerns the *right to receive understandable, honest, timely information* (in appropriate language/format, directly from professionals, including advance preparation for procedures), while Point 7 concerns the *right to take part in and make decisions* about their care (including decisions about bodily parts/substances, second opinions, being heard, informed consent, group consultation, and protections around teaching and research).
