## Steps to Apoptosis
Apoptosis (programmed cell death, or "cellular suicide") occurs in a gentle and controlled manner with no negative impact on surrounding cells. The steps described are:
1. It is triggered by self-generated signals within the cell and mediated by **caspases** — enzymes found in all cells that cleave specific proteins to initiate cell death.
2. The cell **expels moisture** in order to dry out and condense until **fragmentation** occurs.
3. Because the cell does not release its contents into the extracellular environment, there are no morphological changes.
4. **Apoptotic bodies** — small vesicles — form to transport the contents of the cell elsewhere, enabling cells to die gently without causing inflammation.
5. Apoptosis is signaled by **chromatin condensation** in the cell's nucleus, which allows scientists to determine whether a cell is dying naturally or unnaturally.

## Differentiating Factors of Necrosis vs. Apoptosis
- **Process** – Apoptosis is characterized by shrinking of the cytoplasm, resulting in condensation of the nucleus. Necrosis occurs when the cytoplasm and mitochondria swell, resulting in cell lysis or a rupture in the cell membrane.
- **Membrane Integrity** – Blebbing is a defining feature of apoptosis (the cytoskeleton degrades and the membrane bulges outward; apoptotic blebs can form when cytoplasmic capsules detach from dying cells), with no effect on the membrane's integrity. In necrosis, membrane integrity is loosened and significantly reduced, if not completely lost.
- **Organelle Behavior** – Organelles can continue to function even after a cell has died due to apoptosis. During necrosis, organelles swell and disintegrate and are no longer functional afterward.
- **Scope of Affected Cells** – Apoptosis is a localized process that only destroys individual or single cells. Necrosis can spread to affect adjacent cell groups, causing damage beyond the initial site.
- **Bodily Influence** – Apoptosis is a naturally occurring, controlled and timely process that is predictable and beneficial to the host (it controls cell numbers, though abnormal apoptosis in either direction can cause disease). Necrosis is a pathological, unregulated process caused by toxins, infections, or traumas; it is always harmful and, if left untreated, can be fatal.
- **Nature** – Apoptosis is planned cell death in which the cell actively destroys itself to maintain body functionality; necrosis is unintentional/unplanned cell death caused by the cell's external environment, which impedes or interferes with body functions or health.

## Ways a Cell Can Be Affected by Necrosis
The document lists the following external factors/causes of necrotic cell death:
- **Bacterial infection** – bacteria entering through an airway or open wound can cause infections linked to unplanned cell death.
- **Fungal infection (mycosis)** – a fungus infiltrating human tissues, which can result in skin or internal system diseases and unprogrammed cell death.
- **Pancreatitis** – inflammation of the pancreas.
- **Protein denaturation** – weak bonds in proteins break down, causing the cells' ability to function properly to suffer.

Additionally, the document notes that in cell samples, the longer a cell is kept outside its preferred environment, the more likely it will die from necrosis — cells can swell and die as a result of external factors, and dead cells can clump and cause more cell death in the surrounding population (e.g., when using traditional magnetic-based cell sorting).
