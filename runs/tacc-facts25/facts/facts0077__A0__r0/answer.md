Based on `doc.txt`, the difference between hard and soft information is as follows:

**Hard information**
- Consists of conventional financial metrics and documentation, such as sales fluctuations, costs of inputs, and specific industry factors.
- It is typically collected by large lenders from borrowers who possess these conventional metrics.
- It is "already quite uniform" and standardized/digitized, so it can be used in automated and statistical underwriting methodologies to price loans.
- Because it is uniform, large lenders can often handle it with automated technological systems, making reporting less expensive per applicant.

**Soft information**
- Is gathered through relationship lending, where small lenders develop close familiarity with their customers.
- It contains circumstantial details about factors such as non-standardized business risks, insufficient collateral, or weak or thin (business) credit histories.
- It is more unique to applicant circumstances, infrequent, and localized, making standardization of the data for electronic collection and reporting challenging.
- Because of soft information, the loan underwriting process to determine more customized loan products and loan pricing is generally less algorithmic and more labor intensive, and reporting costs per applicant are likely higher for small lenders that lack the volume to justify converting soft information to digital and secure formats.

In short, hard information is uniform, standardized, and suited to automated underwriting, while soft information is idiosyncratic, circumstantial, and requires labor-intensive, relationship-based underwriting.
